# SR3 Social Tests — GM Session Reference

**Edition Lock:** Shadowrun 3rd Edition only. No SR4+ rules, terminology, or paradigms.

---

## Core Rules

- Social test dice pool = **skill rating only**
- Charisma does **not** add dice; it mainly affects **target numbers, resistance, and offset cases** depending on the rule in use
- Good Reputation 2: **−2 TN** on social tests
- Tailored Pheromones: raises **effective Charisma for social interaction purposes at close range**, but does not add dice directly
- Keep deception-based Open Tests secret when player certainty would spoil the scene

## Racism and Social Friction

### When to Call

Use this module for any social test with an NPC whose prejudice has not yet been established. metatype = race

### Determining Prejudice

Roll **2D6 − 6**.

- Positive result = the NPC is prejudiced
- The result = **Racism Points**
- Zero or negative = no prejudice; skip the rest

### Racial Bias Table

| 1D6 | Bias |
|---|---|
| 1 | All except own race |
| 2 | Humans |
| 3 | Elves |
| 4 | Dwarfs |
| 5 | Orks |
| 6 | Trolls |

If the result matches the NPC’s own race, reroll.

Record as: **Racism (type) +N**

### Applying in Play

- Add **Racism Points** to the TN of any relevant Charisma-linked skill test against that NPC
- The player may make a **separate Charisma test** to offset the racism:
  - TN = **twice the racism rating**
  - Do **not** reveal this TN to the player
  - Each success cancels **1 Racism Point** on the social test
- If the player is **defaulting to Charisma**, do not allow a separate Charisma offset test

### Night One / SURGE House Note

Standard Metatype racism may apply as the base where appropriate. More visibly uncanny SURGE presentation may justify an extra GM-discretion modifier on top. Concealment of SURGE markers can reduce the reaction back toward ordinary metatype prejudice.

## Etiquette

### What Etiquette Covers

The **Etiquette** Skill allows a character to fit in. More than just knowledge of customs, rituals, and proper behavior patterns, it represents a character’s ability to talk the talk and walk the walk without stepping on anyone’s toes.

Because of the variety of subcultures, social customs, and mores in the Sixth World, Etiquette also includes a character’s ingrained ability to feel a situation out, instinctively know what is proper, and recognize what will get the character what he or she wants.

Etiquette can also let a character recognize prominent figures within a subculture and have a general idea of their strengths, weaknesses, likes, and dislikes.

Etiquette can be used to:

- put people at ease
- convince someone that you belong
- manipulate a conversation to get information
- judge attitudes
- convince people to do or allow something

Unlike **Negotiation**, which involves give-and-take or an explicit deal, **Etiquette** is about getting something because you look, act, and feel like you belong.

### Etiquette Test Rule

When using Etiquette to influence someone, the **base target number** is the opposing character’s **Charisma**.

Apply appropriate modifiers from the **Social Modifiers Table**.

When using Etiquette against a group, the target number is the **Charisma of the leader** or the **most vocal group member**.

### Specializations

Etiquette is a wide-open skill. Characters can specialize in almost any subculture the gamemaster approves.

Examples include:

- Matrix
- Corporate
- Magical Groups
- Tribal
- Gangs
- Media
- Japanese Corporate
- local-specific social groups

In almost all situations, Etiquette specializations apply to a character’s **local environment**. For example, Etiquette (Gangs) normally refers to local urban gangs, not every gang in the world. Gamemasters should adjust target numbers accordingly when a specialization is too broad, too narrow, or outside local context.

### Etiquette Contexts

| Context | Notes |
|---|---|
| Street | Barrens, gangs, fixers, runners, low-end informal interaction |
| Corporate | Office culture, security desks, executives, reception, procedure-heavy settings |
| Tribal | NAN, tribal communities, traditional protocol, local forms of respect |
| Matrix | Decker culture, matrix social norms, host-user interaction etiquette |
| Media | Public figures, journalists, entertainers, interview and presentation spaces |

## Negotiation and Leadership

### Negotiation

The **Negotiation** Skill governs any interaction in which each side seeks to come out ahead, either through careful and deliberate bartering or through fast talk.

To influence a character through **Negotiation**, the player uses the opposing character’s **Intelligence** as the base target number.

If the character is attempting to influence a **group of NPCs** who have no designated leader, use the **average Intelligence** of the group. Groups tend to react as a whole, with reluctant members often drawn along by the enthusiastic ones.

Alternatively, the gamemaster may appoint a leader or a “ringer in the crowd” and base success or failure on that character’s reaction.

#### Specializations

- Bargain
- Bribe
- Con
- Fast Talk

### Leadership

The **Leadership** Skill governs a character’s ability to get others to do his bidding through example and authority. It includes an aspect of problem-solving, but is not intended to replace actual player planning and clear thinking.

Leadership uses the opposing character’s **Intelligence** as the base target number.

Leadership is useful when the character is directing, rallying, or imposing coordinated action rather than bargaining.

#### Specializations

- Political
- Military
- Commercial
- Strategy
- Tactics
- Morale

### Negotiation and Leadership Modifiers

The base target number for **Negotiation** or **Leadership** tests is modified by circumstances according to the **Social Modifiers Table** below.

Use **extra successes** as a measure of accomplishment. The exact effects depend on the situation.

### Fast Talk, Lies, and Obscuring the Truth

In certain circumstances, a character may use **Negotiation** to lie or obscure something from another person. A common example is using **Fast Talk** to talk oneself out of a bad situation.

In such cases, the gamemaster may call for an **Open Test**. The Open Test result becomes a target number for later tests to determine whether someone notices the lie or misinformation.

The gamemaster may wish to roll this Open Test in secret, since the speaking character will not necessarily know how convincing they were.

## Interrogation and Intimidation

### Interrogation

The **Interrogation** Skill governs the extraction of information from an unwilling subject.

Interrogation uses an **Open Test**. The Open Test result becomes the target number for the victim’s **Willpower Success Test** to resist the interrogation.

A character being interrogated who also has **Interrogation** can use it as a **Complementary Skill** for the Willpower Test to resist.

#### Specializations

- Verbal
- Lie Detector
- Voice-Stress Analysis
- Torture
- Drug-Aided

### Intimidation

The **Intimidation** Skill allows a character to make people do what they normally might not, simply out of fear inspired by the character’s appearance or behavior.

Intimidation uses an **Open Test**. The Open Test result becomes the target number for the victim’s **Willpower Success Test** to withstand the intimidation.

A character with **Intimidation** can use it as a **Complementary Skill** for the Willpower Test when being intimidated or interrogated.

#### Specializations

- Physical
- Mental

### How the Result Works

The gamemaster can judge the amount of information revealed, or how much fear is shown, by the gap between:

- the aggressor’s Open Test result, and
- the target’s Willpower test result

The greater the difference, the stronger the effect.

Specific outcomes are left to the gamemaster and the situation at hand.

## Instruction

The **Instruction** Skill allows a character to more efficiently teach something to another character.

When teaching someone a skill, spell, or another subject, a good instructor can improve the pupil’s chance of learning and reduce the effective difficulty of training.

### Teaching Requirements

- The instructor must possess the skill at **Rating 3 or higher**
- The instructor’s rating must be **equal to or greater than** the rating the pupil wants to achieve

### Instruction Test

Teaching requires an **Instruction (4) Test**.

For **every 2 successes**, the student receives **+1 die** for the test to learn the skill.

### Specializations

**By specific subject**

### Teaching Bonus Table

| Instruction Test Successes | Student Bonus |
|---|---|
| 1 | No bonus |
| 2–3 | +1 die |
| 4–5 | +2 dice |
| 6–7 | +3 dice |
| 8–9 | +4 dice |

## Contact Activation

### When to Call for It

Use this when a runner reaches out to a contact for:

- information
- introductions
- gear access
- service access
- favors
- quiet background help

### General Handling

- Usually starts with an **Etiquette** test appropriate to the contact and context
- Contact Rating helps determine **depth of help**, not just willingness
- A character who ignores established relationship protocol may deserve a TN penalty
- If the contact is under pressure, watched, hostile, or at risk, apply situational modifiers

### Practical GM Use

| Situation | Likely Approach |
|---|---|
| Friendly, known fixer | Street Etiquette, moderate TN |
| Corporate insider | Corporate Etiquette, higher consequences for failure |
| Nervous academic or expert | Etiquette or Negotiation, depending on whether this is rapport or exchange |
| Contact being leaned on by enemies | Social TN increases, possible refusal even with successes |

## Quick Rule Summary

| Skill | Base TN / Use | Resistance / Follow-Up | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Etiquette | Opponent’s Charisma | None by default | Blend in, belong, smooth access |
| Negotiation | Opponent’s Intelligence | None by default or Open Test for lies | Deals, bargaining, convincing exchange |
| Leadership | Opponent’s Intelligence | None by default | Rally, direct, command, organize |
| Intimidation | Open Test | Target resists with Willpower Test | Fear, pressure, compliance |
| Interrogation | Open Test | Target resists with Willpower Test | Extracting information |
| Instruction | TN 4 | Student gets bonus dice based on successes | Teaches skills, spells, subjects |

## Adjudication Protocol

1. Identify the social approach being used
2. Check racism if NPC prejudice has not been established
3. Choose the correct skill and base target number
4. Apply Social Modifiers
5. Apply situational modifiers, reputation, disguise, leverage, and context
6. Roll the skill test or Open Test as appropriate
7. Use extra successes or resistance results to judge outcome strength
