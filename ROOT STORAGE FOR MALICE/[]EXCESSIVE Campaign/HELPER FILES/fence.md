# Fencing the Loot

A character just snatched an optical chip full of hot data fresh off some corp's top-secret database. The character also snagged an exec's limo that has enough ordnance in the trunk to equip half a squad of bodyguards. How does the character get rid of all the swag, preferably for the maximum personal profit?

If a shadowrunning team has a prearranged deal for disposing of loot, then the following rules do not apply. If the runners have a pile of stuff and nowhere to sell it, however, which is the usual situation, then it is time to find a fence.

## Finding a Fence

Depending on what the runners have to sell, the fence can easily be one of the team's regular street contacts, such as a fixer or a talismonger. Other times, the runners may need to rely on the kindness of strangers.

Finding a fence requires a successful **Etiquette (Street) Test**.

- **Base Target Number:** 4
- Apply modifiers from the **Finding a Fence Table** below.

If a team is working together to find a fence, roll a number of dice equal to:

> Average Etiquette (Street) Skill for the group + 1 die for each member

Roll against the modified Target Number.

All characters who contribute their Etiquette (Street) Skills to the dice pool must attend the meet at which the loot will be sold. If one or more of them cannot show, the fence gets nervous and skips the meet.

Successes on the test may be allocated in two ways:

1. Reduce the time required to find the fence.
2. Increase the fence's bankroll.

## The Loot

Most fences buy loot that is easy to dispose of before taking on fancy stuff. A fence typically prefers to buy in this order:

1. **Standard gear**: weapons, clothing, armor, vehicles, jewelry, credit balances, money, and similar goods.
2. **High-tech loot**: ordinary data, equipment, research files, and similar material.
3. **Hot loot**: one-of-a-kind items, secret datafiles, prototypes, procedures for new processes, magical items, or anything that belonged to a very important owner and is irreplaceable.

## Hustle It Along

Locating a fence and setting up a meet takes a base time of **10 days**.

Characters may allocate successes from the Etiquette (Street) Test to reduce this time, to a minimum of **1 day**.

Each day spent looking for a fence increases the chance that the former owners of the merchandise learn that the player characters are trying to move it.

At the end of each day, the gamemaster rolls:

> A number of dice equal to the days spent searching, against Target Number 6.

One or more successes means the bad guys are on to them. If the gamemaster thinks the bad guys would hunt down the team, the gamemaster can set up an ambush at the meet.

## Financing the Fence

Fences have limited means. They are ordinary businessmen trying to turn a dishonest nuyen.

To increase the characters' chances of getting a good price for their loot, the team can allocate successes to increase the fence's bankroll.

The gamemaster secretly rolls **2D6** and multiplies the result by **100,000 nuyen** to determine the base bankroll.

Then multiply that amount by the number of successes the team allocates to the fence's bankroll.

> Fence bankroll = 2D6 × 100,000¥ × allocated successes

The result is the total amount of money the fence has available for the meet. The fence is not necessarily willing to spend all of it without considerable persuasion.

## The Meet

A meet can take place anywhere.

Once the meet is in progress, make an opposed **Negotiation Test** between any one character and the fence.

Each side rolls a number of dice equal to their **Negotiation Skill**, using the opponent's **Intelligence Rating** as the Target Number.

Both sides are suspicious of each other, adding an automatic **+2 Target Number modifier**.

The gamemaster sets the fence's Negotiation Skill and Intelligence Rating.

## Sale Price

The Negotiation Test determines the price paid for the player characters' loot.

The base price for most loot is:

> 30% of actual value

For loot not listed in the gear tables, the gamemaster sets the base price.

Whichever side wins the Negotiation Test alters the price in their favor by **5% per extra success**.

- If the fence wins, the payment price cannot drop below **10%** of actual value.
- If the team wins, the payment price cannot rise above **50%** of actual value.

The fence will almost certainly bring enough muscle to forestall any unscrupulous business practices the team may consider. If the original owners of the loot are coming after it, they may show up at the meet as well.

## Finding a Fence Table

| Situation | Modifier |
|---|---:|
| Using a regular contact | -1 |
| Disposing of standard gear | -1 |
| Disposing of high-tech or other important loot | +1 |
| Disposing of hot loot | +3 |
| While being sought by police | +1 |
| While being sought by a corp or organized crime | +2 |
| Magical loot, including focuses, spell formulae, and similar goods | +2 |

## Quick Procedure

1. Determine the loot category and modifiers.
2. Make the Etiquette (Street) Test to locate a fence.
3. Allocate successes between reducing search time and increasing bankroll.
4. For each search day, the gamemaster checks whether the former owners catch on.
5. At the meet, make the opposed Negotiation Test.
6. Start from 30% of actual value and shift by 5% per extra success.
7. Respect the price floor and ceiling: 10% minimum, 50% maximum.
8. Account for the fence's available bankroll and any muscle, betrayal, or owner ambush complications.
