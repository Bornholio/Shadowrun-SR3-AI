# Scumbag — Main Three Heavy Hitters
*GM-facing SR3 opposition file for the Bitter Lake / Bitter Hills chop house.*

These three are not contact-grade mooks. They are the people who make the house dangerous despite weak formal security. They do not threaten Singer by being stronger than him in a clean fight. They threaten him by being competent, fast, site-familiar, mutually practiced, and willing to use timing, hostages, evidence, doors, darkness, noise, magic, data destruction, and exits. Run them as professionals inside their own ugly little workplace, not as suicidal arena fighters.

Use the three together when the house is alerted. Deacon controls bodies and corridors. The Editor controls files, sensors, feeds, doors, power, chip stock, and exits. Church controls perception, astral pressure, fear, delay, and magical interference. None of them knows Singer's hidden capabilities by default. They react to what they can see, hear, record, infer, or have already been warned about.

# Deacon — House Enforcer / Dirty Street Samurai

Deacon is the visible hard stop in the house. He is not a thug with chrome; he is a failed professional who found a place where his violence could become policy. He carries himself like someone used to ending problems before they become scenes. In the public rooms he can pass as door staff or house security, but anyone who understands movement sees the difference: he watches hands, angles, shoulder tension, exits, and whether someone is measuring the room back.

He is dangerous because he does not posture once violence starts. He closes distance, blocks exits, shoots through furniture if the angle is clear, and moves people into crossfire or cover denial. He is not brave enough to die for the business, but he is proud enough to commit hard for the first few seconds. If he realizes the opposition is beyond him, he shifts from winning to buying time for Church or the Editor.

## Site Use

Deacon's best ground is not the front door; it is the transition between public space and back-of-house space. He wants corners, doorframes, narrow halls, and furniture that slows other people more than it slows him. He knows the first floor well enough to move without looking at his feet. On the second floor he uses doorways and sound. In the basement he is less comfortable but still dangerous because the space is controlled and lockable.

If the alert is quiet, Deacon moves to intercept with one lesser guard behind him or offset from him. If the alert is loud, he moves toward the first hard choke and expects Church or the Editor to complicate the room before he commits. If badly outmatched, he does not monologue. He breaks line of sight, calls the threat, and buys seconds.

## What He Knows If Captured or Questioned

Deacon knows the layout, guard habits, where weapons are kept, who is allowed past the bar, where the basement door is, who handles files, who handles medical extraction, and which customers are too important to touch casually. He does not know every buyer, every blackmail file, or every offsite account. He knows enough to describe the business, not enough to unwind the whole chain cleanly.

# The Editor — Simsense Technician / Data Controller

The Editor is the house's technical spine. He is not a decker running a corporate host and not a battlefield rigger. He is a criminal simsense technician who learned enough computer, electronics, security, and media handling to make a filthy business function. He thinks in feeds, storage, backups, burn queues, access, time stamps, and what can be destroyed before someone reaches the room.

He is dangerous because the house is full of things he understands better than anyone else: hidden pickups, hardlines, cheap cameras, data stores, telecom routing, chip blanks, copied footage, door sensors, and jury-rigged control points. In a fight he does not try to outshoot a combat monster. He blinds, delays, locks, dumps, wipes, moves files, starts decoys, sends false room information, and runs with the smallest high-value storage he can physically carry.

## Site Use

The Editor's best ground is the editing room, but his real strength is that he prepared paths before trouble started. He has fast access to storage media, partial backups, active room feeds, and at least one exit route that does not require walking through the lounge. His panic move is not random flight. It is data triage: pocket the smallest important thing, start or fake a destructive process on the obvious machines, and move toward the back or basement path depending on where the threat entered.

If he gets warning, lights and feeds become unreliable before bullets matter. Cameras may show stale images, room audio may be rerouted, and a cheap lock may suddenly become a delay even if it is not real security. He cannot beat a top-end decker or a world-class infiltrator in open contest, but he can create enough uncertainty that a fast assault has to decide between people, files, exits, and evidence.

## What He Knows If Captured or Questioned

The Editor knows where recordings are made, how they are stored, which rooms have pickups, which drives matter, which chips are finished, which files are blackmail-grade, and which aliases appear often. He knows some buyers by handle and transfer habit. He does not know every real identity. He knows the technical chain better than the social chain and may misread who is truly important.

# Church — House Mage / Astral and Perception Control

Church is the reason the house has lasted longer than its hardware deserves. He is not a grand conspirator or a clean professional magician. He is a practical criminal mage with enough discipline to keep the site from looking magically loud and enough cruelty to use fear, misdirection, silence, and delay as tools. His presence makes the house feel watched even when no one obvious is watching.

He is dangerous because he does not need to win a spell duel to matter. He can change what people perceive, interrupt coordination, cover movement, detect living bodies, silence a room, put pressure on weak minds, slow an entry, or force the intruder to spend attention on the astral layer while mundane threats move. Against Singer, Church should not be played as omniscient. He should be played as a competent mage who recognizes that some threats are too large and immediately starts buying time, hiding assets, or creating ugly choices.

## Site Use

Church's best ground is not the middle of a gunfight. His best ground is one room away, with line of sight, astral awareness, and enough warning to decide which sense to attack. He uses detection first if he has time, illusion second, and direct force only when delay fails. He knows the house well enough to cast for corridors, doorways, stair movement, and the basement access without needing a map in front of him.

If Church senses something extremely wrong about an intruder, he does not swagger. He tells the others that the problem is real and changes the shape of the scene. He may cover Deacon's movement, mask the Editor, silence a room, throw up a barrier, or use Control Emotions against weaker people nearby to create hesitation and noise. He should never casually identify Singer's full nature. He can recognize danger, masking, wrongness, or too-clean absence, but exact conclusions require route and evidence.

## Spirits and Watchers

Church may have watchers assigned to the house, but they should be weak, practical, and limited. They notice crossings, astral motion, emotional spikes, and obvious magical events. They do not function as perfect cameras. A typical standing state is two watchers near the second floor and one near the basement access, with short service windows and simple alert instructions.

For a heavier scene, Church can have one prepared spirit available if the GM wants the house to be significantly harder. Keep it modest unless the scene demands escalation: Force 4 air or city-appropriate spirit, assigned to conceal, confuse, delay, or alert rather than murder everyone.

## What He Knows If Captured or Questioned

Church knows the house's magical habits, who has been magically pressured, which rooms feel wrong, where the lodge materials are, which bodies or victims were magically stabilized, and what astral cleanup has been attempted. He knows some blackmail names and some medical facts, but he is not the accountant, the cutter, or the file keeper. His knowledge is strong on fear, timing, and magical contamination; weaker on money and storage.

# Combined Use Against a High-End Main Character

Do not make the three win by secretly knowing Singer's sheet. Make them dangerous by letting them do what competent criminals would do when a site is breached by an unknown extreme threat.

If surprised, Deacon reacts first, Church reads and complicates second, and the Editor triages data or exits. If partially warned, Church and the Editor act before Deacon commits. If fully warned, they do not wait in one room to be killed together. Deacon becomes delay, Church becomes concealment and separation, and the Editor becomes evidence movement.

Their strongest combined move is not damage. It is forcing simultaneous priorities: living victims, a moving data store, a locked basement route, a masked or invisible fleeing technician, a combat threat in the doorway, and astral pressure over all of it. This is how a low-grade but functioning organ-legging operation threatens someone far above them without pretending they are world-class.
