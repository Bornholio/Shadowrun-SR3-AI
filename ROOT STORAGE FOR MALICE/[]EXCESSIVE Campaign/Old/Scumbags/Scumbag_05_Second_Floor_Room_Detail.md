# Scumbag - Second Floor Room Detail

*GM-facing location file. Room detail only. No plot, trigger lists, rumor lists, or tactical option menus.*

## Second Floor Overview

The second floor reads as a private-room level built out of a former residential upper floor. A person coming up the stairs understands the shape quickly: a short landing, a narrow hall, four service rooms, one locked technical room, and one office. The space is quieter than the first floor but not calmer. Carpet strips, wall panels, curtains, and door seals are used to muffle sound, while cheap lamps and colored strips keep the hall from looking like normal apartment circulation.

The rooms are small enough that the floor never feels like a real hotel or club. It feels like a rental property subdivided for controlled visits. Doors are close together, walls are thin under the added acoustic panels, and the upstairs air carries layers of cleaner, perfume, plastic upholstery, warm electronics, stale ventilation, and bodies moving through rooms too often.

## Stair Landing and Upper Hall

The stairs come up into a small landing with the hallway running forward and rooms opening off both sides. The landing is tight enough that two people passing each other have to turn shoulders. A wall mirror and a low lamp try to soften the arrival, but the hall is still practical: narrow carpet runner, patched wall panels, exposed low-voltage conduit tucked along the ceiling edge, and numbered doors using cheap adhesive plates.

A person walking the hall can tell the floor was not built this way originally. Door frames do not match, trim changes from section to section, and the soundproofing is applied over older walls rather than integrated. The hallway gives a clear sense of controlled movement: rooms are close, staff can hear doors, and there is no comfortable place for a visitor to linger without being noticed.

### Room Contents

- Carpet runner - dark, thin, worn along the center path.
- Numbered door plates - cheap adhesive plates on room doors.
- Wall mirror - narrow decorative mirror near the stair landing.
- Low wall lamps - warm light fixtures spaced along the hall.
- Acoustic wall panels - thin panels added over mismatched wall surfaces.
- Exposed low-voltage conduit - service wiring tucked high along the wall/ceiling line.
- Small linen cabinet - narrow cabinet with towels, sheets, wipes, and basic room stock.
- Utility vent grille - hallway vent with dusty edges and weak airflow.
- Stairs down - narrow residential stairs returning to the first-floor bar/lounge side.

## Service Room One

The first service room is the closest to the stairs and feels the most used. It is small, rectangular, and arranged around a low bed platform against the far wall, with just enough walking space on two sides. The window is sealed and curtained, the floor is covered with cheap synthetic rug panels, and the walls have soft acoustic pads that do not quite hide the hard room shape underneath.

A person entering it knows the room is not a bedroom in any normal residential sense. It has no real storage, no personal life, and no comfort that is not staged for short visits. The light fixture is newer than the rest of the ceiling work, the vent grille is slightly oversized, and the furniture is wipe-clean rather than cozy.

### Room Contents

- Low bed platform - padded synthetic surface with dark cover and wipe-clean underlayer.
- Small side table - cheap composite table with lamp and tissue box.
- Wall lamp / dim strip - low warm lighting with simple switch plate.
- Sealed window - window covered by blackout film and heavy curtains.
- Acoustic pads - thin wall panels around bed-side walls.
- Ceiling light fixture - newer fixture with concealed simsense pickup hardware.
- Oversized vent grille - vent cover fitted into altered ceiling or upper wall section.
- Waste bin - small lidded bin with liner.
- Cleaning wipes / towel stack - small visible supply on shelf or table.

## Service Room Two

The second service room is narrower and arranged more like a sitting room converted for private use. It has a padded bench along one wall, a folding bed panel or low couch, a small mirrored panel near the closet line, and a cheap wall-mounted trid screen that may or may not work reliably. The curtains and rug panels match the first room only roughly, suggesting replacement from whatever was available.

A person walking through notices that this room has more patched surfaces than decoration. The furniture is light enough to move, the trim has been repaired around old outlet cuts, and the wiring path to the ceiling fixture is hidden badly behind a plastic strip. It feels like a room adjusted after the fact, not part of a single planned build.

### Room Contents

- Padded bench - wall-side synthetic bench with storage space underneath.
- Low couch / folding bed panel - convertible padded furniture with wipe-clean cover.
- Small mirrored panel - narrow wall mirror mounted near the closet side.
- Wall-mounted trid screen - cheap screen with scuffed casing.
- Side shelf - shallow shelf holding towels, wipes, and small disposable items.
- Blackout curtains - heavy curtains over sealed window.
- Rug panels - replaceable synthetic floor panels with mismatched seams.
- Ceiling light fixture - altered fixture with concealed pickup hardware.
- Plastic wiring strip - surface-mounted strip hiding cable run.
- Waste bin - small lidded bin near the door.

## Service Room Three

The third service room is the plainest. It looks like it was turned over quickly and maintained with the least imagination: low bed, chair, table, curtains, lamp, and wall panels. The room is slightly colder than the others because the vent pushes more air here, and the ceiling has a faint vibration from equipment elsewhere in the building.

A person entering it gets the clearest sense of cheap repetition. Nothing in the room invites anyone to stay longer than necessary. Surfaces are durable, corners are scuffed, and the chair sits where someone can face the door. The ceiling fixture and vent are positioned to cover most of the room without needing obvious equipment on the walls.

### Room Contents

- Low bed - simple padded frame with synthetic cover.
- Plain chair - armless chair with wipe-clean seat.
- Small table - narrow table with lamp and disposable supplies.
- Heavy curtains - blackout curtains over sealed window.
- Acoustic wall panels - thin, functional sound-dampening panels.
- Ceiling light fixture - modified fixture with concealed pickup hardware.
- Vent grille - ceiling or upper-wall vent with audible airflow.
- Floor rug panels - dark synthetic panels, worn near the bed edge.
- Wall switch plate - cheap dimmer/switch with scratched face.
- Waste bin - small lidded bin near the table.

## Service Room Four

The fourth service room is farthest from the stairs and a little larger, likely made from the better original bedroom. It has the most complete staging: a wider bed platform, two side tables, a low couch or padded bench, better curtains, and a small shelf with sealed bottles and towels. The wall treatment is cleaner here, with fewer gaps between acoustic panels and less visible patchwork near the floor.

A person walking through would read it as the preferred room on the floor. It still is not luxurious, but it has more space, warmer light, and better control over how much of the room can be seen from the door. The concealed hardware is better integrated here: the light fixture sits centered, the vent cover looks deliberate, and the cable path vanishes into trim instead of running openly.

### Room Contents

- Wider bed platform - padded synthetic platform with dark cover.
- Two side tables - matching cheap tables with lamps and small supplies.
- Low couch / padded bench - short seating piece against the side wall.
- Better blackout curtains - heavier curtain set over sealed window.
- Towel and bottle shelf - shallow shelf with towels, sealed water, and disposable items.
- Acoustic panel set - more complete wall coverage than other rooms.
- Centered ceiling light fixture - modified fixture with concealed pickup hardware.
- Vent cover - cleanly fitted vent grille near ceiling line.
- Trim-hidden cable run - wiring hidden behind newer composite trim.
- Lidded waste bin - larger than the other service rooms.

## Editing Room

The editing room is locked and feels different as soon as the door opens. It is smaller than the service rooms and hotter, with the smell of warm plastic, dust, cheap electronics, solder, and overworked fans. The walls are covered less for comfort than noise control, and the floor has rubber mats around the main work area. Tables and shelves crowd the room, leaving only one real chair position and a narrow path to the racks.

A person walking through knows this is the most equipment-dense room upstairs. The gear is not clean corporate hardware; it is rebuilt, patched, labeled by hand, stacked with external drives, blank chips, tools, cable bundles, and cheap fans. The room has the visual language of a technical shop squeezed into a rental bedroom.

### Room Contents

- Three rebuilt terminals - cobbled Rating 3-class decks/workstations on tables.
- Chipburner - Rating 2 jury-rigged chipburner with cables and heat marks.
- BTL chip blanks - stacked blank chips in trays, boxes, or sleeves.
- External drives - multiple storage bricks/drives with handwritten labels.
- Tool kit - electronics tools, cutters, soldering gear, probes, and hand tools.
- Cable bundles - bundled hardwire runs feeding through wall or floor paths.
- Rubber floor mats - anti-fatigue or utility mats around work position.
- Cheap cooling fans - portable fans and equipment fans moving hot air.
- Work chair - one adjustable chair with torn padding.
- Shelving unit - metal shelves holding parts, labels, wipes, spare cables, and cases.
- Locking door hardware - stronger lock than the service-room doors.

## Madam's Office

The office is a converted bedroom or small den set up as a working administrative room. It has a desk, two chairs, filing cabinet, cheap safe, wall shelves, a service terminal, and a small screen showing simple house feeds or records. The lighting is brighter than the service rooms but warmer than the editing room, enough for paperwork, accounts, calls, and face-to-face conversations.

A person walking through would know this is the only upstairs room trying to look like management rather than service. It is still cheap: laminate desk, off-brand chair, dented cabinet, scuffed floor, and shelves holding too many paper folders for a supposedly modern rental. It has enough organization to show regular use, but not enough polish to be corporate or legitimate.

### Room Contents

- Desk chair - padded office chair with cracked arms.
- Visitor chairs - one or two plain chairs facing the desk.
- Filing cabinet - metal two- or three-drawer cabinet with labels.
- Cheap safe - small floor or cabinet safe with basic keypad or key lock.
- Service terminal - desktop terminal or compact workstation for records and messages.
- Small wall screen - screen for simple feeds, schedules, or local display.
- Wall shelves - shelves holding folders, boxes, spare supplies, and personal items.
- Paper folders / binders - mixed administrative paperwork and printed records.
- Locking door hardware - better lock than ordinary room doors.
- Floor lamp / desk lamp - practical office lighting.

## Upstairs Bathroom / Utility Closet

The upstairs bathroom is small, overused, and functional. It has a toilet, sink, narrow shower stall or wet-room corner, towel hooks, a wall cabinet, and a floor drain that looks newer than the rest of the room. The surfaces are easy-clean composite and tile panels, with caulk repairs around every seam. The air smells of disinfectant, damp towels, perfume, and ventilation that does not fully keep up.

Next to it or across the hall is a narrow utility closet used for linens, cleaning stock, replacement covers, and small maintenance items. A person passing through understands this floor turns over often. The bathroom and closet are not comfortable spaces; they are support spaces for repeated use and fast reset.

### Room Contents

- Toilet - compact fixture with wall-mounted flush hardware.
- Sink - small composite sink with stained drain and cheap mirror above.
- Shower stall / wet corner - narrow wash space with plastic curtain or panel.
- Floor drain - newer drain set into repaired flooring.
- Towel hooks - multiple hooks on wall or door.
- Wall cabinet - cabinet with hygiene supplies, wipes, soap, and basic first-aid items.
- Vent fan - loud ceiling fan with weak pull.
- Utility closet shelving - shelves with towels, sheets, covers, cleaners, and disposable stock.
- Mop / bucket - small cleaning set stored in or near closet.
- Laundry bag / hamper - heavy bag or bin for used fabric.

## What a Walkthrough Establishes

A person walking the second floor understands that it is a converted private-room level, not a normal residence and not a purpose-built hotel. The four service rooms are repetitive, small, sealed, and staged for short use. The hall shows after-the-fact subdivision and sound control. The editing room is the obvious technical concentration upstairs, full of rebuilt electronics and storage. The office is the administrative point, with records, safe storage, and practical management furniture.

The floor teaches the building's shape through objects rather than explanation: visitors are moved into rooms, rooms are easy to reset, the hallway controls circulation, the bathroom and closet support turnover, the editing room concentrates electronics, and the office concentrates paperwork and local control. Nothing on the second floor looks expensive enough to be secure by itself, but the layout makes clear that the upstairs is organized, watched, and used regularly.
