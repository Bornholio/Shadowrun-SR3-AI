# Scumbag — Other Crew and Occupants
*GM EYES ONLY*

This file covers the five lesser criminal occupants and the non-combat occupants currently inside the Bitter Lake / Bitter Hills chop house. These people use normal names, not street names. The three main threats are handled separately in `Scumbag_Main_Three_Heavy_Hitters.md`.

Use these occupants to make the building feel inhabited and operational without turning every person into a combat block. The five lesser crew are not civilians, but they are not archetype-scale runners. They are practical street-operational criminals: drivers, guards, handlers, doormen, assistants, and cleanup labor. Everyone else is present because the house needs bodies to function, hide, sell, clean, recover, or wait.

## Lesser Crew — Five Men

### Marcus Bell
**Role:** House guard / front-room pressure  
**Scale:** PR 2 street muscle

Marcus is a local human man in his late twenties, built like someone who has done warehouse work, door work, and collections without ever getting formal security training. He is not clever enough to run the house and not soft enough to be harmless. He knows the front rooms, the bar sightlines, who is allowed upstairs, and which visitors should be stalled until a named crew member takes over.

He fits the house because a place like this needs someone who can stand in a doorway, read drunken confidence, and make smaller problems quit before they become noise. He should be competent with a pistol, a baton, and intimidation, but he is not a professional tactical thinker. If the house breaks open around him, he defaults to holding a threshold, calling upstairs, or trying to get behind a heavier man.

### Owen Pike
**Role:** House guard / rear-door and alley watch  
**Scale:** PR 2 street muscle

Owen is a white human man in his thirties with a service-route background: loading docks, night driving, repair yards, storage lots, and cheap private security work. He is comfortable waiting in bad weather, watching vehicles, and recognizing when a delivery story does not match the load. He is not especially brave, but he understands that backing down in front of other crew costs him his place.

He belongs on the rear side of the property because he knows doors, vans, bins, garages, and alley timing better than people. Owen is the one most likely to notice a strange vehicle parked wrong, a gate left open, or a person carrying something without the right body language. In a fight he is not a star, but he is a practical obstacle around the back door, basement loading path, and alley exit.

### Daniel Cho
**Role:** Driver / body handler / supply runner  
**Scale:** PR 2 logistics criminal

Daniel is a Korean-American human man in his early thirties who used to drive legal routes before sliding into cash jobs. He knows enough about city traffic, pickup windows, fuel cards, rental vans, and service entrances to keep the house supplied without making every trip look criminal. He is not a syndicate operator; he is a reliable criminal driver who gets paid because he does not ask questions after the first answer.

Daniel matters because the basement operation depends on movement. Clean supplies, packaged cyberware, medical waste, laundry, food, cheap drugs, blank chips, and occasional wrapped dead weight all need to move through the rear door or service alley without drama. He is not the person who performs surgery or edits chips, but he knows which containers go out cold, which go out normal, and which vehicle should not sit too long.

### Victor Alvarez
**Role:** Doorman / BTL runner / errand criminal  
**Scale:** PR 1 or weak PR 2

Victor is a Latino human man in his early twenties who wants to be more important than he is. He handles small errands, customer calls, cheap chip movement, drinks, door messages, and basic watching. He knows faces and nicknames better than plans. He is useful because he is always moving between rooms and because people underestimate how much a runner can learn by carrying trash, drinks, and sealed envelopes.

Victor is the weakest criminal link in the house. He is not innocent, but he is not hardened in the same way as the men above him. He lies badly under pressure if separated from the others. He may know who came in, who went upstairs, what vehicle arrived, where the spare keycard is, or what room nobody lets him enter alone, but he does not know the whole chain.

### Kevin Madsen
**Role:** Medical assistant / cyberware strip helper  
**Scale:** PR 2 black-clinic hanger-on

Kevin is a white human man in his late twenties with partial clinic training, failed licensing, or off-book technical experience. He is not a surgeon and should not be treated like one. He can prep a room, bag parts, label containers, run sterilizers, monitor a cheap medkit, shave and clean a patient, hold retractors, manage restraints, and follow brutal instructions from someone more qualified.

Kevin makes the basement more medically real because low-grade organ legging still needs procedure. He knows soap, bleach, gloves, sharps disposal, refrigeration, basic anatomy, cyberware extraction handling, and what gets ruined if it is handled wrong. He is dangerous because he has enough knowledge to harm efficiently, not because he is elite.

## Current Victims / Working Occupants

### Naomi Whitaker
**Position:** Controlled sex worker / upstairs occupant

Naomi is a Black human woman in her mid-twenties who has been in the house long enough to understand the rhythms but not long enough to be trusted. She knows which customers are regular, which rooms record badly, when staff changes, who carries keys, and what sounds mean someone is being moved downstairs. She is careful with her face and careful with her words.

Naomi is not a helpless prop. She is trapped inside a system that punishes obvious resistance. She may cooperate with rescue, mislead crew, hide someone briefly, or refuse to speak until she believes the immediate danger has changed. Her knowledge is practical, not omniscient.

### Felicia Tran
**Position:** Controlled sex worker / upstairs occupant

Felicia is a Vietnamese-American human woman in her early twenties. She is newer than Naomi and still makes small mistakes that get corrected by staff. She knows room routines, customer handling, and which doors are usually locked, but she does not understand the basement operation except through fear, absence, smell, and movement.

Felicia should read as someone watching for a survivable opening. She may know one useful thing very clearly rather than many things vaguely: which staff member drinks on shift, which camera does not point where people think, which private room has a loose vent cover, or when the back door opens for laundry.

### Heather Sato
**Position:** Controlled sex worker / recovering occupant

Heather is a mixed Japanese-American human woman in her late twenties who is currently sick, bruised, sedated, or recovering from recent handling. She is not in condition to run hard unless helped. She has spent enough time near the lower rooms to know that the basement is medical, locked, cleaned often, and used for more than storage.

Heather is useful as evidence of the house’s practical cruelty, not as a lore dispenser. She may remember names, smells, light, hands, voices, cleaning routines, or the feeling of being moved. She should not deliver a clean map unless play creates a reason she saw one.

### Priya Nair
**Position:** Domestic labor / cleaning and laundry

Priya is an Indian human woman in her thirties who handles cleaning, laundry, towels, bedding, trash staging, and surface order under pressure. She may be coerced, paid badly, threatened, immigration-vulnerable, indebted, or simply trapped by fear. She knows the house as work: stains, locked doors, loads, containers, broken drains, soap use, missing towels, and which room needs cleaning twice.

Priya’s presence explains why the building does not look like a horror set. Soap, bleach, laundry, bins, gloves, and locked supply storage are part of the cover and the routine. She knows what is normal for the house, which makes her very good at noticing what changed.

### Elise Morgan
**Position:** Civilian captive / probable next medical victim

Elise is a white human woman in her forties who came into the house through debt, addiction, coercion, or a bad introduction rather than as staff. She is being held because her body, cyberware, identity, or lack of protection makes her useful to the lower operation. She is scared, dehydrated, and not fully sure what day it is.

Elise is not combat-capable. Her role is to show the medical-criminal side before it becomes a corpse. She may know only the person who brought her, the room where she woke, the voice that checked her arm, and the fact that she heard wheels and metal trays below her.

### Thomas Grady
**Position:** Dead man / body retained less than one day

Thomas is a white human man in his fifties, probably homeless, debt-buried, or otherwise disconnected enough that no fast official pressure followed him in. He died recently, and the body is being held short-term for stripping, packaging, documentation, and removal. He is not displayed, not staged, and not left to rot.

His presence should reinforce the practical medical reality: the dead do not stay long. Bodies are covered, tagged, chilled if possible, moved in service containers, and removed through normal-looking rear logistics. The smell of bleach and soap is cover, but the locks and procedures are the real protection.

## Practical Population Notes

At a busy moment, the building can hold the three named heavy hitters, the five lesser crew, three to four controlled workers, one cleaner, one captive, and one dead body awaiting removal. Not all are visible at once. Some are upstairs, some are sleeping, some are in the basement, some are on a run, and some are being kept behind locked doors.

The house should feel like a low-grade criminal business that has survived because it is functional, not because it is brilliant. People live here, sleep here, work here, clean here, eat here, guard here, and hurt people here. The danger comes from enough competence in the right places, secure doors, controlled movement, and people who understand the routine.
