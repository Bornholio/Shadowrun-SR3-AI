# Scumbag - First Floor Room Detail

*GM-facing location file. Room detail only. No plot, trigger lists, rumor lists, or tactical option menus.*

## First Floor Overview

The first floor reads as a cheap private entertainment rental built into a residential-service property. A person walking through it understands the public face quickly: visitors enter into a controlled lounge, move toward the bar, and are kept away from staff and service areas unless someone lets them pass. The rooms are not luxurious, but they are staged to feel private enough for men paying to be insulated from the street.

The floor has five main spaces: entry lounge, bar, kitchen, heavy's bunk room, and rear service hall / back door area. The basement access is concealed behind the bar as a reinforced supply-closet door, but this file describes it only as a visible first-floor feature.

## Entry Lounge

The front door opens into a rectangular lounge that tries to look softer than it is. The floor is dark synthetic laminate over older utility subfloor, scuffed where chairs scrape and dull where spilled drinks have been wiped too often. The walls are covered with cheap acoustic panels and warm-colored composite trim, enough to soften voices without making the room sound professionally treated. The front windows are sealed and covered from inside by blackout film and layered curtains, leaving the room dependent on lamps, wall strips, and the glow from the bar.

The furniture is arranged to slow people down rather than welcome them deeply: two low couches facing each other, a small table between them, a few mismatched club chairs, and a narrow path toward the bar. A visitor would know this is not normal housing anymore. It has the layout of a waiting room pretending to be a living room, with the front door watched, the seating pointed inward, and no casual sightline into the rest of the building.

### Room Contents

- Low couches - dark synthetic upholstery, wipe-clean, worn at the arms.
- Mismatched club chairs - cheap padded chairs arranged around the lounge path.
- Low tables - small composite tables with drink rings and scratched surfaces.
- Wall lamps / strip lighting - warm, low-output lighting built for mood rather than visibility.
- Blackout curtains and window film - layered over sealed front windows.
- Front-door camera monitor - small screen near the bar-side transition, showing the porch feed.
- Cheap camera over front door - exterior feed tied into the small monitor.
- Coat hooks / narrow rack - enough for jackets, not a full cloakroom.
- Trash bin - small, lidded, near the seating.

## Bar

The bar sits past the lounge as the first real divider inside the building. It is a short counter built from prefabricated panels and dark laminate, with enough height to block hands and waistlines from a seated visitor. Behind it, shelves hold bottles, glasses, sealed mixers, towels, and small service items. The surface is cleaner than the lounge tables because it gets wiped constantly, but the corners show sticky buildup and rushed maintenance.

A person standing at the bar can see the lounge, the stairs, the kitchen door, and the reinforced supply-closet door behind the counter. The bar makes the first floor feel managed. It also makes clear that staff control the interior path: visitors stay on the lounge side unless invited farther in, and the rooms beyond the bar are not part of ordinary guest drift.

### Room Contents

- Bar counter - prefabricated dark laminate, waist-high, scuffed along the guest side.
- Back shelves - bottles, glasses, sealed mixers, towels, and service supplies.
- Under-counter cooler - compact drink cooler with a loud compressor.
- Payment terminal - cheap counter unit for credstick or account handling.
- Small comm unit - basic wall or counter unit for calls inside the house.
- Front camera monitor - small screen angled toward staff side of the bar.
- Lockbox drawer - metal under-counter drawer with a simple keyed lock.
- Reinforced supply-closet door - heavy door behind the bar, disguised by shelving and service clutter.
- Bar stools - three or four cheap stools on the guest side.

## Kitchen

The kitchen is a functional rental-property kitchen pushed into heavier use than it was built for. It has a narrow prep counter, sealed cabinets, a cheap industrial-look sink, a consumer-grade autochef, a microwave, a compact refrigerator, and stacked plastic bins for dry goods and disposable service items. The room smells less like cooking than reheating, cleanser, stale air, and old grease trapped in vent filters.

Someone walking through would know the kitchen supports the bar and the staff more than any real household. There is not enough cookware for family living, and not enough professional equipment for a proper club or restaurant. It is a back-room utility kitchen: heat food, store drinks, rinse glasses, hide mess, and keep people from needing to leave the property for small things.

### Room Contents

- Prep counter - narrow composite counter with knife marks and stained seams.
- Sealed cabinets - cheap cabinets holding cups, plates, towels, cleaners, and disposable items.
- Deep sink - metal utility sink with hard-water marks and chemical smell.
- Consumer autochef - older household unit used for reheating simple meals.
- Microwave - cheap, stained interior, mounted or counter-set.
- Compact refrigerator - stocked with drinks, leftovers, and service perishables.
- Dry-goods bins - plastic bins for snacks, instant meals, and disposable stock.
- Cleaning shelf - soaps, bleach, gloves, sponges, and mop heads.
- Wall vent / fan - noisy kitchen vent with old grease and dust around the grille.
- Rear service door path - kitchen gives access toward the rear hall and back door.

## Heavy's Bunk Room

The bunk room is a former small bedroom or staff room converted into an on-site crash space. It is plain, cramped, and more practical than comfortable: one narrow bed or folding bunk, a metal chair, a small table, and a storage locker against the wall. The air is warmer and stale, with clothing, gun oil, deodorant, cheap food wrappers, and boot smell layered over the building's normal cleaning products.

A person entering it would know someone stays here during working hours or overnight. It does not look like a rented bedroom for a resident with a life outside the job. It looks like a place where a man sleeps lightly, keeps spare clothes and gear close, and expects to be called into the rest of the house without warning.

### Room Contents

- Narrow bed / folding bunk - single sleeping space with dark blanket and cheap pillow.
- Metal locker - dented standing locker for clothes and personal gear.
- Small table - cheap side table with wrappers, glass, and charging cords.
- Metal chair - plain utility chair, easy to move.
- Clothes hooks - jackets, shirts, and spare work clothes.
- Boot tray - dirty boots and floor mat near the door.
- Weapon shelf or small rack - secured shelf or rack for a pistol belt, baton, or spare magazines.
- Basic comm charger - charging block and cord for a commlink or handset.
- Small trash bin - wrappers, towels, and disposable packaging.

## Rear Service Hall / Back Door Area

The rear service hall is narrow and more worn than the public rooms. It connects the kitchen, staff room, back door, and the bar-side service area. The floor has more scratches here, especially where bins or crates have been dragged. The walls have exposed conduit, cheap access panels, and patched spots where older fixtures were removed or moved. Lighting is brighter than the lounge, colder, and less flattering.

The back door opens into the rear yard and service alley. From inside, the area feels like the building's working seam: trash goes out, supplies come in, staff step outside, and anything not meant for the lounge passes through here. A person walking it understands the first floor has two faces: the front face for visitors, and the rear face for work.

### Room Contents

- Back door - plain reinforced exterior door with cheap mechanical and electronic hardware.
- Utility light - bright, cold overhead fixture.
- Exposed conduit - visible wiring and service runs along the wall.
- Access panels - small utility panels for building services.
- Mop bucket / utility cart - basic cleaning cart with mop, bucket, and spray bottles.
- Trash bins - lidded bins staged near the rear exit.
- Plastic storage crates - stacked crates for bar stock, towels, and disposable supplies.
- Wall hooks - staff jackets, gloves, keys, and tool pouch.
- Small camera or door buzzer panel - basic rear-door check panel, cheaper than the front setup.

## What a Walkthrough Establishes

A person walking the first floor understands that the building is not normal housing anymore, but it is also not a serious club, clinic, or fortified site. It is a cheap rental converted into a controlled private business: lounge in front, bar as social and physical divider, kitchen and rear hall as service support, and a bunk room for on-site muscle. The furniture, lighting, sealed windows, camera feed, and staff-side doors all show a place built around controlling who waits, who passes deeper, and who never sees the working parts unless allowed.
