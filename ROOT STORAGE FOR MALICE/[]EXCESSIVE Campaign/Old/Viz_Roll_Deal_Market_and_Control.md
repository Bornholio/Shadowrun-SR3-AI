## Core Market Position

Viz-Roll Media is publicly traded but thinly held. Its working public market capitalization is **436,000,000¥** before control premium, debt adjustment, private rights, retention costs, or technical remediation. Most shares outside the founder-estate block are distributed through institutional accounts, trading houses, managed funds, and retail investors rather than controlled by active corporate insiders.

The company needs a single credible buyer. The death of the holder behind the 35 percent legacy block forces estate resolution, while the remaining ownership is too fragmented to preserve control independently. If no large buyer closes, the likely result is a discounted estate sell-down, institutional exits, fragmented control, asset sales, staff departures, and loss of going-concern value.

## Effective Control

No current party controls Viz-Roll alone.

The **35 percent estate block** is the essential anchor. It cannot unilaterally approve a full-company sale, but no credible buyer can obtain stable control without it. Once the estate supports a transaction, the buyer needs another **15.1 percent** to pass a simple-majority voting threshold.

Mark's minimum reliable closing coalition is:

- Kincaid-Hayashi Estate and Ocean Legacy Trust: **35.0%**;
- North Pacific Retirement Cooperative: **8.4%**;
- Cascadia Media Index Fund: **6.8%**.

This totals **50.2 percent**. It is the smallest presently credible majority coalition.

A safer coalition adds the Employee Equity Trust and one additional institutional holder, pushing support above **55 percent** and reducing the chance that injunctions, proxy disputes, abstentions, or record-date complications stop closing.

## Board and Sale Authority

The board can recommend and sign a merger or control-sale agreement, but shareholder approval is required to transfer the company as a whole. The current practical threshold is a simple majority of voting equity, subject to normal disclosure, estate authority, and creditor conditions.

No individual seller-side participant has a personal veto based on cultural, ethical, or operational objections. Those concerns matter only when translated into:

- estate instructions;
- trust voting decisions;
- board recommendation;
- funded contractual protections;
- enough shareholder votes to defeat the proposal;
- legal defects in the process;
- inability of the buyer to close.

Price is not the only legal reason to reject a transaction, but unsupported preference alone does not remove Viz-Roll from sale.

## What Mark Is Withholding Until the Final Deal Window

Mark currently has or expects to receive the following material but is not releasing it to Singer until immediately before the actual transaction stage.

### Exact Financing Ceiling

LapisLazuli's authorized package can support an enterprise transaction equivalent of **503,800,000¥**, including assumed and refinanced obligations. Only **348,000,000¥** can be delivered as immediate cash consideration. The remainder must be built from Renraku or subsidiary stock, debt refinancing, funded retention obligations, and committed remediation capital.

### Estate Requirement

The estate administrator must convert at least **70 percent of the estate block's consideration into cash or cash-equivalent instruments** within thirty days of closing. This is the real reason the transaction cannot be structured primarily as long-term stock.

### Debt and Obligations

Viz-Roll carries **61,400,000¥** in funded debt and **24,600,000¥** in deferred rights, residual, pension, and production obligations that must be assumed, refinanced, or separately secured.

### Control Premium

Mark's accountants assess that the public 436,000,000¥ equity value requires a **15 percent control premium** to obtain the estate block and institutional recommendation. That produces a working equity purchase basis of **501,400,000¥** before debt and obligation treatment.

### Internal Renraku Limit

Mark's formal authorization is only sufficient if the transaction is presented as:

- a controlled acquisition of a functioning company;
- immediate technical remediation of a material risk;
- retention of essential rights and relationship infrastructure;
- a fully capitalized subsidiary capable of visible profit.

If the package becomes an open-ended rescue, a cultural-preservation expense, or an asset purchase followed by mass retention guarantees, his authorization can be withdrawn or transferred to Graham Vey's portfolio.

### Competing Interest

Kintsugi Media Systems has prepared a fallback structure worth **476,000,000¥** in headline value, but only **302,000,000¥** is cash-equivalent. The remainder depends on contingent stock, asset-transfer credits, and performance conditions. Its apparent price is competitive; its closing quality is weaker.

Mark is withholding this because revealing it early would allow every seller bloc to negotiate against Kintsugi's headline number rather than against Viz-Roll's actual closing needs.

### Institutional Commitments

- the estate supports it;
- the cash-equivalent value is at least **485,000,000¥**;
- funded post-closing obligations are not counted as purchase price;

These two indications are nonbinding and will be withdrawn if leaked.

## Failure Result

If Mark cannot secure a single buyer and a majority coalition, the estate will begin a controlled sell-down within sixty days. Institutional holders will follow, the share price will fall, and the board will begin selling separable rights, regional contracts, archives, and distribution relationships to preserve creditor and estate value.

Viz-Roll may continue as a legal shell, but it will cease to function as the integrated company being negotiated for now.

## Negotiation Consequences

- The seller side is fundamentally aligned on the need to sell.
- Continuity, culture, staff protection, and regional authority are negotiated conditions, not separate reasons to preserve ownership indefinitely.
- The estate block is necessary but insufficient.
- North Pacific and Cascadia form the fastest path from estate support to voting control.
- Retail ownership cannot organize a competing control position in time.
- Corporate insiders cannot clear the company from sale.
- A buyer that cannot produce at least **475,000,000¥** in realizable equity value is unlikely to receive a fairness recommendation.
- A clean **501,400,000¥** equity structure is likely to win despite a higher but contingent competing headline offer.
