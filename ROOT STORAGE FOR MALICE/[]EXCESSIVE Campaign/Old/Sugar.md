## Story Detail

Evangeline Mae Kincaid was born into Oklahoma oil money: mineral rights, private wells, petrochemical leases, lawyers with old family names, and a household where Mass was not optional. The Kincaids are old-school Catholics in the hard-edged way: rosaries in every bedroom, saints on the walls, Latin phrases in the family chapel, polished manners at dinner, and obedience treated as a virtue only when it flows upward.

They called the headware an education investment. Her tutors called it "remedial discipline." Evangeline remembers it as a private clinic, a white ceiling, and waking up with expensive hardware behind her eyes and ears before she had enough adult rights to meaningfully refuse. Datajack, chipjacks, memory, orientation, and machine-aided cognition were all sold as preparation for the family business. An oil heiress should know contracts, maps, drilling rights, numbers, etiquette, and how to smile without giving anything away.

Then she left.

She did not leave with the trust. She did not leave with the accounts. She did not leave with lawyers. She left with the ware already in her head, a bad bag of clothes, her boots, a pocket secretary, a cheap pistol she hates carrying, and a style loud enough to make people look at the pink hair instead of the fear.

In Seattle she goes by **Sugar**. The longer tag, **糖度過剰警報**, is something between a warning label and a dare: too much sugar, too much color, too much noise, too much girl for anyone back home to fold back into a chapel pew. She is not Japanese; the tag came out of club slang, machine translation, vending-machine pop culture, and a teen's instinct for making a name that sounds like a sticker slapped over a warning sign. Most street people just call her **Sugar**.

She is not a finished shadowrunner yet. She is the step before that: courier, rooftop scout, club kid, distraction, listener, staircase rat, illegal tenant, and sometimes the only person in the room who knows how to talk both to a corporate brat and to the ganger blocking the fire exit. She is young enough to still mistake survival for freedom, but not young enough to miss the locks on the doors.

## Final Appearance Description

Sugar is a slim eighteen-year-old human woman with a bright, artificial, over-sweet street presence. Her hair is usually dyed a loud candy pink and worn in high twin ponytails, too long and too carefully chaotic to be practical. She keeps glossy pink goggles on her head or over her eyes, more fashion than function, though she treats them like armor. Her eyes are sharp, watchful, and usually framed by the bored look of someone pretending not to be hungry.

Her clothes are a deliberately Sugar mix of thrift-store punk and salvaged protection: black cropped synthleather, an oversized white jacket splashed with pink paint, black-and-white cargo pants, belt straps, cheap chains, and heavy platform boots with scraped toes. From ten meters away she looks like nightlife. From two meters away the illusion breaks: the jacket is patched, the boots are old, the jewelry is plated junk, and the expensive part of her is all under the skull.

She is usually chewing pink bubblegum. It gives people something to remember that is not her accent, her hands, or the way she counts exits.

On rooftops she looks most natural leaning against HVAC units in grey Seattle mist, high above apartment blocks she cannot afford to live in properly. She acts like she owns the skyline because she owns almost nothing else.

## Home / Lifestyle

**Lifestyle:** Low address, Squatter reality  
**Cost:** 500 nuyen/month, treated as Squatter cost by agreement  
**Location:** ninth floor of a damaged Seattle apartment block  
**Actual condition:** the door lock works and there is one functioning light in each room. Nothing else is reliable.

The unit is legally enough of an apartment to receive a name on a rent ledger, but not enough of one to be comfortable. Plumbing is dead. Heating comes from neighboring pipes, stolen extension cables, body heat, and whatever warmth leaks up from the building. The windows are dirty, badly sealed, and unsafe to open. The elevator will not stop on nine.

**Furnishings:** one bedroll, one blanket, two milk crates, a cracked plastic basin, and a line of scavenged wire for drying clothes.  
**Security:** a functioning door lock, a chain, and paranoia.  
**Important detail:** the apartment is cheap because nobody with options wants it.

## Contacts and Complications

### Deke "Broker" Malloy — Level 1 Contact, fixer-wannabe

Deke wants to be a fixer badly enough to become dangerous. He has a little street information, a few weak introductions, and an ugly sense of ownership over people he "discovers." He thinks Sugar is his breakout act: pretty enough to remember, desperate enough to pressure, and connected enough to maybe become valuable.

He gave her a piece of jewelry as a gift: a bright charm that fits her style and looks like cheap affectionate trash. It is actually a locator beacon / micro-transmitter. She does not know that at campaign start.

**Use at table:** Deke can provide minor jobs, rumors, and introductions, but every interaction should feel like a favor with hooks in it. He is a contact and a threat.

### Mrs. Annette "Auntie Nettie" Voss — Level 1 Contact, hidden street doc

The old lady across the hall looks like a lonely neighbor with tea, rosary beads, swollen hands, and too much patience. She complains about the stairs, leaves soup outside doors, and knows exactly when Sugar comes home bleeding.

She is actually a retired or semi-retired street doc operating out of rooms the building forgot how to bill. Sugar does not know the full truth at first. She knows only that Mrs. Voss has steady hands, old medical slang, and no shock at the sight of blood.

**Use at table:** starts as a nice old lady / neighbor contact. Once revealed, she becomes the safest medical help Sugar has, though not necessarily the cheapest.

## Character Use at the Table

Sugar is best used as a starting runner who is strong at social survival, stealth, rooftop movement, and low-end technical intrusion. She is not built to win stand-up fights. She is built to get into a building, talk past the first obstacle, move across the roof, open the wrong panel, grab the thing, and leave before anyone important decides she matters.

Her strengths are high Charisma, high Intelligence, high Willpower, excellent Etiquette, excellent Stealth, and enough Electronics / Computer skill to make small systems nervous. Her weaknesses are low cash, low Body, no initiative augmentation, no reliable team, no safe family, and contacts who can hurt her by helping.

### Good first jobs

- rooftop courier work between apartment blocks
- finding a hidden junction box on a building roof
- distracting a security guard in a lobby
- getting a runner team through a maintenance stairwell
- identifying gang tags before the team walks into the wrong hallway
- delivering data physically because the Matrix route is watched
- stealing a tenant ledger, access code, or security schedule
- escorting someone through nightlife without making them look like prey

### Personal hooks

- Her family has disowned her and things she sold her body on a BTL host, reality is that AI overlays of her were made by a jealous high school enemy
- Deke's tracking jewelry leads trouble directly to her ninth-floor unit.
- Auntie Nettie recognizes the clinic work in her head and knows who installed it.
- One of the chips in her old education set contains data that was never meant for a runaway.
- The apartment building's HVAC system hides a connection into a private security network.
- Someone in Seattle knows the Kincaid name and thinks an oil heiress must still be worth ransom money.

## Tone Notes

Play her as poor, not helpless. Loud, not carefree. Young, not stupid. She is colorful because the city is grey and because fear is easier to manage when everyone is looking at the costume instead of the girl inside it.

She has oil money in her past, expensive chrome in her head, one working light in each room, and no guarantee that the door will hold.
