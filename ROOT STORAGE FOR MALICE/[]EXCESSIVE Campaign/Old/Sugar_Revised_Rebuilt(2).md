## Story Detail

Evangeline Mae Kincaid was born into Oklahoma oil money: mineral rights, private wells, petrochemical leases, lawyers with old family names, and a household where Mass was not optional. The Kincaids are old-school Catholics in the hard-edged way: rosaries in every bedroom, saints on the walls, Latin phrases in the family chapel, polished manners at dinner, and obedience treated as a virtue only when it flows upward.

They called the headware an education investment. Evangeline remembers a private clinic, a white ceiling, and waking with expensive hardware behind her eyes and ears before she had enough adult rights to meaningfully refuse. The ware was sold as preparation for family business. An oil heiress was expected to understand contracts, maps, drilling rights, numbers, etiquette, and how to smile without giving anything away.

Then she left.

She did not leave with the trust, accounts, lawyers, or a safe identity. She left with the ware already in her head, a bad bag of clothes, her boots, a pocket secretary, a cheap pistol she hates carrying, and a style loud enough to make people look at the pink hair instead of the fear.

In Seattle she became **Sugar**. The longer tag, **糖度過剰警報**, is a warning label and a dare: too much sugar, too much color, too much noise, too much girl for anyone back home to fold into a chapel pew. She is not Japanese; the tag came from club slang, machine translation, vending-machine pop culture, and a teenager's instinct for making a name that looks like a sticker slapped across a warning sign.

Sugar arrived in Seattle with real competence: elite social instincts, professional athletic control, strong stealth, trained pistol handling, technical literacy, and a private-school education built to make an oil-family daughter useful. Her early street failures came from weak criminal ecology and bad dependencies, not from lack of ability.

**Hermione Alonzo** is a performed civilian/corporate persona, not a separate personality and not a replacement for Sugar. Hermione is used when people and systems need a polished civilian identity. Sugar remains the street identity.

## Appearance

Sugar is a slim eighteen-year-old human woman with a bright, artificial, over-sweet street presence. Her hair is usually dyed loud candy pink and worn in high twin ponytails, too long and too carefully chaotic to be practical. Glossy pink goggles sit on her head or over her eyes, more fashion than function.

Her clothes mix thrift-store punk and salvaged protection: black cropped synthleather, an oversized white jacket splashed with pink paint, black-and-white cargo pants, belt straps, cheap chains, and heavy platform boots with scraped toes. From ten meters away she looks like nightlife. From two meters away the illusion breaks: patched jacket, old boots, plated junk, expensive hardware hidden under the skull.

She is usually chewing pink bubblegum. It gives people something to remember that is not her accent, her hands, or the way she counts exits.
