Friday, February 25, 2050 — 12:08–16:42 — Sandy/Hermione House / Bitter Lake / North Seattle feeds

For the first few minutes after Sugar leaves, the house is too quiet.

Not silent. The new place has too many systems to be silent. Refrigerator compressor. air cycling. water in pipes. the little settled creak of a large house warming and cooling unevenly. The crock pot ticks and steams in the kitchen, garlic and onions pushing hard through the rooms like a claim of ownership. Bread dough rises under covered bowls. Boxes sit in ordered rows instead of panic piles.

The house looks more like a residence than it did yesterday.

That is useful.

That is also irritating.

The Sandy/Hermione cover begins to develop the first kind of boring weight that can survive daylight: detergent stacked where detergent belongs, meal boxes marked by date and use, bathroom goods separated, household fabric staged, not everything perfect enough to look staged. A few things are deliberately left slightly human: one blanket folded badly, a cup near the sink, a shopping bag not yet broken down. Enough order to be lived in. Enough imperfection to be normal.

Outside, the security rhythm stays dull.

One service vehicle rolls through the block at 12:31, slow enough to be part of somebody’s route, not slow enough to be a probe. Its driver never looks at the house directly. A neighborhood delivery drone stutters over the next street and corrects course. A dog barks two houses down. A woman in a dark coat walks past with a folding shopping cart and does the normal middle-class glance at the big house in the center of the block: inventory without interest.

The block believes it is protected.

That is one of the invisible luxuries of the place. The neighbors do not need to know the true shape of the protection to benefit from it. They know big house, money, security response, maybe someone important, maybe not worth the trouble. The astral side knows more and less at the same time. Something warm with teeth owns the house. Something high and storm-heavy is not local enough to pick a fight with. Something city-shaped passed through and left the kind of feeling awakened people talk around instead of about.

No one tests it.

Not today.
