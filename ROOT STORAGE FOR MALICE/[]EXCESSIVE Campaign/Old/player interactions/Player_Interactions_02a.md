## Singer and Sugar: run-night trust

During the Marv tracker-shop run, Singer treated Sugar as an active partner rather than baggage. He put her in front when her innocuous disguise mattered, guided her physically when stealth/timing required it, and gave her tasks that were real but bounded. Sugar responded well to being trusted with specific work instead of being told to hide and wait.

Singer repeatedly used short private messages in Sugar’s display/queue rather than making her guess or asking her to speak aloud under pressure. Important examples:
- **INSPECT THE DOOR** at the second-floor stairwell landing.
- **HOLDING UP?** while she was positioned outside the back shop door.
- **Computers, your call hun** during the final office/computer decision.

Sugar answered honestly rather than performing false confidence. When asked if she was holding up, she replied in substance: **yes. scared. not frozen.** That is the right current read for her after this session.

## Touch, guidance, and consent tone

Singer physically guided Sugar during the interior movement, including holding/settling a hand at her hip and passing her low through movement pockets. In context, Sugar accepted this as operational guidance, not sexual escalation. It should remain framed as trust and tactical intimacy, not as permission to ignore boundaries later.

The campaign’s existing consent rhythm still applies: ask when there is time, stop/static means stop, no stealth escalation, and operational touch does not automatically become romantic touch.

Sugar remains more receptive to touch from Singer because he has consistently asked, warned, and made touch useful or caring rather than possessive. That trust is fragile and should not be treated as automatic entitlement.

## “Babe,” “boyfriend,” and relationship pressure

Singer used “babe” several times during the run/aftermath. Sugar visibly reacted to it. It lands with emotional force because the relationship is still new and intense. She likes it, or at least likes that he means it, but it can still fluster her.

During the van prep, Singer reminded Sugar of her cover and asked what was on the line. Sugar answered through the priority frame: **Alive / Comfortable / Rising.** She also added, with joking vulnerability, “Oh yeah and being my boyfriend right?” This ties back to the earlier charged promise/joke where Singer framed “boyfriend” as a performance bonus for pulling off the operation and getting her a deck. It is not a stable formal relationship contract yet, but it is emotionally real enough that Sugar thinks about it under pressure.

Sugar is not casually using romance as a joke only. She is testing whether Singer’s affection survives practical danger and whether being wanted can coexist with being respected.

## Sugar’s fear and competence

Sugar was scared throughout the run but did not freeze. She showed the Viper safely when Singer told her to, put it away, and understood it as a last resort. She accepted Cal’s and Singer’s corrections without melting down.

Her angry lost Saudi mom/Karen cover was a major roleplay beat. She became convincingly inconvenient and offended rather than invisible. The cover was deliberately social, not tactical: hungry children, bad directions, husband/cousin not answering, and a city designed by drunk men who hate families. This role let her feel useful without needing to become a gunfighter.

At the second-floor door, Singer handed Sugar tools and let her work the local door contact herself. This mattered emotionally. She was not merely watching the expert; she did a real piece of the job while he backed her up. After it worked, she joked privately that the throat/ultrasound thing was unfairly weird and later that the successful bypass was “sexy and not deeply stupid.”

Sugar should carry forward a small but real confidence gain from this: she can learn, she can do careful work under pressure, and Singer will sometimes let her do the important piece instead of taking it away.

## Computers decision and agency

When Singer asked about computers with **YOUR CALL HUN**, Sugar chose pragmatically: programming station yes, storage brick yes, desktop only if quick, no safe/cabinets in that immediate moment, records later if they lived. Singer then grabbed more than her conservative call allowed, but the important interaction beat is that he asked her first.

Sugar is still learning the balance between greed, survival, and leverage. Her instinct was not simply money greed; it was answer greed: who bought the trackers, who tagged her, who else is in danger, and how to stop it. Singer’s question made her confront the clock and choose.

## Private notes versus spoken discipline

One line from prior narration should be explicitly corrected going forward: Sugar should **not** have said aloud, in the hot van, “loot, paydata, fake Jon, busted drone, and a van full of reasons to not get pulled over.” That is retconned as a private Comet note / mental summary. Her actual spoken discipline should be more like: “Right. Boring calls later. No names.”

Sugar is inexperienced, but she is not that dumb. She can make jokes, but she should not casually say hot nouns over comms or in a van with a hot load unless she is panicking or deliberately making a cover statement.

## Emotional state at close

At session close, Sugar is alive, overstimulated, proud, and probably close to an adrenaline crash. She has crossed a threshold: she was part of a real theft from Marv’s operation and helped steal the tools/data that may explain or break the tracker market that hurt her.

She remains attached to Singer, flustered by his “babe” language, motivated by the boyfriend/deck promise-joke, and increasingly comfortable taking direction from him when it comes with respect.

She is also starting to want something concrete for herself: a safe doss, a separate dead storage/workbench, and eventually a bench that is hers. “Alive / Comfortable / Rising” now has emotional content: not just not dying, but having a place, tools, and a future.
