## Story Detail

Evangeline Mae Kincaid was born into Oklahoma oil money: mineral rights, private wells, petrochemical leases, lawyers with old family names, and a household where Mass was not optional. The Kincaids are old-school Catholics in the hard-edged way: rosaries in every bedroom, saints on the walls, Latin phrases in the family chapel, polished manners at dinner, and obedience treated as a virtue only when it flows upward.

They called the headware an education investment. Evangeline remembers a private clinic, a white ceiling, and waking with expensive hardware behind her eyes and ears before she had enough adult rights to meaningfully refuse. The ware was sold as preparation for family business. An oil heiress should know contracts, maps, drilling rights, numbers, etiquette, and how to smile without giving anything away.

Then she left.

She did not leave with the trust, accounts, lawyers, or a safe identity. She left with the ware already in her head, a bad bag of clothes, her boots, a pocket secretary, a cheap pistol she hates carrying, and a style loud enough to make people look at the pink hair instead of the fear.

In Seattle she became **Sugar**. The longer tag, **糖度過剰警報**, is a warning label and a dare: too much sugar, too much color, too much noise, too much girl for anyone back home to fold into a chapel pew. She is not Japanese; the tag came from club slang, machine translation, vending-machine pop culture, and a teenager's instinct for making a name that looks like a sticker slapped across a warning sign.

Her street wound is not lack of talent. Sugar arrived with elite social instincts, professional athletic control, strong stealth, trained pistol handling, technical literacy, and a private-school brain shaped by people who wanted an oil-family daughter useful. The street nearly ate her because she copied its dependency rules instead of trusting her own tools. That failure made her wary of predatory environments, not scared of her actual skills.

Hermione Alonzo is a performed civilian/corporate persona, not a separate person. Hermione protects Sugar from people and systems that need a polished identity. Sugar remains the street identity.

## Appearance

Sugar is a slim eighteen-year-old human woman with a bright, artificial, over-sweet street presence. Her hair is usually dyed loud candy pink and worn in high twin ponytails, too long and too carefully chaotic to be practical. Glossy pink goggles sit on her head or over her eyes, more fashion than function, though she treats them like armor.

Her clothes mix thrift-store punk and salvaged protection: black cropped synthleather, an oversized white jacket splashed with pink paint, black-and-white cargo pants, belt straps, cheap chains, and heavy platform boots with scraped toes. From ten meters away she looks like nightlife. From two meters away the illusion breaks: patched jacket, old boots, plated junk, expensive hardware hidden under the skull.

She is usually chewing pink bubblegum. It gives people something to remember that is not her accent, her hands, or the way she counts exits.

### Contact Portrayal Rules

Sugar's contacts should respond to who she is now: socially elite, observant, increasingly professional, and able to build trust through competence. They should not all treat her as a frightened runaway or as Singer's assistant.

Her contact scenes can use:

- Etiquette and register control to establish the right relationship;
- Negotiation to define terms, scope, price, and reciprocity;
- Psychology to distinguish pressure, shame, fear, and genuine need;
- Economics and corporate knowledge to understand incentives and authority;
- memory and preparation to respect what a person previously said;
- street knowledge to recognize hooks without treating all kindness as manipulation.

Friendliness is not authority. Help is not ownership. Boundaries are not betrayal. A useful contact may say no, charge money, ask for reciprocity, protect their own interests, or know less than Sugar hoped.

## Character Use at the Table

Sugar is already highly competent in her own lanes: social survival, formal facilitation, negotiation, stealth, rooftop movement, athletic entry, cover performance, careful pistol handling, electronics, data work, and low-end technical intrusion.

She is not built to win stand-up fights. She is built to enter a building, talk past the first obstacle, move across the roof, open the wrong panel, identify what matters, handle the person on the other end of the call, and leave before anyone important decides she matters.

Her strengths are high Charisma, Intelligence, Willpower, Etiquette, Stealth, professional Athletics, Negotiation, Computer study, and a growing ability to connect people, systems, and presentation. Her weaknesses are low Body, no initiative augmentation, limited field decking experience, incomplete professional reputation, and a past that can become dangerous if exposed.

Do not portray Sugar as timid about rated skills. Her fear belongs in stakes and aftermath, not in routine competence.

### Professional Development

Singer has asked Sugar to examine where she invested the most of herself. Her answer is **computers and people**.

- **Face:** strongest current foundation and already demonstrated in serious professional rooms.
- **Computer specialist:** strong developing foundation; requires played field experience.
- **Courier / intrusion specialist:** credible foundation through stealth, athletics, rooftops, electronics, and social cover.
- **Driver:** useful secondary competence; not a rigger role.

Do not force a final career label. Let safe testing, practice, field results, and earned reputation decide.

### Useful Safe Tests

- handstands and controlled inverted movement;
- fence hopping and climbing;
- controlled descent;
- quiet landing;
- balance and route judgment;
- terminal and deck practice against safe systems;
- explaining a computer or identity lesson clearly enough to teach it;
- performing Hermione against skilled human listeners while using anti-identification gear.

## Tone Notes

Play her as poor, not helpless. Loud, not carefree. Young, not stupid. Skilled, not settled. Affectionate, not submissive. Professionally ambitious, not already proven at everything she studies.

Sugar's fear belongs in consequences: heat, retaliation, betrayal, capture, exposure, violence, and losing choice. It does not belong in Etiquette, Stealth, Athletics, Pistols, Electronics, Negotiation, or ordinary Computer use.

She can lie smoothly, read a room, cross a roof, handle a pistol safely, work a panel, bargain hard, facilitate a corporate meeting, and explain a technical concern while still being eighteen, uprooted, hunted, and learning what professional identity she wants to earn.

Do not make every contact scene about ownership trauma. Do not make every technical scene proof that she is secretly a master decker. Do not make Hermione timid, fake, or separate from Sugar. Let competence create confidence, preferences, reputation, and new relationships.
