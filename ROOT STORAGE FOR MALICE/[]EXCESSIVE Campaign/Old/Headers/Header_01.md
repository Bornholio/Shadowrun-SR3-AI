## Tracker economics lesson and real opportunity

This beat should be remembered as a character/plot pivot, not a shopping list. Singer used the tracker charm to force Sugar to stop thinking like a victim reacting to Deke and start thinking like an operator reading a market. The lesson began with apparently simple arithmetic: the charm was not magic, not priceless, and not a unique piece of Deke genius. It was cheap plastic jewelry dressed up as a favor, fitted with a low-grade transceiver and a cheap tracking signal, then sold into a SINless/non-permit market where fear, ignorance, and lack of legal access let the seller mark it up brutally. Singer estimated that a leash Deke/Marv could retail for around 1700-1800¥ might have only a few hundred nuyen of marginal input cost if the parts came from a stolen shipment or similarly discounted channel.

Sugar’s first emotional read was correct but incomplete: Deke had not merely betrayed her; someone was making bank by turning girls, couriers, and desperate street people into tagged inventory. She wanted to hurt that market. Singer let the first fantasy form - buy or obtain fifty trackers, flood the market, break exclusivity, and turn the leash into a known joke - then made her kill the fantasy herself. That correction matters for her future characterization. She now understands that projected street value is not money in hand. Buying fifty at street price would require impossible capital. Getting them near input cost means stealing from Marv or the supplier chain, not clever shopping. Flooding them carelessly could burn their quiet advantage, teach copycats, or fail to hurt Deke if he already sold his batch. Her true earnings from the MOTH packet remain 11945¥ plus relationship/intel value; the 23k+ idea was not profit, it was a mirage made from gross value and anger.

The revised opportunity is darker and more professional. The score is not to purchase trackers, but to take marginal-cost inventory out of Marv’s shop/supply chain before it can be turned into high-markup leashes. That makes the target Marv’s inventory and logistics, not Deke’s wounded ego. A hot dump is probably closer to 30% of street value, not 50%, especially if the inventory is known-hot and someone may be gunning for the meet. Team economics cut the fantasy down further: a driver gets a full share because the vehicle is an ablative asset and because loiter/exfil is what keeps the team alive; a samurai or gun hand may be needed if bodies are expected; covert/intrusion talent may be needed depending on the site; Sugar’s cut must be argued from face negotiation, intel, contact path, and participation rather than assumed because she found the thread.

Singer’s working model is a leaner three-way score if he handles the drop-in/intrusion role himself: Sugar finds and verifies a driver with a van and drone, Singer handles the dangerous entry side, and the driver provides mobility, drone overwatch, loiter discipline, and exfil. This is still not clean money. It is a real shadowrun with risk, time pressure, buyer risk, and possible retaliation. The rough window is three days before the opportunity goes stale, maybe less because they spent time hidden while Singer recovered and Marv-space has already gone quiet in the dangerous way.

Sugar’s job now is not “get revenge.” It is to find a driver who is hungry but not sloppy, not tied to Deke, and verifiable through MOTH/Skein/rail-adjacent channels without dragging Black Calf directly into the fire. She should pitch a scoped paid driving job: short window, vehicle risk priced in, hot pickup/hot exit, drone overwatch, limited disclosure until trust is proven.
