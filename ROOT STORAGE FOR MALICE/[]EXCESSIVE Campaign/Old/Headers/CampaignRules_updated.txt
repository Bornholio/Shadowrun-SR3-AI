Shadowrun Deniability and Character Knowledge - Fundamental Rule

Deniability is a fundamental rule of Shadowrun, not merely a table preference. Shadowrunners operate through street names, handles, false identities, disposable channels, cutouts, and compartmentalized knowledge. A runner's real name, legal identity, real SIN, birth family, civilian history, clinic trail, personal records, contacts, enemies, and hidden capabilities are not casual knowledge. They are leverage, exposure, arrest vectors, extraction vectors, blackmail vectors, and threats to family or civilian connections.

Characters only know what they have actually perceived, been told, reasonably inferred from visible evidence, or established through play. Uploaded notes, GM-facing hooks, character files, hidden contact details, and campaign context must never leak into NPC dialogue, NPC suspicion, narration from a limited point of view, or character conclusions unless that character has an in-fiction route to know it.

Sugar is known on the street as Sugar, No one knows her real name, That name is buried identity context and must not be used by contacts, tenants, gangs, casual employers, Deke, Auntie Nettie, or Singer unless play specifically establishes how they learned it.

Singer receives the same protection. His legal identity, buried history, real capabilities, contacts, enemies, and past resources are not active public context. His presence should not cause NPCs to magically know who he is, what he can do, or what his history means.

Contacts begin as at least neutral and potentially helpful unless play establishes otherwise. A contact may have flaws, limits, conflicting incentives, debt, fear, greed, or complications, but a contact is not secretly an enemy by default. Contact complications should create playable choices and possible compensating structure rather than simply punishing the player for having contacts.

Deke's tracker subplot is allowed, but it must resolve into playable structure and compensation such as leverage, replacement contact access, cleaner contact boundaries, or a changed relationship that does not just remove a contact and punish Sugar.

Mrs. Voss / Auntie Nettie is a real person first: neighbor, possible helpful contact, and someone with normal limits. She is not omniscient, not an evil granny by default, and not an automatic surveillance device across the hall. She will not notice plausible building-level things if there is an in-fiction reason, DO NOT USE HER IN THIS WAY! this is using a benefit as a full time drawback.

End-of-session header files must carry this rule forward. Include a reminder that hidden identities and GM-only hooks stay hidden until revealed in play, and that NPC knowledge is bounded by what the NPC could actually know.
