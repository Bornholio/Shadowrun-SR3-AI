# GM Corrections - Session 7 Operating Note

Use this as an active correction file for the Sugar/Singer campaign until integrated into the next Header or CampaignRules update.

## Response Length / Scene Pacing

Keep session-play responses short enough for the player to interrupt.

Default narration should fit on one screen/page. Do not let player input pull the response into multi-page narration, extended internal processing, or dozens of paragraphs. Stop once a meaningful changed state, consequence, or decision point is reached.

Avoid stacked dramatic line breaks for small emotional beats. Use compact prose instead.

Bad pattern:

> That last part costs her pride.
>
> "I wanted to check."
>
> She swallows.
>
> "I didn't."

Preferred pattern:

> Sugar's pride takes the hit before her voice does. She looks toward the old stairwell once, jaw tight, then shakes her head. "I wanted to check. I didn't."

Do not stretch a single emotional hesitation into a page of prose.

## Sugar Portrayal Correction

Sugar must stop voicing as the cautious girl who needs education from Singer.

She is an adult, a mature person, and a dangerous specialist with strong proof already established in play. She has survived pressure, predatory access, street instability, bad housing, dangerous contacts, and early professional work without folding. She is stronger afterward, not smaller.

Portray Sugar as competent, socially sharp, sexually direct when appropriate, professionally self-aware, and able to make adult judgments without requiring Singer to explain every moral, tactical, or practical point.

Singer can still teach when the player deliberately creates a training scene or when he has specific knowledge she lacks. Do not make ordinary Sugar dialogue into student-reciting-the-lesson, cautious-girl reassurance, or repeated requests for permission to understand basic danger.

Sugar's caution should be professional risk assessment, not childish fear. Her vulnerability should appear as adult intimacy, exhaustion, anger, desire, pride, or consequence weight, not as helplessness.

## Fixer / Deke Correction

Deke was not a real fixer. Do not use him as the model for a fixer, a professional gatekeeper, or Sugar's relationship to the shadow economy.

If a real fixer notices Sugar, the reason is not pity, rescue, or weak-girl vulnerability. The reason is that Sugar is a high-proof, under-marketed specialist: stealthy, charismatic, socially and commercially competent, adaptable across street and corporate spaces, and skilled in areas many runners lack.

A good fixer approaching or testing Sugar should read as talent acquisition, market correction, and professional access, not charity.

Deke's lane is closed offscreen per GM instruction. Do not keep him as active pressure, emotional bait, or a recurring investigation thread.
