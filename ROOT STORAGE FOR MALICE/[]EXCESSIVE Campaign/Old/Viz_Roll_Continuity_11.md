## Technical Compromise

### Structural Flaw

Viz-Roll's generated executable and media directory formation is structurally poor.

The flaw allows programs and media to be split, recovered, and cracked in chunks rather than as one industrially difficult whole.

### Demonstrated Scale

- Small advertising material can expose original source unusually quickly.
- A tiny children's-show sample demonstrated the problem to Mark.
- A conventional 500 Mp Full-X production might represent roughly 750-1,000 days of base effort before expert parallelization.
- With proper expert parallel effort, that might fall to roughly ten days.
- Viz-Roll's flaw reduces comparable recovery to same-day work, approximately one twenty-fourth of the expected expert-parallel time.
- The danger is not that every casual pirate can crack everything.
- The danger is that industrial source recovery becomes operational rather than strategic.

### Three Verification Titles

Sugar acquired three low-cost, under-50 Mp, simvert-heavy releases:

- **Neon Orchard, Episode 4**;
- **Little Comet Bakery**;
- **Steel Harbor recap**.

Results:

- all showed vulnerability by approximately ten percent completion;
- more than half of each package was advertising material;
- each was cracked and recompiled;
- each optimized to under 12 Mp;
- final copies were burned to three optical memory chips.

### Business Meaning

- The flaw threatens production and distribution value, not merely ordinary piracy exposure.
- It can justify urgency, remediation authority, seller protections, and controlled technical governance.
- It must not be used as a threat to crush price.
- Public exposure before terms are controlled could damage every side.
- Mark requested answers to:
  - whether the weakness persists across multiple titles;
  - whether it appears accidental or designed;
  - whether exploitation can be detected afterward.
- Multiple-title persistence is now established.
- Designed versus accidental and post-exploitation detectability remain open.
