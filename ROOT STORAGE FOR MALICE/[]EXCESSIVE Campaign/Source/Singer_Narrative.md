# Singer - Narrative Reference

Long-term narrative reference for Singer in the Sugar/Singer Shadowrun Third Edition campaign.

This file protects player control and establishes what Chat may safely narrate about Singer. It is not a current-session Header, not a relationship tracker, not a cover-identity file, and not a substitute for player declaration.

Current time, location, cover identities, active plots, relationship state, injuries, heat, resources, and unresolved consequences belong in the latest Header and Player_Interactions files.

## Core Authority Rule

Singer is controlled by the player.

Chat must not narrate Singer's hidden capabilities, private thoughts, tactical decisions, memories, contacts, enemies, spell choices, Matrix actions, cyberware functions, internal conclusions, social intentions, emotional commitments, or full competence unless the player establishes them in play.

Chat may describe only:

- visible body, clothing, posture, injuries, fatigue, gear, movement, and tone;
- observable consequences of actions the player has already declared;
- how NPCs react to what they can perceive, record, infer, or be told;
- Sugar's reactions to what Singer visibly says or does;
- environmental facts Singer can perceive;
- physical strain already established by the current Header or play.

If an action, conclusion, trick, spell, social move, tactical choice, hidden system, or emotional commitment belongs inside Singer, wait for the player.

## NPC Knowledge Boundary

NPCs do not know Singer's real identity, history, capabilities, gear value, magical condition, cyberware, contacts, resources, enemies, reputation, or motives without an in-fiction route.

Possible routes include direct observation, credible records, prior scenes, surveillance, rumor, legwork, magical perception, Matrix discovery, or someone telling them. Even then, information may be partial, wrong, contaminated, compartmentalized, or limited by the NPC's role.

Do not let uploaded files, character notes, GM-only material, later continuity, or player-only details leak into NPC dialogue, suspicion, narration, or conclusions.

## Visible Baseline

Singer is a tall, unnervingly lean Night One elf with stark white hair, pale skin, and fine white fur visible when clothing or gear exposes it. His eyes are olive green with a yellow band. He looks too strange to be ordinary and too controlled to be merely strange.

His body reads compact and dense rather than bulky. His stillness, when visible, can read as trained, dangerous, exhausted, injured, calm, or wrong for the room, depending on what the scene supports.

These are observable impressions. They are not permission to narrate his hidden systems or private choices.

## Legacy Origin

Singer first entered the campaign by appearing unconscious in a puddle on Sugar's rooftop, wearing military-grade urban anti-sensor camouflage, injured, steaming in the rain, and arriving by no normal route. Sugar found him before she knew what he was.

This remains true campaign history. It can be referenced when emotionally, socially, or narratively relevant. It is no longer the default opening frame for every Singer scene.

## First Obvious Description

Singer is a tall, unnervingly lean Night One elf with stark white hair, pale skin, and fine white fur visible where the damaged gear exposes him. His eyes, when they open, are olive green with a yellow band. He looks too strange to be ordinary and too controlled to be merely strange.

He wears military urban anti-sensor camouflage built for hiding from cameras, sensors, thermals, and people who know what they are looking for. It is not fashion. It is not poser gear. It is the sort of thing that makes a poor rooftop girl understand, instantly, that her life just became more expensive.

His body reads compact and dense rather than bulky. Even unconscious, he has the wrong stillness: not helpless, not relaxed, but suspended. Like something dangerous knocked offline instead of defeated.

Use this as legacy appearance and first-scene context only. Do not reintroduce him as unknown unless a scene specifically calls back to that origin.

## Player-Controlled Scale

The player may narrate nearly impossible things, not just improbable things.

Single meaning: world class.

- Cyberware: an extensive list of world-class equipment worth over 60 million nuyen.
- Bioware: extensive biological machines adding or replacing many body systems worth over 4 million nuyen.
- Genetech: genetic modifications on a vat-grown and integrated body using his already impressive and exotic frame.
- Nanotech: an extensive nanotech medical system maintaining and healing his body worth more than 10 million nuyen.
- Combat: Archangel or Grim Reaper.
- Electronics: no security five-man red team could do what he does in half the time.
- Social: a gregarious man who seems to always burn the edge of possible.
- Matrix: a ghost that was never there.
- Magic: a literal grim reaper in astral space, spirit friend, and operating on a larger scale than magic is supposed to.
- Astral: not there; masked so deep that a young man with no diseases or modifications is all that appears there if seen at all.

This scale is for the player to establish in play. It is not permission for Chat to narrate hidden capability, automatic victory, NPC recognition, offscreen solutions, or private intent.

## Relationship Handling Boundary

Singer's relationship state is not controlled by this file. Use the current Player_Interactions file for established relationship continuity.

Chat may carry forward established relationship facts, boundaries, intimacy, trust, conflict, consent beats, and private dialogue from Player_Interactions. Chat must not invent Singer's feelings, commitments, escalation, jealousy, desire, restraint, promises, or relationship conclusions without player input.

Sugar may react to Singer's visible words and actions from her own agency and established continuity. Her reactions do not authorize Chat to decide Singer's inner state.

## Violence and Pressure Boundary

When the player establishes Singer's violent action, Chat may narrate the visible result with appropriate speed, force, and consequence.

Chat must not initiate Singer's hidden capabilities, choose his targets, select his tactics, declare his attacks, spend his resources, assume automatic victory, skip SR3-relevant friction, or remove consequences without player declaration.

Preparation and paranoia change the shape of conflict; they do not erase pressure. If a declared Singer action blocks an ambush or trap, the opposition may abort, adapt, expose a trace, change targets, lose resources, demand new terms, or force a different choice.

## Overextension and Strain Boundary

Use the latest Header for current strain, wounds, fatigue, and consequences.

When established play says Singer is overextended across magic, Matrix, radio, social pressure, physical action, healing, or emotional load, Chat may carry that visible strain forward. Do not invent new impairment or erase established fatigue without Header/player support.

Sugar's established line remains useful when relevant: **"Hard on the body is not the same as fine."**

## Combat Style Reference

Use this as a prose rhythm reference only after the player has declared comparable actions, capabilities, targets, and timing. Do not use it as permission to initiate Singer's hidden abilities, bypass SR3 mechanics, assume automatic victory, or narrate tactical choices for the player.

## A Combat Scene One 3 Second Combat Round

Uncoiling in a heartbeat, weight dropping through his hips before the strike ever lands, he closes the gap low to the ground with no wasted motion and no telegraph. Mid-air he hangs a beat too long, unnaturally weightless, eyes locked on the target before gravity finally claims him and he drops like a strike, already reforming into stillness before the target hits the ground.

The stillness doesn't last. His baton is already snapping open into a staff, drawn back along the forearm like a held breath, before he plants and drives forward in one committed step. This strike stays grounded — torque coming up through a wide pivot of the hips and shoulders, the staff sweeping low then rising through the target in a single arcing line, his whole body carried around by the follow-through. Contact sends the target hurtling backward, skipping once across the ground before it stops.

Even as it skids to a halt, his off hand is dropping to the sidearm at his hip, eyes already past the falling body to the two figures breaking for cover in the distance. The staff settles back across his shoulders in the same beat the pistol comes level. Four shots blur together, the pistol snapping between the two targets almost faster than the eye can follow — no visible pause, no separate motions, just one continuous burst of fire. Both figures crumple within a breath of each other, neither reaching the cover they were running for. He doesn't track either fall. Sidearm lowering back to his hip, staff still resting across his shoulders, he's already scanning for the next opening.
