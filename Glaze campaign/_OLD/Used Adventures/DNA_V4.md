If Singer and Glaze bypass, expose, neutralize, or alter a published encounter, follow the consequences of that change and then reconnect to the remaining adventure wherever causally possible. Do not force the original scene to happen anyway, and do not replace it with a new invented plot.

# DNA/DOA — GM Conversion Notes

## Plot Synopsis
**The Setup**
Biogene Technologies, a mid-size genetic engineering firm, hires the team to run a datasteal against Aztechnology's Tacoma Research Park. Objective: pull data and physical samples, deliver both to the client for payment.

**What's Actually Being Stolen**
The Metavirus — a recombinant DNA process using a viral vector to inject a fertilized host egg with RNA and enzymes, grafting chosen traits into the developing organism. Unlike enzyme-based bacterial splicing, it scales to organisms as complex as humans and can graft multiple characteristics at once. Perfected, it lets Aztechnology manufacture custom metahuman subspecies.

**Biogene's Plan**
Biogene has located the Tacoma facility and obtained the address of Aztechnology's offsite backup system. They're running a simultaneous double hit: the team physically enters the Research Park to grab the mainframe data and a virus vector sample, while a separate decker team hits the backup system to steal or wipe the research files.

**The Hands of Five**
The Tacoma chapter of Alamos 20,000, tipped off by an informant inside Biogene. They want the virus as a purification tool — revert fertilized metahuman eggs to human, erasing all metahuman species in one generation. Their political front, the Humanis Policlub, would lobby to legalize a variant as a legitimate alternative to "the unfortunate genetic damage that can occur during pregnancy."

They lack the time and the facility's exact location to mount their own raid. Their play is to let Biogene's operation do the work, then take the prize at the handoff.

**The Job Offer**
The team meets Mr. Walker of Biogene at the Space Needle. Entry is via the city sewer system into the Tacoma Research Park — steal the data, get out. The run happens almost immediately after the meeting, leaving effectively no time for legwork.

**Into Beta Section**
The team enters the sewers roughly a kilometer out and works toward the breach point into Beta Section on the facility's lower level, dealing with sewer denizens along the way. Inside, they find an accident has already happened: an infectious variant of the Metavirus spilled about 24 hours earlier, tearing through Beta Section, killing or mutating everything it touched. Survivors began showing physical manifestations within hours; by the team's arrival, few lab workers resemble their original forms. Aztechnology has sealed Beta and the connecting Alpha Section to contain it.

**Objectives On-Site**
Two locations: a secure vault holding viral samples, and an inert data storage unit that can only be accessed via cyberdeck in its current state. Getting both means dealing with the lab's current inhabitants — sane and insane.

**The Docks**
The team heads to the drop point to meet their Biogene contact. The Hands of Five got there first and killed the contact; Alamos 20,000 heavies are waiting in ambush for whoever shows up carrying the goods.

Almost immediately an Aztechnology full-force combat team arrives in pursuit. The firefight between Alamos and Aztechnology gives the team an opening to escape into a nearby warehouse.

**The Warehouse / Ork Underground**
The warehouse was partially burned during the Night of Rage eleven years ago; Ork survivors now use it as an access point to their underground town. Ork children are scurrying in the shadows — a trigger-happy team can easily kill them. Their "baby-sitter," a Metahuman Rights Activist, leads the team to the enclave either way. Reception varies accordingly.

**Wilhem Park**
The Ork town sits in the buried shopping mall formerly known as Wilhem Park. Allan Bronston leads the Night of Rage descendants. Friendly or hostile depending on the children, he tells the team that the Aztechnology facility has been scouring nearby sewers and capturing test subjects. A few days ago a group of Ork women and children were taken — Bronston believes they're held in Alpha Section.

**Dr. Carol Owens**
Bronston also reveals that the Metavirus's original designer, Dr. Carol Owens, is detained in Alpha Section — held over her protests about the corporation's handling of the project. A second run serves three purposes: rescue the Ork captives, free Owens, and potentially recruit her for Biogene. Owens initially believes the team are Aztechnology agents in disguise.

**Second Run**
Alpha Section is untouched by the virus — meaning intelligent, sane guards and functioning security systems. Once inside, the team locates the missing Orks and Owens, who has to be persuaded to come along. Getting out is easy; staying alive is the hard part.

**Aftermath**
Biogene, Alamos 20,000, and Aztechnology are all looking for the team. A Biogene rep settles up quickly for services rendered. The other two are another matter — the team may need to lie low until the heat dies down, though their reputations should rise in the meantime.

## At Hand Over Fist — The Meet

Touching the walls, you feel them damp with various kinds of evil muck. Underfoot, the ground feels dry in some places, then knee-deep in something vaguely passing for water in others. **Behind the Scenes**

This network of tunnels is still useful as a catch for runoff and as an overflow vent for the new system, but mostly it just collects debris — natural, Human, and otherwise.

The odor is staggeringly brutal. Though the team may be repulsed, they will not have problems breathing after a bit. Only the most sensitive will have difficulties, and then only if the GM is in a particularly vile mood.

The GM's map of the Tacoma Sewer System is accurate in showing a cave-in or two that will make passage less direct than the team's own map would indicate. **Light and Navigation**
Visibility in the sewer tunnels is restricted. Without external light, nothing is visible — not even to a character with low-light eyes. (Low-light works through light amplification; *some* light must be present.) A flashlight illuminates 20 meters in a narrow area. An area-style camping light illuminates up to 10 meters all around.

At each intersection there is a 3 in 6 chance of coming upon a manhole opening into the streets. In any other section, the chance is 1 in 6.

**Toxin Exposure**
Regardless of whether the team wears the chemsuits provided by Biogene, they will be subject to minor physical effects from contact with sewer toxins. These show up as rashes and blotchiness over the body.

Each character makes a **Body Test against TN 10**. Chemsuit rating applies as a TN reduction, as do any applicable disease resistance modifiers. Baseline coverage is 80 percent of the body; **each success eliminates 15 percent** of the affected area.

The team should be led to believe the effects could be much worse. Note that the effects will only surface 1D6 days *after* the completion of the entire **DNA/DOA** adventure — leaving the team more inclined to blame possible contamination during their visits to the Aztechnology labs. Very few things get characters squirming faster than the threat of BIO-HAZARD.

**Through the Wall**
Once the team reaches the appropriate tunnel section, they must decide *how* to get through the wall. The tunnel is Normal Concrete (Barrier Rating 8). The team may attempt magic or explosives, but they should have considered this ahead of time.

Beyond the tunnel wall is the Plastisteel-7™ wall surrounding the primary lab area — off-white plastic with an almost mirror-like gloss, some 30 centimeters thick, Barrier Rating 35, and virtually impervious to any form of attack. The team should have the catalyst agent provided by Biogene. Contained in a small, palm-sized canister, the catalyst is in aerosol form and must be sprayed directly on the material. The spray covers roughly 4 square meters (4m x 4m). Each canister contains enough for two uses.

Once applied, the catalyst turns the Plastisteel-7™ bright pink. After a moment the material begins to bubble slightly, then steam, then lose its luster. At this point the catalyst has done its work, and the plastisteel takes on the consistency of chewed bubble gum. To enter the ventilation duct system, the team will have to push their way through the wall section — and it will look and feel exactly as though they are crawling through a wall of bubble gum.

By reaching through, they can locate the ventilation duct by feel. The energy given off by the chemical reaction has damaged the duct enough to melt its plastic walls. The team can crawl from the tunnel directly through the destabilized wall and into the ventilation duct.

Once through, the wall seals up again behind them, retaining the bubble-gum consistency for one hour. In this state it has a Barrier Rating of 16 against high-impact, high-energy damage such as firearms, projectile weapons, and explosives. Against low-energy attacks — simply trying to walk through it, or magic — it has a Barrier Rating of 1.

After passing through, the team exits covered in sticky, membranous sections of wall. They can spend about five minutes removing it. If they don't, the substance hardens again in one hour, reducing Strength and Quickness by 4 each. Reaction Rating and Dice Pools must be recalculated accordingly.

Once through the wall, the team should quickly begin to realize that something is very wrong in the lab.

**Debugging**
There is little that can go wrong here unless random chance runs against the team, making sewer encounters come so fast and furious that it jeopardizes their ability to complete the run. The GM should be wary of overdoing the encounters. The real action takes place immediately after the team enters the Aztechnology lab — they'll have been expecting strange things in the sewers, but certainly not in the lab.

Movement in the sewers should be restricted to the local area, i.e., this side of the Aztechnology facility. If the team somehow ends up exploring or gets lost, take pains not to let them wander into the area occupied by the hidden Ork town.

The GM must also remember that this is an *abandoned* sewer, not the monorail station during rush hour.

For every turn (roughly three seconds) the team is in Beta Section, there is a chance the Mage will spot them. Roll 2D6 every turn; on a result of 4 or less, they have been spotted. At that point Blount returns to his body and reports intruders in Beta Section.

**Response**
It takes some time for Blount to convince his superiors that intruders have, in fact, penetrated the isolated, sealed, impregnable Beta Section. When he finally does succeed, his bosses spring into action.

They send Blount back to the team's location, where he tells the Security Elementals not to interfere during the initial conflict. He cannot do much more in the way of commanding them, for they are not his. Aztechnology policy is to pull the Security Elementals back into a second line of defense behind the security Mage.

He can, however, affect any Elementals or Spirits the Mage may have. Any magical items the Mage is carrying have a presence in Astral Space, so Blount can affect them too. He will first try to eliminate any Elementals or Spirits present, assuming the Security Elementals haven't taken care of them. He will next try to destroy any high-powered magical items the Magician may be carrying, such as a Power Focus. (A better tactic would be to destroy as many low-powered items as possible, thereby inflicting as much damage as possible.)

Unless a Spirit or Elemental has been instructed to report, a controlling Magician will not know one has been destroyed until after the fact. He *will* immediately know when any of his magic items are under attack. His alternatives are to stay out of Astral and take the attack, or to go astral and defend his items. At the point the Magician goes astral, Blount engages him directly.

The Security Elementals remain separate from the fight unless Blount orders them to attack, which he will not have the head to do. Even after Blount has been removed from the fight, the Elementals remain passive, for they have not been instructed otherwise.

At this point, the team should realize they have been discovered and time is of the essence. At that very moment, Aztechnology is dispatching a Response Team to the area. The team arrives at the facility, evaluates the situation, and concludes the intruders must have entered Beta Section from the sewers. ### Into The Flames — Prologue (Paraphrased)
*A retelling for GM use. Read aloud, summarize, or hand over as background.*

An old Ork — old by the standards of his listeners, which means barely past thirty-five — settles his bulk into a chair in front of a room full of silent young people. To be an Ork, he tells them, is to know your own particular history. And knowing it means remembering the ones who came before and suffered. Their story, he says, belongs to the listeners as much as to him. He can still see it happening as he speaks. For him it will always be yesterday.

**February 7, 2039. Tacoma.**
A cold, windy day, with a hard wind off the north. In the failing light, a young Allan Bronston made his way home down an empty street littered with blowing refuse. Trouble was already in the air — the big Humanis Policlub rally hadn't even started yet. Common sense said this was not the night to test his legal right to walk a public street. He took the back alleys instead.

He was nearly home when the amplified voice from the rally carried to him. He stopped to listen, then heard something about the "guardians of human purity" and decided he'd heard enough. The memories came anyway: Goblinization, the first transformations, the early quarantine camps. His fists clenched and his vision went white before he forced it down. A siren brought him back. Didn't people love to say an Ork out of control was a dangerous thing? He climbed a fence and kept walking.

The next few hours were quiet. The rest of the family had made it home safely. Allan lay in bed listening to the wind — a sound he'd always associated with warmth and safety — and fell asleep easily.

He never knew how long he'd been out when he heard glass breaking somewhere distant. He thought he was dreaming. Then came a low rumble, like surf or far-off thunder. Or marching feet.

The pounding on the front door woke him fully. Three or four fists, furious. Light under his door, then his father's footsteps on the stairs, the maglock releasing, strange voices. Then his mother going down. Then the voices coming up. His door opened and a light blinded him. A man's rough voice said something about city health regulations and inspection codes before moving on to another part of the house.

"Father?"
"Stay there and be quiet."
"What's going on?"

His father didn't answer, and followed the stranger down the hall.

Allan got up and nearly collided with a second man in a heavy white chemsuit, punching data into a portable identification scanner, who shoved past him as if he weren't there. Something began burning in the young Ork's chest and got hotter with every breath the man in white took. Why were they doing this? What right did they have? After all the harassment from outside, this was still their home — the place where it was supposed to stop.

His jaw shook. He stepped in behind the man in the chemsuit.

"Allan!"

His mother's panicked voice spun him around. Behind her stood two men with assault rifles up. For a fraction of a second he considered defying them — then saw his mother standing between him and the muzzles, and backed off with an audible hiss.

Outside were hundreds of Orks, Trolls, Dwarfs and Elves crowded into the cold, their homes declared health hazards. Human family members were being turned out too. Many stayed defiantly beside their Awakened relatives; others loudly denied any connection to them. Humanis supporters milled around the edges, jeering. More police kept arriving, and the mood kept souring.

As the Bronstons filed out onto the street, someone in the crowd threw an empty bottle. The shattering glass triggered a rain of debris onto the families. Allan heard a scream and saw a young girl crumpled on the pavement, blood running from a gash on her face. He gathered his two sisters in his arms. His younger brother was in their mother's arms; their father watched it all with open fear.

Then came heavy wheels from up the street. Three riot vehicles took up positions abreast, water cannon and machine guns manned. Allan noticed the guns were pointed only at the huddled Orks and their families. Ten riot police spilled from each vehicle and formed a cordon across the street.

A flat voice from inside the command vehicle announced that by authority of the Seattle Metroplex Guard, all citizens without properly authorized shelter were to report immediately to the nearest deplacement center. Violators would be detained. After a pause it repeated the order, adding that they had fifteen minutes to gather belongings, after which everything would be decontaminated and shipped out.

Allan looked to his parents. Like everyone else, they had no idea what to say or do. The voice came again: transports would take them to the docks, where the liner *Asian Princess* was waiting to sail for a deplacement center near San Francisco. Part of a nationwide resettlement program. Their cooperation was encouraged. Repeat — their cooperation was encouraged. That last phrase put ice down every Ork spine.

"What are we going to do?" Allan asked. "You know who they really want."
"Go help your mother pack."
"They can't do this!"
"They already have."

The Bronstons joined thousands of others in the forced exodus. Helicopters circled overhead; armed Metroplex guards lined the convoy routes. The docks were only minutes away, and so crowded that vehicles stacked up on one another. Orders were shouted from every direction and for a while nobody seemed to know anything. Any chance of slipping away died under the floodlights and the surveillance drone screens. Watching his family, Allan thought it wasn't fair they should suffer because of his condition — especially his younger brother and sister, who might never be afflicted at all. Then again, was it fair that he should suffer either?

An amplified voice rolled down the docks: all personnel to proceed to designated warehouses for processing. Boarding would begin shortly. By order of Governor Allenson, any Metahuman without proper identification would be detained without warrant.

Two Metroplex Guards passed in front of the Bronstons' transport, and Allan's gut turned over as he watched each man cross his arms over his chest — the Hands of Five salute. That faction inside Alamos 20,000 was believed responsible for years of terrorist attacks on Metahumans. Their presence meant this was more than a security operation. He thought about telling his father, then held his tongue. He wanted to act. He had no idea what action was even possible.

Half an hour later the family was ordered out and directed into the long line moving toward the warehouses.

"All contaminated Humans process to the left. All Orks process to the right. Families will be reunited before boarding."

Children were pulled from parents, siblings from each other. Without a word, Allan took the hand of his one afflicted sister, looked at his parents one last time, and joined his line. Passing through the warehouse door he caught a guard's eye. "Damned freaks," the guard muttered, gripping a shotgun and waiting for an excuse.

Inside, high ceilings and hardwood floors turned every sound into a din. Rows of Orks stood shoulder to shoulder under bright lights, waiting. The air smelled of dry lumber and heavy oil as more subjects were packed in. Be ready to present all forms of identification on request, they were told; the sooner processing finished, the sooner boarding could begin.

What happened next happened too fast to assign blame. A scuffle broke out among frightened Orks near the front, and the violence rippled backward. A short burst of noise — probably gunfire — and a chorus of fear went up through the room. Shouted orders vanished under screaming. A surge of bodies shoved everyone back against the wall. Another burst, then the blinding flash of a concussion grenade. The blast shook everyone and knocked out a row of ceiling lights. Smoke curled around the remaining ones, and the smell of burning wood rose. The tinderbox had caught.

In the crush, Allan lost his grip on his sister. He could see her but couldn't free his arm. He shouted; the roar swallowed it. Wall to wall, everyone was locked in place.

Within seconds the lights dimmed under black smoke. The coughing started; the heat became real. Allan watched his sister pushed further away, and forced his body toward her, fingers almost touching hers — and then one of the side doors gave way. The few who escaped created a vacuum, and his sister was swept off in the rush like a swimmer in a flood. He could only watch her face disappear into the crowd.

Flames appeared above the heads of the crowd, and the first howls of pain came with them. *This is it*, he thought. *It's over. Damn your human race.*

Unable to move or fight, smoke burning his lungs, he shut his eyes and waited.

Seconds later he felt cold air. The double doors he'd come through had been torn off their hinges by people clawing for life. Bodies were being fired out through the gap like rounds from a gun. There was still hope.

The fresh air fed the fire, and it climbed. Thousands of Orks were still trapped. Some escaped; not enough. Allan felt the heat on his back, getting closer to the opening, the flames getting closer too. The roof was burning and the middle had begun to sag. Embers rained down while Orks everywhere tore off burning clothes. The poisoned smoke started killing by the hundreds.

He was within meters of the door but the pressure from every side pinned him. So many wanted to live. So few would.

Between the smoke and the crush he couldn't breathe. One last push was all that was left, and there was no room to make it. Under his feet he felt what he thought was a metal grate. That was when everything erupted.

The next thing he knew he was on the ground breathing cool, damp air, feeling something like reborn. He got to his knees and looked up. Above him was a blackened hole crossed by smoldering beams, and past that, the stars. Around him were tunnel walls — a drainage tunnel running down from the warehouse into unknown depths. The darkness below was the answer.

Then something caught him from the side: the butt of an assault rifle. "Hold it right there, Ork, or I'll put you away for good."

Allan turned with animal fury, tore the weapon out of the guard's hands, shoved him against the wall and put the sights on him. His rage slid into a smirking, victorious grin. His finger found the trigger. "Your cooperation," he gasped, squeezing, "in this… matter is… greatly… encouraged."

"Did you kill him?" one of the young Orks asks.
"I did what I had to."
"Did you see your family again?"
"No. Never." A silence settles over the room. "Every Ork in the land lost someone close that night."
"Is that why they call it 'The Night of Rage'?"
"In part, yes."
"How many escaped in the tunnels?"
"Enough for us to start over."
"Is it because we're afraid of the Humans that we live underground?"

He thinks about it a moment, pushing himself up out of the chair. "Not afraid," the old Ork says proudly. "Different, but never, never afraid."

All along the Tacoma waterfront that night — and across the whole continent — the flames of hatred against Metahumans were fanned. Whether that first warehouse fire was deliberately set is still disputed, but within an hour Tacoma's entire downtown district was gutted. Only direct intervention by the United Corporation Council got Lone Star Security to step in and stop the spread. By then the damage was done.

The Orks who survived the Tacoma fires took refuge in the network of sewers and drains beneath the city. And so the underground town of Wilhem Park was born.

### Behind the Scenes — Wilhem Park
Jenny Hernandez has led the team to Wilhem Park, a former enclosed shopping mall buried following the Tacoma volcano eruptions of 2017. Written off as a total loss, it was left entombed under ash and rubble. A modern block of condoplexes is built directly above it. Discovered by the Orks who escaped the burning Tacoma warehouses, it has since become their home and refuge. They maintain little contact with the outside world except to stay informed. Their main fear is that someday their sanctuary will be discovered and they will be forced to leave their now-generational home.

Though a major portion of the upper level is devoted to hydroponic agriculture, Wilhem Park is not self-supporting. Small meat animals are bred in one of the former department stores and fed a nutri-grain substitute grown in hydroponics. Illumination comes from dozens of small gas-burning lights illicitly tapped into a nearby gas main.

In all, close to 400 men, women, and children live in Wilhem Park. The majority are Ork, but some are the Human offspring of the older Orks. Care is taken to educate the children and assure their literacy. Any who reach adulthood are free to leave, but only on the condition that they never reveal the existence or location of the enclave.

The group lived in relative peace until the past year, when their calm was shattered by activities at the Aztechnology Tacoma Research Park. Desperate to test Peterhoff's gene-splicing experiments, the Aztechnology bosses sent out their minions to scour the sewers for test subjects. A number of Orks vanished this way, the most recent group just before the viral accident.

Allan Bronston and his people have been aware of the team's activities ever since they first entered the Tacoma tunnels. He knows they penetrated the Aztechnology facility and got back out again. Though he is not aware of the details of the viral accident, he does know something serious has occurred.

The tone of this encounter depends on whether the team killed Ork children in the warehouse. If they did not, Bronston will be friendly and offer assistance. If they did kill any Ork young, he will demand they stage a raid on Aztechnology's Alpha Section to rescue the kidnapped Orks. He will mention that Dr. Carol Owens, aka "The Tailor," is also being held in Alpha Section. He tells the group she is the designer of the Metagene virus, but apparently wants to leave Aztechnology.

If asked, Bronston reveals that he knows of her presence from an Ork who was captured by Aztechnology but managed to escape. It was this individual who told him about the goings-on in the lab. Bronston and Owens knew each other in another era, and he cannot believe she would be responsible for something as hideous as the Metavirus. He wants to confront her personally to learn the truth, though he says nothing of this to the team. The only thing preventing him from staging his own raid has been means of entry and a fear of retribution from Aztechnology, who suspect the existence of an underground Ork colony. To date, Aztechnology has not investigated further, except for the occasional "specimen sweeps." Bronston and the others fear Aztechnology would root them out with force if they knew the enclave's exact location.

The arrival of the team presents Bronston with an unhoped-for boon. Whether he is friendly to them or not, Bronston will try to use them to do the enclave's dirty work. In exchange for their services, he will offer the team escape from the sewers and the potential for a much bigger payment from Biogene (to whom he claims he can relay a message if the team wishes). He will not mention his personal interest in Carol Owens.

**On the Orks**
The most important thing to note about the Ork enclave is that it is civilized. These are not the grimy, illiterate, brainless beings most non-Orks hear and read about. These Orks live among their families and have the same concerns as any other "unawakened." They read the same data-faxes and watch the same Urban Brawl games. Young Orks even listen to the same brain-twisting music as other adolescents.

Indeed, there is no "Ork culture" and no "Ork language." How could there be when the oldest Orks are barely 30 years old? Though many Orks claim a distinctly Ork culture or language, much diversity still exists in both areas. These various elements may one day mesh to create a culture that gives birth to a language, but not within this century.

Orks receive much of their bad press from the fact that their adolescent males tend to be more "active." Physically, Orks reach maturity at about age 12, while a Human does so at 18 or so. Though an Ork reaches an advanced state of physical development earlier, he still has the emotional maturity of a 12-year-old. (Put a 12-year-old in the body of a body-builder to imagine what happens.) A venerable, old Ork is one who has reached the ripe age of 35, and he will begin to show signs of aging at about 20 years.

If the team reveals any kind of anti-Ork prejudice, and especially if they have shot any Ork children, Bronston will go out of his way to enlighten them on what it means to be an Ork. He will give them a full tour of Wilhem Park and show them how the Orks have been able to survive, and then some. Orks are not monsters. Orks are *people*.

**Debugging**

## Data File Extract 1
**To:** Dr. William Espinata, Supervisor of Genetic Systems, Aztechnology Corporation
**From:** Dr. Carol Owens, Metagene Project Coordinator, Tacoma Facility (AZ-TECH)
**Re:** Message ATS/921621

[Message Begin]

Dr. Espinata:

Congratulations on your new position. Having been following your researches, I was quite pleased to learn of your recent transfer from Europe. I am certainly looking forward to working with you.

Regarding your request for info on this project, I refer you to master internal file MTG/OV-1/318 for a detailed analysis.

In overview, the Metagene Project is concerned with the isolation and documentation of the so-called Metagene responsible for the recent emergence of variants of Homo sapiens.

We have located and mapped said gene's position in the DNA spiral, but all replication and splicing/bonding attempts to date have failed.

We have, however, been able to alter the influence of the Metagene on organic development, but in an uneven and unpredictable manner. That aspect of the project is being supervised by Dr. Simon Peterhoff, and all questions should be referred to him.

It is my hope and expectation that the Metagene project will soon begin to show results. We project that isolating, mapping, and documenting of the actual gene will allow us to engineer it, as we have done with other genes in the past.

I see the controlled development of the Metagene as a wondrous tool in the hands of an evolving humanity. Properly manipulated, the Metagene can play an important role in the future of a progressive, adaptive mankind. Within reach is the salvation of the next generation of "lost children" — Orks, goblins, and such, who will no longer have to face the torments and prejudices that scarred their ancestors.

Consult file path MTG*AT841-GH for a continued update on our progress.

Sincerely,

Dr. Carol Owens.

[Message End]

*/Copy To File (C. Owens)

--(Begin Attached)--

This is a listing of our most progressive successes to date.

**Wolf Sapiens (MTG:WS104)**
The timber wolf/human cross-breed was one of the more difficult applications, but proper stimulation of the Metagene eventually assisted in the bonding.

Product WS104 has proved to be the most stable. A few prior products are still alive, but it appears that most progress will come from WS104. It is scheduled for termination and analysis in 16 days.

Product shows an increase in body mass and proportional muscular increase. Reflex-response activity is below expectations, but tweaking should improve them.

Sensory acuity is at .38 on the Leverman scale, a full 1.3 points above human average.

Intelligence, fortunately, owes more to its human parentage than anything else. Prognosis is good for advancement in this area. Rudimentary speech capability, with about a 100-word vocabulary.

Product tends toward bipedal locomotion with occasional relapses to quadrupedal position. Product has improperly developed hands, with minimal dexterity. Again, advancement is expected in future products.

**Tiger Sapiens (MTG:TS64)**
Product TS64 shows fewer signs of the neurological deterioration prevalent in the prior TS series products.

Less tendency toward quadripedal motion than WS series, except when in preparatory attack-mode.

Hand development is minimal, but that appears to be the trade-off for full claw persistence.

Estimated Stabler/IQ rating is roughly 40 percent of human average. Vocabulary approaches 60 words comprehension and 12 words spoken. Progression is fully anticipated.

Sensory acuity rated at .42 on the Leverman scale.

Product TS64 is scheduled for termination and analysis in 20 days.

**Bear Sapiens (MTG:BS261)**
The BS series has proven to be a nearly unmanageable headache. The genetic coding compatability problem seems nearly insurmountable.

Product BS261 shows virtually no intelligence, has no vocabulary, and will accept no command.

Preference seems to be bipedal motion, but said motion is slow and lumbering. Significant body mass increase, but expected muscular increase is only 72 percent.

Others to follow.
