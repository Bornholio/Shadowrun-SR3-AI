# LATE NIGHT — SUNDAY, MAY 28

## Infinite Warehouse / Tesseract Discussion

Singer and Glaze discussed their respective forms of impossible storage.

Singer demonstrated his **Infinite Warehouse** ability. He opened a narrow line in space, approximately three feet, placed a pillow through it, retrieved the pillow, and then opened a larger threshold into a gray, featureless “non-space.” He stood partly inside the opening while discussing it.

Singer explained several practical observations about the ability:

- The storage is transient rather than a fixed mundane room.
- He had previously cleared it because carrying stored material seemed to affect or distort his astral form in ways he did not want.
- He believes the destination is metaplanar or otherwise outside ordinary physical space.
- He learned or developed the storage ability after another ability, **Astral Shift**, associated with an old ork swordsman friend who left Singer certain abilities.
- Organic material does not rot while stored. Singer has stored food and eaten it approximately a week later without noticing spoilage or a change in taste.
- Singer has never deliberately placed a living being inside the Warehouse.
- He worries that abandoning someone inside might kill them in a way analogous to some astral or metaplanar hazards, though he does not believe the storage automatically sterilizes living material.

Glaze’s storage was contrasted with Singer’s. Her small copper-scale pouch contains a stable tesseract capable of storing a substantial amount of material. Singer observed that Glaze’s foci remain magically connected and bonded while inside her pouch. By contrast, Singer suspects his Infinite Warehouse may separate a focus from its owner, interfere with the bond, or possibly de-bond it. This remained untested. Glaze was unwilling to risk one of her bonded foci merely to answer the question.

The distinction became important: Glaze’s pouch is an ancient stable magical storage object but is **not** itself automatically categorized as a modern focus, while Singer’s Warehouse is an ability whose metaphysical behavior may be different.

## Astral Shift Discussion

Singer described his **Astral Shift** ability from personal experience.

He said he can remain in the shifted astral mode for approximately fifteen minutes before pain becomes severe enough to force the issue. He described his practical travel speed as roughly **1,300 km/h**. He can bring other people through with him, but they share the injury risk of the transition.

Singer said ordinary people he has rescued or transported this way have often suffered minor to moderate vascular injury. Singer himself tolerates it much better because of his unusual resilience and body.

Singer speculated that Astral Shift may explain how he escaped death and appeared in Glaze’s sealed mountain lair. He suggested he might have been drawn toward the pooled mana or magical concentration around her location. Glaze did not accept that as established fact. Attraction, resonance, accident, or another unknown mechanism all remained possible explanations.

The discussion also established that ordinary astral travel is much faster than Singer’s stated Astral Shift speed. Glaze did not regard her foci as what makes ordinary astral movement faster; ordinary astral movement depends on the magician’s own magical nature/ability. Singer’s gray threshold and Warehouse do not behave like ordinary astral projection.

Singer said he had spent or sacrificed some of his magical potential on augmentations and had spent more on a precise/fast-motion ability that improved smaller-scale coordination, motion, and multitasking. Singer suggested that without those investments he might have been closer to Glaze’s magical scale if her focus were excluded from the comparison.

Singer also clarified that he can shift other people with him. Glaze’s practical reaction was that such a power makes sense for escaping death or bypassing overwhelming opposition—such as avoiding one hundred guards—not for saving a few minutes of ordinary travel time.

## Ancient / Fourth World Magical Objects

Singer and Glaze discussed very old magical objects and the limits of modern categories.

Singer remembered Fourth World objects that required knowledge of their stories or legends before a person could properly bond with them. He described one ancient object he had previously possessed or encountered: an **orichalcum key** that seemed timeless, extremely powerful, and did not behave like an ordinary bonded focus. He recalled it as crossing multiple focus-like categories and as making some magical operations, particularly banishing spirits, extremely easy. He speculated that it might have been pre-Fourth Age or otherwise older than the magical systems he knew.

Glaze’s position was that ancient magical objects need not fit modern Sixth World categories such as standard foci, and that a modern classification failing to describe an object does not make the object invalid or incomprehensible.
