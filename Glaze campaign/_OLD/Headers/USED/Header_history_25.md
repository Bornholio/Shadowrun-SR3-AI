## Restaurant and laundry equipment deal — Luis and Nessa

Singer met **Luis**, a dwarf restaurant-equipment salvager, and **Nessa**, an ork commercial-appliance seller and repairer.

They established an initial arrangement for:

- one commercial high-temperature restaurant sanitizer/dishwasher;
- one commercial washer;
- one commercial dryer.

Singer would repair isolated failed parts or assemblies, while Luis and Nessa would reinstall and test them. Singer's repair labor would be credited against the equipment rather than ordinary sticker price, while vendor diagnosis or reassembly errors would remain the vendors' responsibility rather than becoming additional repair work charged to Singer.

The first machines were tagged for **Cole pickup the following morning**.

Singer also stated that he wanted **three additional matched sets later**, for an intended eventual total of four sanitizer/washer/dryer sets. Used, ugly, or repairable equipment was acceptable if it could be made functional.

Luis, Nessa, and nearby vendors learned that badly damaged internals could have trade value to Singer rather than making an entire machine worthless.

Singer openly used **Fix** on worn motors and subsystems and fully restored several pieces in front of witnesses. He explained that magical repair became increasingly difficult as objects became more heavily processed, that microprocessors were especially difficult, and that the effort and recovery cost made mass magical repair uneconomic. He described his intended model as technicians plus ordinary repair and salvage infrastructure rather than Singer personally performing endless magical repair work.

Approximate equipment weights discussed for the first equipment class were:

- old Hobart pass-through sanitizer: about **120 kg**;
- commercial washer: about **130 kg** each;
- commercial dryer: about **95 kg**.
