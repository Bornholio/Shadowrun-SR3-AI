# Session 25B

**Thursday, June 8, 2051 — 22:23 PDT, Redmond**
Singer and Glaze are leaving the salvage rows after the Ramos/Celia business. Nothing presses on them—no attack, no pursuit, no surveillance contact, no emergency—and the night ahead is theirs to decide.

**Thursday, June 8, 2051 — 22:24 PDT, Redmond**
Singer pulls up his appointment listings for the next few days and goes over them with Glaze. After the last week, the list is unusually short.
Friday has the 10:30 delivery of six beef sides to Taetzel, followed by the full-moon barbecue Friday evening. The barbecue has no hard start time entered. Saturday has the one firm personal appointment: Dr. Miriam Okafor is expected to call at 16:00 regarding the reproductive/genetics work. Nothing is actually scheduled yet with Celia. Derek Vale's promised technician names, the elderly talismonger's possible introductions, and the Holly Brighton–Daviar route are all pending contacts rather than appointments.
Glaze studies the listing with him, tired but attentive.
"Tomorrow morning is meat arriving at our house," she says. "That is not an appointment. That is logistics." A small pause. "The barbecue is different. I intend to be awake for that."
She looks again at Saturday. "And Miriam, yes. I want to be there for that call. Not because you need me there." Her mouth quirks. "Because it concerns my reproductive biology, which gives me a modest professional interest."
She glances back toward the Friday listing. "So. Beef at half ten. Party when we decide it begins. Miriam at sixteen hundred Saturday." She looks at Singer. "That leaves considerably more empty space than I expected after looking at our lives." Then, more practically: "I would not schedule Celia tomorrow morning. We have been awake through enough portions of the clock lately that I no longer trust your definition of 'available.'"

**Thursday, June 8, 2051 — approximately 23:00 PDT, Taetzel**
Glaze catches Singer's quizzical look at *half ten* almost immediately. "What?"
"I know we talked about number systems…" he begins, and she gives him a look that says she understands perfectly well she has stepped on some obscure modern linguistic rake.
Singer works through the modern conventions while they walk home: ten-thirty rather than half ten in the local American usage; twelve-hour clocks and AM/PM; the cleaner twenty-four-hour system, where 10:30 becomes 1030 and 22:30 needs no qualifier. From there he ranges into the older numerical inheritances still embedded in ordinary life—the mixtures of fives, twelves, sixties, and tens that survived because people kept using systems long after forgetting why they were convenient.
Glaze follows the structure easily. She occasionally interrupts over vocabulary, but not the mathematics.
"So your civilization," she eventually says, "has standardized measurement by agreeing to preserve several incompatible ancient systems simultaneously." A few steps later: "And then soldiers solved part of this by refusing to say 'evening.'"
Singer gets into military time and time zones after that: local civil time, standardized offsets, Greenwich as the old reference meridian, and coordinated reference times when several places have to act on one clock.
Glaze tries it back once to make sure she has it. "Sixteen hundred Saturday, Miriam. Ten-thirty Friday, meat. Twenty-two…" She checks the time. "…whatever it is now, you are giving me a lecture about Babylon while walking home."
She seems quite content with this. By the time they reach Taetzel, the lesson has wandered through enough examples that she has stopped translating the clock convention consciously.
Inside, Singer locks the building down. The ordinary external access is closed. Cameras, locks, sensors, building communications and the Red-7 environment remain functioning, but access is restricted rather than left in the more permissive daytime/work configuration. Glaze retains household access. Cole's credentials remain sufficient for the areas and systems he needs for building work, and Riku retains the access required for the first-floor shop and his legitimate routes through the building. Neither receives broader access merely because their credentials remain live. The elevator-core weapons remain under their established deliberate activation restriction; the lockdown does not turn Taetzel into an indiscriminate automated kill zone.
The system accepts the changes cleanly. Nothing reports a forced entry, unexpected credential use, camera fault, or exterior alarm.
Glaze watches the last status indicators settle. "Good. Now, unless Greenwich has another surprise for me, I am going upstairs."

**Thursday, June 8, 2051 — approximately 23:02 PDT, Taetzel**
"Only the fact that the time systems are coordinated to correct for relativity," Singer says, "both general and special."
Glaze turns her head toward him. There is a short silence. "Of course they are."
Singer leaves it there. They take the high-speed executive elevator instead of the ordinary lift. The doors close, the car accelerates hard but smoothly, and the lower floors fall away in seconds. Glaze leans back against the wall during the ascent.
"So," she says, "tomorrow you may explain why knowing what time it is requires understanding gravity."
The elevator decelerates into the 28th floor and opens onto home. Glaze steps out first, then looks back at him. "But not tonight."

**Thursday, June 8, 2051 — approximately 23:05 PDT, Taetzel**
"It's even worse than that," Singer laughs. "Understanding gravity to correct clocks to know where you are."
Glaze gives him a flat look. "So gravity tells the clocks where we are." A beat. "I withdraw my complaint about Babylon."
Singer tells the automation to start the shower on hot at midnight, then strips off his armor and weapons, stows them, and starts exercising. Glaze watches him for a moment, already looking considerably more interested in bed than in joining him.
"Did you want me to wait?" he asks. "I can just go to bed right now, but I'll probably bother you." He makes grabbing hands toward her.
She looks at the hands, then at his face. "No. Exercise."
She steps deliberately within reach anyway and catches one of his hands before it closes on empty air, pulling it against her waist. "You do not have to synchronize your sleep with mine merely because we share a bed." Then, with a tired grin: "And if you bother me later, I will decide whether that is a problem when you do it."
She gives his hand a squeeze, releases it, and starts toward the bedroom. "Midnight shower. Then sleep. That is my recommendation to the man who thinks three hours constitutes a useful quantity."
Singer blows her a kiss.

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:25 PDT, Taetzel**
Singer's first stretch of exercise is a ridiculous sequence of high-intensity work, absurd even by the standards of the equipment. The treadmill simply refuses his requested speed.
"The treadmill won't go to sixty-five kilometers per hour!"
From the bedroom, Glaze's voice carries back: "That sounds like a problem with the treadmill."
He drives the routine at full power without pushing into failure. When the first real fatigue arrives, he backs off into what qualifies for him as a mellow, sustained routine—one that would kill most people. By midnight he is thoroughly exercised rather than exhausted, and the shower is already hot when he reaches it.
Afterward, in bed with Glaze, Singer logs into the local systems and checks Taetzel rather than immediately sleeping. The review of logs and forensics is clean: no unexplained physical access, no suspicious credential use, no camera discontinuities suggesting tampering, and nothing in the local host or available security records that resolves into an intrusion or reconnaissance event. The lockdown is behaving as configured. Ordinary system activity and background traffic remain ordinary; nothing justifies waking anyone or changing posture. Glaze is asleep before he finishes.
Four hours later Singer wakes without moving, still wrapped around her. Glaze has shifted during the night until she is fitted comfortably against him, one leg tangled through his and one hand tucked near his chest. Her breathing remains slow and regular; she has not awakened with him.
Outside, the early morning is cool and dry, a 50°F low ahead of a 75°F high and no precipitation—the coming day substantially warmer than the night. Taetzel remains quiet. And there are still a little over six hours before six sides of beef are supposed to arrive.

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:28 PDT, the Matrix**
Without stirring from Glaze, Singer goes in. He glides through a three-stage satellite hop, setting up data diffusers and agents in each relay.
Aztechnology's computer systems are a mountain. Seattle links its hosts to the main Aztechnology system only at deeper points, weighted against their risk of leaks. Singer focuses on pure stealth, seeking whatever they hold on Tacoma.
On level thirty, his tally reaches 12, and the floor's untested Black IC activates. By the time he has erased every trace of the information in the backups, five powerful Black IC constructs have fallen, along with three deckers—none of whom ever gets a proper Analyze on him. The first Trace IC to complete a successful location cycle is relocated. Personal reports from individuals are left edited in ways that make cooperation difficult and stymie the intelligence expert systems. His last planted report states that explosive gases are still trapped in the labs and that the building's subfloors are all offline pending an expected nine-month repair process.
All the data is tabulated: 180 Mp of proper paydata, and more that has value but is not saleable, because it presents information he does not want released.
His retreat is much more hectic. At every floor his tally brings multiple IC seeking him; on the lower floors, hosts go straight into shutdown as his persona crosses down. Leaving a wake of crashing hosts behind him, he makes his way back out. Graceful logoff—with eight Trace IC still trying to get a Locate cycle to find purchase.
His validated account and data logging on the 15th floor will report the damage five hours later: a large gang of deckers, likely of Yamatetsu origin, hammering the system, resulting in 3.7 million ¥ of downtime losses and backup restoration. Singer does not collect that information now. He leaves it on Shadowland for open use, along with a SAN map to the 30th floor—which will earn him unlimited access a month later, when sysops confirm it is true.
Then, to Walker: "Wake up!"
A while later the message follows: "Walker, the datastore you want is coming your way—here is the package. Also, confirm backup destruction of the datastore. You need to decide its value. Hold payment and info release. (Jordan) Pay us through Singer. Burn him and we will have dice to roll." The Jordan voice does not wait for an answer, even if one comes. "Looks like we are clean, but we're still scattered, so we are not available."
The data is 9, 12, and 10 points' worth—155k on the open market, and easily three times that to Biogene.

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:30 PDT, Taetzel**
For most of the 126 seconds, Glaze does not move. She wakes somewhere in the middle of it.
Singer is physically still beside her, but not normally still. His eyes are moving beneath half-lowered lids in tiny, rapid corrections; individual muscles in his face tighten and release faster than ordinary conversation would ever demand. There is nothing dreamy about it. Whatever he is doing, he is working hard, and he is a little warm when it stops.
The cable comes free. External access drops behind him as the local host closes itself off, leaving the house isolated again.
Singer turns back to her. "Good morning, sorry if I woke you."
The passionate kiss eliminates any remaining ambiguity about whether Glaze is awake. For a second she simply accepts it, one hand coming up against the side of his face. Then she catches him by the jaw before he can retreat very far and looks directly into his eyes.
"You were doing something ridiculous." Not accusing. Certain. Her thumb moves once against his cheek. "I watched your face."
She kisses him again, shorter this time, then settles back enough to study him. "You were asleep beside me. Then you became extremely busy without moving. Then you became warm. Now our house has cut itself off from the outside world." A pause. "So either you have discovered a remarkably complicated form of masturbation, or you were in the Matrix." The corner of her mouth lifts. "And since you apologized for waking me instead of looking embarrassed, I am choosing Matrix." She remains tucked against him, awake now. "What happened?"

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:47 PDT, Taetzel**
"Mastication," Singer says. "Mental mastication."
That gets a sleepy laugh out of her.
"We are in the clear for the Tacoma mess. Erased the backups and got the details."
For the next fifteen minutes she listens while he reconstructs the run in ordinary time: the satellite hops, diffusers and agents, the descent through Aztechnology's layered systems, the tally climbing, the Black IC and deckers coming in without ever getting a clean read on him, the relocated trace, the selective editing, the destroyed backups, and the final retreat through hosts already beginning to collapse behind him. He spends a while longer detailing the information gleaned and, hopefully, sold.
When he reaches the false internal picture—explosive gases still trapped below, subfloors offline, nine months of repair—Glaze's expression sharpens. "So they will spend effort maintaining a lie they believe is their own conclusion." She understands immediately why the personal reports were altered rather than simply deleted. "And the people who remember pieces of the truth will disagree with the systems that are supposed to reconcile them."
By the time Singer gets to the Shadowland release, the SAN map, and the damage report blaming likely Yamatetsu deckers, she is fully awake. "Three point seven million because you were inside for two minutes." A pause. "I am beginning to understand why corporations are afraid of people who know where the doors are."
He details the package sent to Walker, the withheld release, and the valuation problem. "Walker can figure what he owes… Jordan."
Glaze nods once. "That part is his problem." She shifts closer, resting her head against him. "You gave him something that protects his side, made him responsible for deciding what it is worth, and did not make payment the condition for keeping dangerous information contained." After a moment: "And if he burns Singer, Jordan becomes expensive." Her eyes narrow slightly with amusement. "I approve of Jordan."
She touches his face where the tiny muscle movements had been most obvious. "But next time you are lying perfectly still beside me while five Black IC and three deckers are trying to kill you, I would appreciate knowing whether I should continue sleeping." A beat. "Not because I need to supervise you." Another. "Because apparently 'mental mastication' now includes corporate infrastructure damage."

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:48 PDT, Taetzel**
"They were very chewy," Singer says.
Glaze closes her eyes. "Mm." A few seconds pass. "Then you should have used more sauce." She shifts tighter against him, one hand settling over his ribs. "And less Aztechnology."

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:49 PDT, Taetzel**
"Imagine if I did that in the middle of the workday…" Singer muses. "Also, how are you this beautiful once again? It's not even five a.m. yet. I thought dragons were extra sleepy?"
He pivots her on top of him and closes his eyes, feeling around like a blind man. Glaze braces one hand beside his head while he gropes theatrically.
"Extra sleepy does not mean ugly before sunrise." She catches one of his wandering hands and redirects it to her waist. "And you woke me with Aztechnology, kissed me, explained a Matrix war, and then started insulting dragons as a species."
"You smell good enough to eat."
She leans down until her hair brushes his face. "That is dangerous language from a man currently pretending he cannot see." A brief kiss lands against the corner of his mouth. "And yes. Middle of the workday would have been funnier." She settles comfortably on him. "Much more expensive, too."

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:51 PDT, Taetzel**
Singer slips out from under her and returns a few moments later with a knit cap fitted with 'trodes. "Try this out?"
Glaze props herself up on one elbow, eyeing it, then him. "You escaped from bed to fetch me a hat."
She takes it and turns it over in her hands, immediately recognizing what the embedded contacts are for. Her expression changes from sleepy amusement to interest. "This is yours?"
She pulls the cap on, carefully seating it over her hair and adjusting it until the contacts sit properly, then looks at him expectantly. "All right." A beat. "If this puts advertising directly into my head, I am setting it on fire."

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:52 PDT, Taetzel**
"Two things," Singer says. "First, I will show you what I see, in the moment—just sitting here in the dim light. Then I'll show you the Matrix run. It will feel like two to seven hours. Log out whenever you want, but it's really only about two minutes."
Glaze goes very still. Her eyes narrow slightly—not from fear so much as recalculation. "And I can leave whenever I choose."
She checks the fit of the cap again, then looks directly at him. "Yes." A small pause. "Show me the room first." Her hand settles against his chest, giving herself an ordinary physical reference point. "Then show me what you did." Another beat. "And if I discover that you spent seven subjective hours being chased through Aztechnology while lying beside me for two minutes… I reserve the right to revise my earlier opinion about your sleeping habits." She closes her eyes. "Go."

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:53 PDT, Taetzel**
Glaze can feel her own body in his hands, resting against his chest: beating pulse and breath.
Then the smell. Everything is completely obvious. Singer's body sorts the room into identities before conscious analysis seems necessary, like animal instinct—*her* scent and *his*, separate and known; cleaners, detergent, dust, warm electronics, evaporating plastics, the tang of her taste from their kiss.
The wide-frequency hearing interests her less than what it does with space. She can feel and hear the exact location of the small things moving and breathing in the house, even through walls and doors: a robot changing direction beyond a wall, the scratch and breathing of a mouse, crickets positioned in darkness she cannot see. The building becomes populated by small, exact points of life and machinery.
Then Singer narrows it all away. The sounds isolate until only her heartbeat and breathing remain, distinct as a concert in an opera house. Through his senses, her pulse is not an abstraction felt beneath fingertips; it has position, depth, rhythm, and her breathing moves beside it with almost orchestral separation.
Singer lights his flashlight—specially made, not LED, but a wider, softer spectral white. Color ceases behaving quite the way modern displays taught her it should. Yellow is not an agreement between red, green, and blue; it is yellow itself, and so is every other color of the rainbow. Like her, he sees near infrared and soft UV, and those fringes sit naturally beside the visible spectrum. His nictitating membranes flicker open and the room seems brighter. His eyes focus on detail in the distant skyline, then come down to her face—individual lashes, pores on her cheek, tiny variations of skin and moisture.
"Ready for the trip?" She hears it in his voice, through his ears.
Glaze smiles, and through his senses she gets the peculiar doubled experience of feeling that smile form while also seeing it in microscopic detail. "Yes." Then, fascinated despite herself: "But afterward we are discussing how you ever tolerate a crowded restaurant." Her hand remains against him. "Take me."

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:54 PDT, Taetzel**
Singer lets the file play: his point of view in the Matrix. Not what the system saw, but what he saw of its places—how his persona interpreted what the system wanted him to see, until he forced its secrets bare. It does not feel like watching a recording. It feels like being admitted into Singer's way of seeing a war: the negotiated surface between system, persona, metaphor, expectation, and force.
Once again a wide spectrum of mythic creatures—hounds, foxes, and angels—do his bidding or swaddle his persona and local space. Time immediately becomes unreliable. Many actions are as precise as sword slices by a master swordsman, cut after cut so clean they barely seem to occupy duration at all. Other moments drag into oppressive thickness while searches and analysis slow the moment to a crawl.
The first satellite host is a pretty Amazon rainforest. Canopy rises like cathedral architecture, great trees serving as the transfer locations, rivers of data flowing with real current and volume. Glaze feels Singer's comfort there—not safety exactly, but practiced command. The second is a stellar symphony, movement in vacuum made musical and architectural at once. The third is a science-fiction set-piece, starships zipping through gates in a theater of maneuver and transit.
And then Aztechnology.
It appears as it does in the Seattle RTG. The pyramid receives him on its ground floor, sculpted throughout in Maya and Aztec stylings, sacred iconography appropriated into status and control. Magic is thematic and pervasive—a declaration that power and ritual are the same thing.
"So this," Glaze says somewhere inside it, in the way one speaks while watching something impossible, "is how they want themselves imagined."
The machinery of the hosts is smooth and fluid. Floor by floor the dangerous tone elevates, but only very slowly does anything catch on. The place does not feel sloppy; it feels rich enough to make coherence beautiful. The last few floors before the thirtieth are occupied by fortresses and priest-agent things of great power, but their eyes do not see him yet.
On the thirtieth floor everything changes. Battle lines are readied and war-things caper—not because they know or see, but because this is where the most powerful combat agents live, a main battle line guarding the ruling strata above. Simsense carries the filtered feeling of the fear and power they project as weapons, and Glaze physically tightens against Singer in the bed despite knowing it is already over.
He hammers systems looking for the data along the full path, and here he finds it: the back structure for the entire company, hand-monitored by real people with powerful decks and programs nearing his own. Even then, it is only happenstance that a cascade of encounters finally begins. Great Scythe barely makes the kills on a Red system edging toward Ultraviolet. Hog utilities hammer the deckers, turning their programs into mush. Agonizing lengths of time pass in deep, darkened, blood-stained halls holding the backups. Glaze does not know every technical meaning of what she sees, but she knows what deliberate ruin feels like, and there is nothing casual in it.
The exit comes as he told it: the cascading tally has had its effects, and everything goes active before the twentieth floor. Harder than he wants, he flees—not panic, but urgency with teeth. Defenses boil attacks away from him, and Orthogonal Gates twist trace away from his path over and over, a felt, repeated shearing of pursuit just before closure. As he enters the twelfth floor, it erupts into klaxons and trumpets, signaling shutdown to protect itself from the invasion.
It finishes with a smooth exit from the Amazonian forest, into the telecom gateway, and back to the bedroom.
The dim room returns with shocking softness. Glaze opens her eyes. Her breathing is deeper than when they began, and one of her hands has closed against him hard enough to leave the memory of grip in her fingers. Her expression is not frightened. It is impressed, thoughtful, and a little appalled.
"That," she says quietly, "was not at all like reading a report. The beautiful parts were beautiful. The ugly parts were much more informative." She shifts up enough to see his face. "I understand more now. Not only that you were in danger. I understand the *texture* of the danger. And the corporate aesthetics are repellent."
Amusement wins out. "The forest was lovely. The stars were lovely. The battle of starships was absurdly entertaining. And then the blood halls under the temple reminded me that all of it is attached to payroll." She studies him a moment longer. "You fought beautifully." Then, more dryly: "And next time you tell me something took two minutes, I will ask how many cathedrals, forests, battle fleets, fortresses, priest-warriors, and blood archives were hidden inside the statement."

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 04:56 PDT, Taetzel**
Singer opens the connection again, this time casually over wireless, not needing maximal bandwidth. He ports it to Glaze the way a hitcher jack would—less his own feelings and perceptions than what the persona sees.
She recognizes the difference immediately. She is no longer inside Singer's senses. She has a viewpoint of her own, carried along with his persona but free to turn, look, and examine while his masking folds and reforms around them. Her body remains a distant, unmistakable reference in bed.
He takes them to the satellite gateway of the science-fiction news and entertainment host in low Earth orbit, and there he null operates—staying to watch while the dance happens, his only actions the sensor operations the deck continues to make, subtle masking seething around them. Ships cross impossible distances in seconds. Gates flare open, swallow traffic, and close. Tiny courier craft vanish almost before she can follow them; massive vessels move with stately deliberation, trailing streams of subsidiary icons. Nothing reacts to their presence.
"Here our words happen at rates similar to dense telepathy," Singer says.
Glaze turns toward his persona. "That is much closer."
"The action does not slow unless something with density makes it do so. This world seems to flow four to forty times as slow—time dilated and stretched—but torrents of data still pass. That's all the ships and gates are: satellite gateways heading to their receivers."
The spectacle reorganizes itself in her understanding. Those aren't ships. They're connections—gateways to other satellites, terrestrial receivers, uplinks and routes, all rendered into comprehensible geography.
She watches a bright cruiser slip through a gate. "So the earlier slowness was not merely your mind being accelerated." Another ship fractures into smaller streams and disappears toward different receivers. "The work itself creates duration."
She spends a while simply watching. The longer she does, the less it resembles entertainment. Patterns emerge: repeated routes, traffic densities, pauses before transfers, the difference between something merely crossing the host and something actually interacting with it. She points toward a formation disappearing through three successive gates. "That entire battle formation is traffic?"
When Singer confirms it, the enormous apparent scale suddenly strikes her as funny. She laughs. "You people built a universe because looking at a routing table was unpleasant." More thoughtfully: "But this is much better than the recording. I can look where I choose."
She turns completely away from him, studying a region he isn't looking toward, testing the independence of her viewpoint. "And you are doing almost nothing." She glances back. "Which I now understand is one of the more difficult things you do."

**Friday, June 9, 2051 — approximately 05:02 PDT, Taetzel**
"You might recognize that the pace of time when we talk is similar to astral time when projecting," Singer tells her. "That is because it is the non-dilated mental speed of thought. My deck is good enough to reach that parity and just about exceed it."
"Yes. That is what was familiar." Glaze turns her viewpoint back toward him. "Not the place. The pace. When I project, I do not experience myself as accelerated. Flesh simply ceases to be the limiting part." A ship streaks through a gate beside them. "So this machine has become fast enough that the machine is no longer substantially slowing *you*."
Singer brings them home. The science-fiction sky collapses back into the connection, then the bedroom: dim early-morning light, warm sheets, physical weight, breathing, and the comparatively ponderous tempo of mouths having to form words.
Glaze pulls off the knit cap and examines it before setting it beside the bed. "That also explains something else." She settles against him again. "Dense telepathy never seemed fast to me. Speech seemed slow." Her eyes close.
Singer rests while they talk, and the conversation wanders between media rather naturally—through the telecom host, the real world, even the astral. A few sentences spoken aloud. Thoughts traded when looking at each other makes language unnecessarily cumbersome. At one point Glaze briefly slips her awareness astral and returns with an amused certainty that the comparison holds from her side as well.
"The Matrix is stranger," she says afterward. "Astral space does not need to pretend that information is a starship. But the *thinking* is close."
She curls back against him. "And now I understand why you can accomplish an unreasonable amount of work while everyone else believes two minutes have passed." Her hand finds his. "I still intend to sleep through some of it."
