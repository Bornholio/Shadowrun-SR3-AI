# PLAYER INTERACTIONS — GLAZE & SINGER
Interpersonal additions from the Struggle session.

This file carries only relationship-relevant development from this session. Stable prior relationship history remains in the earlier interaction file.

# CURRENT STATE ADDITIONS

Singer and Glaze remain established lovers and companions. This session did not materially redefine the relationship; instead it showed the relationship functioning across technical, magical, domestic, instructional, professional, and ordinary public life.

The important development is not greater intensity but greater normality: intimacy, mutual reliance, and affection increasingly coexist with each person doing independent work rather than every scene becoming a relationship event.

## Shared magical trust
Singer showed Glaze abilities and uncertainties he does not treat casually:
- Infinite Warehouse.
- Astral Shift.
- His concern that the Warehouse may interfere with focus bonds.
- His uncertainty about whether Astral Shift helped cause his arrival in her cave.
- His memory of ancient magical objects that do not fit modern focus categories.

Glaze did not turn those disclosures into a demand for proof. She refused to risk one of her bonded foci merely to satisfy curiosity about the Warehouse, reinforcing that trust does not require reckless experimentation.

Singer and Glaze continue to distinguish between what Singer suspects and what is established. Glaze did not accept his theory of being drawn to her lair as fact simply because it was emotionally or magically plausible.

## Domestic and sexual familiarity
The night ended with an ordinary shared shower and private intimacy, then sleep. This did not create a new relationship category or require special interpretation. Their sexual relationship is established enough that intimacy can be part of the background of a full day rather than the day's defining event.

Practical supplies purchased Monday—including condoms and female hygiene supplies—fit the same pattern: Singer and Glaze are treating human-form life and sexual consequences as matters to prepare for rather than dramatize.

## Teaching style and autonomy
Singer gave Glaze intensive English pronunciation coaching. The session emphasized physical articulation, playback, and practical correction rather than broad lecturing. Glaze allowed close physical guidance where useful, including direct mouth/tongue positioning, because it served instruction she had chosen.

The more important relationship point is Singer's continued reduction of unnecessary rescue behavior. He helped with reading and vocabulary but increasingly allowed Glaze to struggle productively through spoken English and ordinary interactions.

Glaze continues to prefer support that increases competence rather than support that replaces her action.

## Arcology cover dynamic
Inside the Renraku Arcology, Singer and Glaze jointly performed the cover of a wealthy Tir elven couple. Their public behavior was affectionate and coordinated without becoming a control dynamic.

Singer leaned into the role of the decorative, romantic affluent companion while Glaze shopped. Their teasing reinforced the relationship's ease:

Singer: “You are quite lovely hun.”

Glaze, in elaborate ancient Sperethiel: “You say this as though you have only just noticed.”

After Singer returned from his Matrix work and handled the bags, Glaze teased him that he appeared well trained and that she might keep him. The tone was mutual play, not ownership.

## Fashion and social learning
Glaze's clothing questions were not merely about what looked good. She increasingly treated clothing as a social language:
- how to signal power without appearing military;
- how to signal wealth without suggesting dangerous importance;
- how to appear attractive without demanding attention;
- how trend signals spread through imitation and public attention.

Singer did not impose a style identity on her. He helped her access people who could explain the signals and allowed her to make her own purchases.

## Bell / identity autonomy
The completed Debbie Harry identity is now functional enough to support Glaze's own electronic life. Singer used it to initialize a pocket secretary, comm-codes, handset, AR glasses, reader, and data store, then hardened the devices so they would not constantly expose one persistent signature.

The relationship significance is the same as earlier money/identity work: Singer is giving Glaze infrastructure for independent action rather than making himself the required intermediary for every modern task.

His summary—“Now they will lie for you.”—was a concise continuation of his teaching that privacy preserves choice.

## Face role
Singer explained the Shadowrunner Face role and told Glaze she would be very good at it.

Glaze's interpretation was important: she understood Face work as managing people in much the same way Singer manages systems—through information, positioning, trust, access, timing, surveillance, counterintelligence, and misdirection.

She did not treat herself as a novice socially. She correctly identified the real weakness as modern context: current slang, references, rapidly changing customs, and jokes.

Singer's answer—that he could teach the fast-changing context while the deeper social competence was already hers—reinforced a recurring pattern in their relationship: he supplies modern scaffolding without defining her underlying ability.

## Libraries and ordinary shared life
Glaze strongly liked the Downtown Library and later Mara Velez. Singer deliberately let Mara and Glaze continue talking and exploring without inserting himself into every exchange.

This continued an important prior development: Singer can remain present without being Glaze's social engine.

Glaze's response to the library also shows growing personal taste independent of Singer. She was interested not only in information but in the institution's role as a place for research, access, shelter, meeting, and simply existing without a purchase requirement.

After dinner she said:

“I like libraries.”

Then:

“And librarians.”

## Evening initiative
After dinner, Singer let Glaze choose the route for the remainder of the evening. She chose a walk, selected a path with enough people to reduce pointless trouble, and then chose a small open-stage music venue because she wanted to hear what ordinary people sing when nobody important is listening.

This is consistent with her broader modern development: curiosity is becoming self-directed rather than a sequence of Singer-selected demonstrations.

The relationship remains:
