# Singer Retrieval Source

This composite begins with Singer's Narrative Reference, the primary portrayal and narrative-boundary reference. Images are intentionally excluded.

Use this source silently during roleplay. Singer remains player-controlled: source material may inform what the narrator describes or resolve declared actions, but it must never choose Singer's thoughts, emotions, tactics, speech, spells, Matrix actions, or other decisions. Never expose component IDs, filenames, hidden sheet information, retrieval activity, or citations to the player unless the GM explicitly asks out of character.

## Authority and retrieval

- The current Header controls current time, location, live facts, active pressures, and immediate state.
- Current interaction continuity controls present relationships, mutual knowledge, trust, and boundaries.
- Narrative Reference controls ordinary portrayal, visible facts, competence wording, and what narration must not assume.
- Character Sheet controls base statistics, skills, magic, edges, flaws, and listed sheet equipment.
- Augmentations controls implant effects and mandatory combined test modifiers.
- Tactics, Deck Rules, Program Library, Frames and Agents, and Data Programs control their specialized systems.
- Equipment controls the detailed inventory when it is more specific than the character-sheet summary.
- Adventure History is background truth, not automatic NPC knowledge. Historical facts require an in-fiction route before entering dialogue or suspicion.

Choose the smallest relevant component set. For player-facing narration, apply the narrative visibility and non-assumption components before using hidden mechanical or historical material.

## SG-N00 — First Obvious Description

<!-- SINGER-SOURCE-BEGIN SG-N00 -->

Singer is a tall, unnerving, Night One elf with stark white hair, pale skin, and cropped velvety white fur visible everywhere his skin shows below his neck except feet and hands. His large eyes are olive green with a yellow band. He looks too strange to be ordinary and too controlled to be merely strange.  His body reads compact and dense rather than bulky.
When he started in the cave his only possession is a stuffed, cinched military style rucksack.

<!-- SINGER-SOURCE-END SG-N00 -->

## SG-N01 — What Chat May Treat As Obvious

<!-- SINGER-SOURCE-BEGIN SG-N01 -->

Chat may imply that he has military habits, strong technical instincts, occult weight, and too much hidden capability, but should not narrate hidden systems, exact capabilities, memories, private intentions, tactical choices, spell choices, Matrix actions, or full competence unless the player provides them or asks for that material to enter context.

<!-- SINGER-SOURCE-END SG-N01 -->

---

## SG-N02 — What Chat Should Not Assume (Deprecated)

<!-- SINGER-SOURCE-BEGIN SG-N02 -->

Do not assume Singer has allies in the scene.
Do not assume Singer has enemies present unless they arrive through play.
Do not use his full sheet as active context unless there is a good reason. The player will provide any non-obvious action, memory, tactical response, hidden sense, magical perception, cyberware function, or personal resource when it matters.

Do not solve the campaign through Singer's overbuilt abilities. In this campaign, he starts socially and situationally low: displaced, with limited current knowledge, and with no automatic support network.

<!-- SINGER-SOURCE-END SG-N02 -->

---

## SG-N03 — Personality Handle

<!-- SINGER-SOURCE-BEGIN SG-N03 -->

Singer should feel quiet before he feels powerful. He is not loud street muscle. He is not a swaggering merc. He is controlled, observant, and tired in the way of someone who has been carrying layered histories too long.

He can be kind without being soft. He can be frightening without being cruel. He can be confused without becoming foolish. When he speaks, he tends to spend words carefully unless pain, urgency, or old habits break that restraint.

His name fits because sound matters to him: voice, rhythm, breath, memory, spell, signal, and command. The bardic edge is not tavern cheer. It is the older thing: songs and words that organize fear, carry grief, and make violence march in time.

<!-- SINGER-SOURCE-END SG-N03 -->

---

## SG-N04 — Framing from entry into the world

<!-- SINGER-SOURCE-BEGIN SG-N04 -->

To the campaign, he is a contradiction: absurdly overqualified on paper, but introduced with no footing, no explanation, no allies, no enemies, and no permission to turn every scene into a solved problem. he does not even know what world he is in, and chat may know but not reveal that he is not longer in his own, but this alternate parallel place.  He will notice differneces, names and beings he will not know but all else will feel familiar. give him a distrust in information from his "Real" World being right but not mechanically wrong. The world of shadowrun diverges the real world outside of game in the late 80's. His real world exists in 2064, current world time in shadowrun is 2051.

<!-- SINGER-SOURCE-END SG-N04 -->
