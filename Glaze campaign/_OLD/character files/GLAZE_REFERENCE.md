## Discoverable Historical Traces

These are the only presently confirmable historical records of Glaze.

### The Great Dragon and the Bull

"She who devoured the bull of the city's heart" in Proto-Dravidian

Near the end of an ancient era, Glaze descended on Silaṅkoṭṭai at night trailing blue fire, crossed the city, took the temple bull in her forejaws, and ate it in the open square while priests and families hid.

She did not burn the granaries or destroy the gates. She sat afterward with blue light pooling from her throat and looked over the city before departing before dawn.

The memory survived in whispers and an iron pillar raised so the fear would not be forgotten.

**Plot access:** a Vedic priest or academic might eventually connect the account to Glaze after a public Great Dragon appearance. The connection is remote.

### The Neah Bay Feeding

"the bare one who became the storm, eater of the blackfish." in Wikáasidaq a precursor to proto-Makah

Glaze came ashore near what is now Neah Bay as a naked, pale, white-haired woman and received guest-fire from a Wikáasidaq travelling party.

By dawn she had taken dragon form and fed on a beached orca before disappearing into cloud.

The party raised a bilingual stone in Wikáasidaq and early proto-Makah. Its halves were separated for centuries and later catalogued together at the University of Washington without recognition.

In 1970 a graduate student briefly understood enough of the Wikáasidaq text through the known Makah column to make fragmentary notes. The translation contradicted accepted migration chronology, was never published, and survives only as obscure marginalia in an unfinished thesis file.

**Plot access:** research following a public appearance might eventually uncover the source. The likelihood rises substantially if Glaze teaches either lost language and that teaching enters the academic record.

## Lair and Awakening Reference

The entrance to Glaze's cave is sealed beneath scree on a breccia shelf below Lincoln's summit block in the Mt. Baker region.

Inside, the cavity is barely large enough for her: coiled nose to tail, wings folded flat and doubled back, body filling the chamber like a fist in a glove. Pale claw marks score the stone where centuries of settling sleep have ground against the same few inches.

The surrounding astral background is savage and uneven, pooled around the buried entrance and thinning outward. Astral observers near the ridge may experience vertigo or the sensation of being regarded by something with no eyes pointed at them.

This establishes the awakening location. It is not itself evidence of Glaze's identity.
