## The Lair, Mt. Baker region, Salish-Sidhe 
The entrance is barely that anymore. A collapse, ice-shove or a shift in the old breccia, it doesn't much matter which, sealed the low crack that once let something her size into the rock. Nothing has cleared it since. Inside, there's no room to speak of: coiled nose to tail, wings folded flat and doubled back against the stone, she fills the cavity the way a fist fills a glove, the walls scored pale in a slow half circle where claws have ground against the same six inches over what must be centuries of settling deeper into sleep. It isn't a lair in the sense of a hoard hall or a throne room. It's closer to a burrow that happens to hold something the size of a building, cramped, black, silent except for a breathing so slow between breaths that anything human waiting nearby would eventually assume the sound had stopped for good.

What isn't cramped is the astral shape of the place. The background count around that stretch of rock is savage, not evenly distributed the way a ward or a lodge would produce, but pooled and curdled, thick near the sealed entrance and thinning unevenly outward, the astral signature of something old and singular rather than a location that was ever meant to hold power. Magicians who've gone up onto the Black Buttes for the climbing, not for anything occult, have come back down reporting nothing worse than a bad feeling on that particular ridge, but the handful of people who actually perceive astrally anywhere near Lincoln's north face describe it closer to vertigo: a directionlessness, a sense of being regarded by something with no eyes pointed at them, gone the moment they're back over the col. Enough people have logged the same complaint about the same quarter mile of rock that it's picked up an informal reputation in Seattle's small community of people who'd notice such things, not investigated, not explained, just quietly avoided, the way the ordinary population avoids Sherman Crater's gas.

The entrance sits on the breccia shelf just below Lincoln's summit block, where the rock steps out over the Deming Glacier's bergschrund. Scree buries the seam, indistinguishable from any other loose pitch on that ridge, crossed by climbers who read it as nothing. The last short pitch up to Lincoln's own summit lies within easy reach of that shelf, the one point on the ridge from which the Deming Glacier, Baker's ice cap, the Sound, and the city's glitter would all lie open at once, whenever the seam is finally cleared from the other side.

In the last score of years before the era's ending, when the granaries of Silaṅkoṭṭai stood full and its gates had not yet learned rust, on a night when the moon gave no light, a shape came down out of the dark western sky trailing a tail of blue fire, and the watch on the wall raised no cry before she had already crossed the market square; she went to the temple yard where the bull of the city's center was tethered before the shrine of the harvest god, and she took it in her forejaws as a woman might lift a lamb, and she fed there in the open square while the priests lay flat on the stones and mothers carried their children from their beds too frightened to look back; when she had finished she did not burn the granaries or break the gates, she only sat upon her haunches with blue light pooling from her throat like water finding the low ground, and looked at the city the way a queen looks at a room she has already decided belongs to her, and then, before the eastern sky had gone from black to grey, she was gone the way smoke goes, upward and apart, and no one in Silaṅkoṭṭai spoke her name aloud for a generation, though the old women taught their daughters to call her, in whispers only, Eṟuttiṉṟavaḷ, the one who ate the bull of the center, and this pillar of iron was raised in the year following so the fear would not be forgotten with the fear-holders, and so that any who came after would know why the temple bull of Silaṅkoṭṭai is, to this day, never tethered in the open square after dark.

## The feeding
*Sitl'aqa Ho'niitsa Qwa'looma* "the bare one who became the storm, eater of the blackfish." Short form: *Sitl'aqa*

She came ashore near what is now Neah Bay as a naked, pale, white-haired woman, was given the guest-fire by a Wikáasidaq travelling party, and by dawn had taken dragon form and fed on a beached orca at the point rock before vanishing into cloud. The party raised a stone recording the event: the front in narrative carving, the back in two parallel scripts — Wikáasidaq and early Qʷi·qʷi·diččaq (proto-Makah) — a bilingual key against the language being lost.

The stone cracked and its halves separated for centuries before both were catalogued together, unrecognized as a pair, at the University of Washington. In 1970 a graduate student cross-reading the two columns had a sudden, complete understanding of the Wikáasidaq text — unlocked through the known Makah column — and transcribed fragments before it faded. The translation contradicted the department's accepted migration chronology, was quietly set aside, and was never published. The stone remains in the collection; the translation exists only as unindexed margin notes in an unfinished thesis file.

These two stories are the only confirmable historical record of her existence. One a written record catalogued and half-forgotten at a university, the other a story kept alive in whispers by the women of a city that no longer stands

## Forms

### Full Dragon Form
*30m body · 4.5m shoulder · 25m tail · 45m wingspan*

Dusk on the ridge, and she comes off the stone like the mountain letting out a breath. Fifty tons cross into open air on the first downstroke. Wings catch the last orange light along their trailing edge. The whole vast weight of her folds into motion. Her hide drinks the color of the evening, obsidian pooling into shadow along her flank. Veins of sapphire trace the ridge of her spine, catching the light deep and wet rather than sharp. Climbers on the scree below watch the sky and lose her in it, a shape sliding through blue becoming blue becoming dark.

Slender fins run the length of her tail, blue and smooth, rippling with each wingbeat. They taper into a fringe of fine blue filament that trails behind her like smoke off a wick. Her horns curve back from her brow in a long, wavering blade, catching the last light and throwing it back in a thin edge. When she turns her head, amber answers from somewhere behind her eyes, banked and steady, the color of coals. At her throat a second light glows blue in time with it, the same pale thread running quiet through every strand of her hair.

She parts her jaw to test the wind. For a moment her throat lights white-blue from within, a held note before the sound arrives. Later, out over open water, she lets it go: a narrow lance of blue-white flame, thin as a torch-cutter, that turns the crest of a wave to steam and leaves the air behind it tasting of scorched salt.

### Partial (Hybrid) Form
A humanoid frame, roughly human-scaled but unmistakably not human. Pale skin carries that same faint blue undersheen. Waist-length hair the color of glacier ice. Vestiges of the dragon are retained rather than hidden: a three-meter tail, the same black curved horns, faceted amber eyes. The same obsidian hide and sapphire veining persist over her torso, shoulders, and limbs in smooth, armor-like segments. The glowing blue core at her throat is visible here too, worn openly rather than concealed. This is the form she wears when she wants to be recognized as what she is without the full scale of the dragon.

### Human Form
No tail. No horns. No scale. But nothing about her reads as ordinary. She carries herself like a queen who has never once doubted the room was hers. Her outfit is glossy black and form-fitting, seamed everywhere with fine orichalcum fittings and banding. Not decoration, but living metalwork: the orichalcum carries threads of her own magic woven through the fabric so the garment survives the transformation with her rather than being shed or torn. The same pale blue-white hair and ice-pale skin carry over from her other forms, along with that lingering amber warmth in her eyes that a careful observer might find unsettling on a human face. The glowing core at her throat is banked low but never fully dark, a soft blue glint just visible beneath the collar of the outfit.
