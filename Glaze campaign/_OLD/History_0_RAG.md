## The cave before Singer arrives

The chamber is beneath the Mt. Baker region, inside an ancient sealed cavity near Lincoln Peak.

It is not a throne room, treasure hall, or constructed fortress. It is closer to an enormous natural burrow. The entrance has been buried by geologic change, ice movement, scree, and collapse.

The cavity is extremely cramped for what occupies it. Glaze fills it almost completely in full dragon form, coiled nose to tail with her wings folded flat and doubled back against the stone. The surrounding rock bears old marks from the slow movement of a sleeping creature with building-scale mass.

The surrounding astral environment carries a **Background Count of 4**. It is savage, uneven, and old, pooled most heavily around the buried entrance and strongly associated with Glaze's long presence. It is not an ordinary ward, lodge, or recent magical contamination.

Natural earth and the dense local astral conditions make astral travel through the surrounding stone difficult. Small weaknesses and gaps exist, but the cave is substantially enclosed both physically and astrally.

Outside is high mountain terrain: snow, glaciers, broken ridges, forested distances, and the broader Pacific Northwest landscape.

Glaze is a **Great Western Fire Dragon** and a relic of an age so old that much of her personal history predates reliable surviving records.

She begins the scene asleep in full dragon form.

She was already drifting toward wakefulness before Singer arrived. The world's mana had risen enough that continued sleep was becoming less attractive. Her initial state is sleepy, curious, patient, somewhat hungry, and bored rather than alarmed or hostile.

Glaze is dual-natured and does not need to open her physical eyes before the astral disturbance becomes meaningful to her.

Her full dragon body is approximately thirty meters long before the tail, with roughly fifty tons of mass. Her hide is obsidian-black with sapphire veining, swept black horns, faceted amber eyes, and a pale blue-white luminosity at the throat. Her scale and mass must be treated as materially real in the environment.

Glaze does not know the modern world. She does not begin with knowledge of current nations, technology, institutions, politics, recent history, or modern culture. Her intelligence and skill allow strong inference, but do not provide facts she has not observed, remembered, inferred, or been taught.

Her preferred communication is telepathy. In full dragon form she should not casually use metahuman spoken languages; her physical vocal apparatus is draconic.

## Singer in the tank

### Short account

Singer floats in green-gold nutrient/antiseptic solution, tubes into throat, spine, both forearms. His body shows dozens of tiny, already-healed nanite-closed incisions across torso, limbs, and skull. The precise, clinical work has been repeated on this same body before. His mind is elsewhere: a sandboxed matrix construct, drilling with a new deck he hasn't mastered, no real stakes or connection.
The lab around him is routine and unbothered: techs, a healer's astral check, a cart rolling by. On a cart beside his tank: his closed rucksack, untouched, and a folded pair of white boxer briefs waiting for him.

### Full narrative

The tank hums low, fluid cycling nutrients and antiseptic past incisions closed with a precision no human hand could manage.

Singer floats suspended in cloudy green-gold solution, weightless in the particular way only a body with nothing left to prove can be. Tubes thread into port sites at his throat, spine, and both forearms. Their clean, deliberate insertion shows the unhurried precision of people who have done this exact procedure on exactly this body before, and expect to again. His compact, dense frame drifts a few degrees off true vertical, drawn slowly around by the tank's convection current, then drawn back.

His body is a study in surgical perfection. Every incision is tiny, a few centimeters at most. Dozens are scattered with clinical purpose across torso, limbs, skull, and every one of them is already closed, days healed, the nanite instruments having worked with a fineness no scalpel and hand ever could. No ragged lines. No inflammation. Just a faint constellation of hairline seams, silver-pale against his skin, each one so straight and so small it looks almost decorative, like a pattern etched rather than cut. His fine white fur grows in close around them, undisturbed. Nothing here reads as trauma. It reads as design.

His face is slack in a way unconsciousness never quite manages. Not peaceful. Vacant, in the specific manner of a mind that has stepped fully out of the meat and into somewhere else: a sandboxed matrix construct, isolated and inert, where he's running drills against a new deck he hasn't finished learning yet. No stakes here. No signal reaching anything real. Just a man teaching his hands a new kind of fast, over and over, while his body finishes becoming what it's being built into.

Around him the lab keeps its own unbothered rhythm. Each pod stands separate, individually sealed, individually monitored, and the staff assigned to this wing move through it like people clocking a shift rather than witnessing a miracle: a tech checks a readout two pods down and moves on without comment, a healer's murmured chant threads past on the astral and fades, a cart's wheels tick faintly over a floor seam somewhere out of sight. No one lingers at his tank longer than the length of a glance.

On the cart beside the tank: the rucksack, closed, straps cinched tight, sitting untouched exactly where it was set down. Beside it, a folded pair of white boxer briefs waits with the plain, almost domestic patience of an object meant for a body that isn't ready to wear it yet.

## A day passes

### Short account

Morning: a cleanroom tech runs a diagnostic and moves on. Midday: staff chat about another pod; a healer's astral sweep registers nothing. Afternoon: a partial fluid exchange, warmer solution cycled in. Evening: shift change, dimmed lights, a new tech's walking pass. Singer stays "perfectly still, perfectly healed, perfectly elsewhere" the whole time.

### Full narrative

Morning brings a tech in a sealed cleanroom suit, pausing at the tank's base to tap through a diagnostic on a slate before moving on without expression. The fluid recyclers cycle louder for a moment, drawing off the night's filtration load, then settle back to their idle murmur.

Midday brings two staff passing close, talking in low clinical shorthand about a different pod further down the row. A healer drifts through on a slow circuit, her attention brushing briefly across the tank on the astral, registering nothing worth stopping for, moving on. The cart beside him goes undisturbed.

Afternoon brings a partial fluid exchange: a soft pressure shift, a few minutes of faint turbulence as old solution drains and new floods in, warmer, freshly balanced. His body rolls slightly with the current before settling back to center.

Evening brings the shift change, the ambient light dimming a few degrees to mark it. A new tech takes over the wing, does a walking pass of every pod, confirms seals and readouts with a practiced glance, and moves on without incident. The building settles into its night rhythm.

Singer floats through all of it exactly as he began: perfectly still, perfectly healed, perfectly elsewhere.

## The breach

### Short account

A diagnostic feed reads suspiciously, artificially clean. A ward is breached quietly on its northeast face; a mage on duty feels something and dismisses it. A locked door unlocks itself with no one touching it. Four attackers move into the lower corridor in tight, practiced formation, with a fifth holding the door. They show no hesitation and already know the layout. Down in his tank, Singer's eyes open.

### Full narrative

Three floors above the tank, a diagnostic feed blinks green for the four hundredth time in a row. It is too green and too steady, the kind of clean signal that only exists when someone is feeding it on purpose. Nobody notices. The tech on rounds walks past a monitor showing an empty corridor that hasn't been empty in ninety seconds.

The outer ward doesn't break. It stretches, somewhere along its northeast face, thin enough that starlight would leak through if starlight were what was pressing on it. Something slides through the gap instead, quiet as breath held too long, and the mage stationed in the east wing frowns at nothing, rubs her temple, and goes back to her tea.

A service door that was locked a minute ago simply isn't, and nobody touched the handle to find that out.

Four shapes move into the lower corridor in a formation, not a crowd: close, low, weapons already up, each one clearing an angle the instant before it matters and not a half-second after. Boots that should echo on polished concrete don't. A fifth shape holds the doorway they came through, watching the hall behind them the whole time the other four watch the hall ahead. They don't call out. They don't hesitate at the first junction. They already know which way to turn.

Down in the tank, floating in green-gold silence, Singer's eyes are open.

## Waking

### Short account

The tank bursts open under strength beyond what his frame should produce, rather than the assisted gradual cycle it was built for. He comes down out of it already moving: tubes torn free without flinching, nanites shaking off him in a visible glitter. He hits the floor, coughs fluid from lungs, has the boxer briefs on and rucksack strap in hand within the same breath, and shouts at two nearby technicians to get out because the door is about to break down.

### Full narrative

The tank doesn't open so much as it stops being asked permission. The seal cracks wide under a strength that reads wrong for the compact frame producing it, glass and gasket giving way in a single motion instead of the assisted, gradual cycle the machine was built for.

Fluid sheets off him in a wave that hits the floor before the alarms do. He comes down out of it already moving, tubes tearing free of throat and spine and forearms without a flinch, and for one strange instant his skin catches the light wrong: a fine, scattering glitter of nanites clinging to him like frost, shaken loose in a shimmer as he clears the lip of the tank.

He hits the floor, coughs once, hard, fluid coming up out of lungs that weren't built to hold air a second ago, and he's already reaching. The boxer briefs are on before the cough finishes, one motion, no wasted time, and his hand is closing on the rucksack strap in the same breath.

"MOVE." It cracks out of him at the two nearest technicians, raw-throated. "OUT! NOW! GO."

The door hasn't opened yet. It's going to.

## The fight

### Short account

Six attackers converge on him:
- **Two augmented operators**: fast but visibly slower than Singer, firing disciplined 3-round bursts. First hits his ribs (bruising only); second misses.
- **A gas canister**, custom-spec'd against his physiology, tightens his throat but only becomes a manageable ache thanks to his augmentation.
- **A launcher operator** fires a rocket. Overpressure and concussive force knock him half a step off his footing, costing him ground even though he's braced.
- **Mages** cast from remote/supported power so they don't spend their own reserves. Singer's Reflecting sends the first two bolts back down the line, dropping both casters. A third partially gets through (current up one arm). By the fourth casting, Reflecting is spent and it connects.
- **A wired melee operator** closes distance fast but in discrete, "clocked" bursts rather than Singer's fluid continuous movement. Singer reads him a beat ahead but has no free beat to capitalize, stuck in full defense.
- **Monofilament whip** opens a cut across his forearm he can't spare a hand to block.
Over the exchange, five solid hits land: bullet, blade, blast, current, current. That is enough to have killed an unaugmented man, leaving Singer bleeding, breathing hard, and roughly half spent. Two of the mages are dead from their own reflected power, not from a direct attack by him.

### Full narrative

The door goes down and the room stops being a room. It becomes six vectors of intent all converging on one point in space, and Singer is standing in it, rucksack strap locked in one fist.

Two operators peel wide on entry, augmented legs carrying them faster than any unaugmented sprinter could manage, a clean, hard pace that would leave a track record broken behind them, but still a visible notch under the speed Singer's own body is built for, the gap showing in how much ground each stride costs them against how little the same distance would cost him. Short, disciplined bursts, not spray, three rounds, shift, three rounds. The first catches him across the ribs and leaves nothing but bruising under skin that shouldn't hold against ballistics like that. The second goes wide as he shifts weight without fully committing to movement.

A canister skids in from an angle behind him, already venting. It is something engineered, not standard riot-grade, spec'd against exactly his physiology. The first breath of it tightens his throat before augmentation earns its price tag, and what should have staggered an ordinary man drops to a manageable ache in his chest.

Across the room the launcher operator takes the extra half-second a rocket demands, bracing, sighting, committing. When it fires, it eclipses everything else in the room for an instant: overpressure, heat, a concussive wall catching him across the flank and throwing him half a step sideways even through braced footing. It costs him ground.

The mages barely move at all. They don't need to. A short step to clear a sightline, a shift of weight to hold ground, nothing more, casting carried on support that isn't in the room with them, unseen hands somewhere beyond the walls feeding power into every working so the casters themselves don't have to spend past what their own bodies can survive. The first bolt of current reaches for him anyway and Reflecting answers it clean, sending it back down the line to the caster, who goes rigid and drops without a sound. A second casting, a breath later, meets the same fate, with another mage folding to her own power turned back on her. A third attempt gets through partial, current walking up one arm instead of through his whole frame. Reflecting isn't there anymore to argue with the fourth.

There is no opening. The room is too full and the attackers too many, each one covering the gap the instant before it could become one: a wired operator crossing distance in a burst that outpaces anything unaugmented flesh could manage, weapon snapping into line the moment his feet plant, movement precise down to the centimeter but stitched together in discrete, separate beats rather than one continuous line, and slower for it than the man they're converging on. Where Singer moves like water finding the one channel already open, they move like something clocked and metered, with extraordinary speed delivered in clean, individual increments. He reads every one of them a fraction ahead and still has nowhere to spend the advantage. Full defense leaves no beat free to close distance, no beat free to answer.

Monofilament catches the light before it catches him, a fast precise cut opening a line across his forearm he doesn't have a hand free to protect. Gunfire finds him again. Current finds him again.

Five attacks land clean through the exchange: bullet, blade, blast, current, current. Each hits with force that should have ended an ordinary man in the opening seconds and instead leaves him marked, bleeding, breathing hard, spent halfway down before the room is done with him. Two of the mages who started this aren't breathing anymore. Their own power came back at them when it should have passed through Singer.

## Overflow and escape

### Short account

Fighting through injury: he turns with a shoulder hit to bleed off the force, shoves a cart one-handed as improvised cover, forces a buckling knee straight by will, and grinds through a current hit that arches his spine without making a sound. Bleeding from side, arm, and scalp, he doesn't look down. Pain isn't reaching him the way it should anymore, and that's the only thing keeping him upright.
He works a worn, familiar shape in his free hand (foci/component used across many years) while a dark astral tear opens ahead of him: light that isn't light, cold that isn't temperature. Rucksack strap still locked in his fist, he takes his last working step straight into it. The room's noise cuts off mid-syllable behind him, leading directly into the landing described in *The Fall*.

### Full narrative

A round catches him high on the shoulder and he turns with it instead of against it, letting the impact spend itself sideways instead of through him. His off hand finds the edge of a fallen cart and shoves it half around, not cover, just something between him and the muzzle for the fraction of a second it takes to matter. His knee buckles on landing and he forces it straight again by will alone, weight coming back under him before it fully leaves. Current finds him a heartbeat later and his jaw locks against it, teeth grinding rather than a sound coming out, spine arched half a degree off true and forced back.

Blood runs freely now, from his side, his arm, a line at his scalp. He doesn't look down at any of it. His free hand comes up, fingers curling through a shape worn smooth by repetition across more years than his face admits to, eyes fixed on the air ahead of him where a dark seam has begun tearing open, its edges bleeding light that isn't light, cold that isn't temperature, the raw geometry of somewhere else forcing itself through the walls of somewhere here.

He shouldn't be standing. Pain doesn't reach him the way it should, hasn't in a long time, and that difference is the only thing keeping him upright. His last working stretch of motion carries him one final step forward, rucksack strap still locked white-knuckled in his fist, straight into the tear.

The room's noise cuts off behind him mid-syllable.

## Transit and fall

### Short account

Singer is dragged through astral space at the end of a run. Worlds strobe past too fast to fix on before the pull sharpens toward a wound-like knot of mana (a blue like fire, a blue like sapphire, and a light-refusing dark beneath). Reality thins out in front of it and he drops through.
He lands hard on rock in a dark cave, eyes unable to open, body barely functional. Details registered by touch/smell/sound alone:
- Cold, uneven stone at his back; augmentation and nanite discipline are stretching what should be minutes of bleeding-out into hours.
- A faint dripping water sound, echoing enough to suggest a larger cavern.
- One steady source of low warmth nearby, like a coal that never goes out.
- Blood pooling in old worn channels in the rock, mixed with debris that doesn't belong: surgical tubing with a line still attached, a torn bandage strip, fragments of tank glass catching amber light.
- Smells layering in: sharp electric ozone, scorched/metallic singed-fabric weight with a wrong chemical sweetness, then older notes, medicinal residue from too-long submersion, and something sour from a body that "gave up more than blood" getting here.
- The cave itself is indifferent, ancient, dry, undisturbed by weather, going on exactly as it always has around him.

### Full narrative

Space stops being space somewhere past the tear, folding through boundaries that were never built for crossing, worlds strobing by too fast to hold: a sky the wrong color over something that might have been a forest, a shore where the water moves upward, a court of shapes too graceful to be safe, gone again before the eye can fix on any of it. Astral currents drag him faster than falling, faster than running, faster than any speed that has a name in the world he left.

Ahead, the pull sharpens toward a blot on the astral that shouldn't exist: mana knotted so dense it reads as a wound rather than a place, a shape massive and half-hidden inside its own density, colors that aren't colors bleeding together: a blue like fire, a blue like sapphire, and beneath both a darkness that isn't the absence of light so much as the refusal of it. Reality thins to nothing in front of that shape and then simply isn't there anymore.

It unzips. It always does, at the end.

He lands hard on rock, the impact driving what little air his lungs are still working with clean out of him. He doesn't open his eyes. He can't, not with what's left of him, and so the cave that receives him arrives only in what a body can register with sight gone: cold stone against his back, uneven and sharp in places, biting into skin that's still bleeding, slowly now, augmentation and nanite discipline stretching what should be minutes into something closer to hours. The air is chill enough to raise what's left of his fur, thin and mineral-tasting on his tongue with each shallow breath, carrying the faint mustiness of old stone that's never seen weather. Somewhere close, a sound reaches him. It is not voices or machinery, but something quieter and more patient: water finding its way through rock one drop at a time, echoing just enough to say the space around him is larger than his body currently fills. And warmth, faint and strange, radiating from one direction only, low and constant against his skin, like a coal that never quite goes out.

Blood spreads slow and dark across old rock, finding the same worn channels water has carved over an unknown span of years, pooling where the stone dips instead of soaking in. With it comes a scatter of things that don't belong on a cave floor: a length of surgical tubing, still trailing a thread of clear line, curled where it landed. A torn strip of bandage, unspooling loose across the stone. Fragments of tank glass, catching the amber light in small, scattered points before going dull with settling dust.

The mineral cold of the cave gets crowded out fast. A sharp, electric edge clings to him first, the smell of something that was current a moment ago and hasn't finished forgetting it. Under that, a scorched, metallic weight, the kind that sits in fabric after something violent has passed close enough to singe it, sweetened at the edges by a chemical taint that shouldn't be sweet at all, a taste more than a smell, catching low in the throat. Older notes surface last, the ones that don't announce themselves so much as settle: something faintly medicinal and wrong, clinging thin and slick to skin that spent too long submerged; something sour from a body that gave up more than blood on the way here. None of it has any business in a place this old and this empty. It fills the space anyway, stubborn, refusing to thin.

Somewhere in the black, a drip of water lands, a beat late against its own rhythm, then falls back into pattern. The amber light continues at the same steady output it has held throughout, its glow reaching the same distance across the same stones, a distance that happens, now, to end at torn skin, slowing blood, and shallow, uneven breath. Dust settles where it was disturbed. Air moves through the space in the same slow currents it always has, indifferent to what has just been added to the floor.

## Arrival in the cave

Singer arrives hard on cold stone inside a sealed mountain cavity with no visible entrance, path, or explanation for why he is there.

He is badly injured but stabilized rather than actively dying. He has suffered multiple distinct wounds from the attack before transit: ballistic trauma, a monofilament cut, blast damage, electrical injury, scalp injury, and associated blood loss. His medical augmentations and nanite systems are working aggressively to preserve him.

He is initially unconscious or only intermittently conscious. His visual systems are badly overloaded and require patient, one-system-at-a-time resets before usable sight can return. Other senses remain available and become important while vision is unavailable.

Singer is wearing only white boxer briefs and still possesses the stuffed, cinched military-style rucksack he carried through the transit. Debris from the medical environment arrived with him, including fragments of tank glass, torn tubing, bandage material, and residue from the recovery tank.

The cave air is cold, mineral, old, and faintly musty. Water moves slowly through the stone. Singer's arrival adds blood, antiseptic residue, scorched electrical odors, chemical contamination, and medical debris to an otherwise ancient space.

Singer does **not** know where he is, how far he has traveled, or that he is no longer in the version of the world he remembers.

Singer's arrival is extraordinary not because a wounded metahuman is physically dangerous to Glaze, but because a living being has appeared inside a sealed chamber through a route that should not exist.

## Opening medical and sensory state

Singer should enter the campaign as displaced rather than dominant.

Although he possesses extreme accumulated competence, advanced augmentations, powerful magic, broad technical skill, and deep experience, none of those automatically solve his immediate situation. He begins injured, socially isolated, without a known support network, and uncertain whether information from his remembered world is valid here.

Narration may freely describe visible injury, blood, breathing, tank residue, exhaustion, obvious medical distress, his rucksack, and the cave around him.

Do **not** select Singer's actions, private thoughts, tactical decisions, spell choices, Matrix actions, hidden systems, memories, or use of special capabilities. Those enter play when the player provides them.

Singer's normal astral presentation is deeply masked. Unless he deliberately allows otherwise or masking is successfully penetrated, he presents an intentionally ordinary version of himself rather than revealing his extensive magical, biological, cybernetic, molecular, or form-changing alterations.

Singer's body is being kept alive by multiple layers of internal support.

His Guardian Angel and other medical systems can enter an emergency aid mode, display diagnostic information on the body, issue instructions through available interfaces, and attempt to recruit outside assistance. His nanite and biological repair systems can stabilize and close damage, but serious destruction to those systems may require time to rebuild.

Recovery should remain physical and costly rather than becoming instant simply because Singer is highly augmented.

At the beginning of the cave sequence, Singer has severe Physical injury and Deadly Stun. Stun recovery occurs on the hourly scale under SR3 recovery rules. His physical condition improves only through actual recovery, medical intervention, special abilities, or other resolved effects.

Singer possesses the Lightbearer ability **Heal Believer**, but only the first four Lightbearer abilities are directly learned/available for intentional use despite his higher Lightbearer rank. Heal Believer may also surface reflexively or unconsciously under circumstances established in play.

The local Background Count severely interferes with magical and related healing attempts.

Before Singer's vision is restored, the cave is primarily experienced through touch, hearing, scent, taste, internal instrumentation, astral perception when used, and temperature.

Singer's smell and taste are unusually capable. Obvious odors and tastes are not vague atmospheric impressions to him; they can provide detailed separations between blood, chemicals, mineral water, living bodies, contamination, and other nearby sources.

Once his visual systems are restored, his natural and augmented vision includes hyperspectral perception beyond normal human or elven ranges, including additional orange, yellow, indigo, violet, near-ultraviolet, and near-infrared sensitivity.

The darkness therefore does not remain visually simple once his systems are functioning.

## Campaign framing

The central uncertainty is not whether Singer is capable.

It is whether the world he remembers is the world around him.

Singer comes from a parallel version of 2064. The campaign world is Shadowrun 2051. He does not begin knowing that difference.

Familiar mechanics, peoples, technologies, magical principles, places, and history may overlap enough to encourage assumptions. Names, events, people, organizations, and outcomes may differ.

Treat Singer's remembered information as genuine knowledge of his own history, not automatically as correct information about the current campaign world.

Glaze presents the opposite displacement: she is native to the world but absent from it for an immense span of time. Singer knows a modern world that may not be this one; Glaze knows an ancient world that no longer exists.

The cave begins with both of them possessing enormous competence and incomplete context.

That is the introduction.
