# Interpersonal 08 14

Historical interpersonal material from Sessions 8–14, arranged chronologically. This file preserves events, exchanges, and changes in Singer and Glaze's relationship. Repeated state summaries are retained only where they show a change in the relationship. Instructional portrayal language, future-behavior directions, and bulk descriptive material that does not record an interaction have been removed.

## Sunday, May 28, 2051 — morning and early afternoon

### Ordinary physical comfort after becoming lovers

During the night Singer became cold again, pulled the second bed's covers onto the shared bed, and continued sleeping wrapped around Glaze. In the morning he asked whether he had made her too hot.

By this point sleeping in contact, adjusting around Singer's abnormal temperature, and waking together had become ordinary rather than a new intimacy milestone.

Singer also showed Glaze the medical display visible beneath the skin of his forearm and explained more of his continuing nanite/nanosymbiote recovery. This extended the medical transparency that had begun when she helped keep him alive in the cave, but now occurred between healthy-enough lovers rather than patient and rescuer.

---

### Fertility becomes a question they intend to answer

Singer explicitly connected their sexual relationship to the unresolved question of whether they could conceive. He described accidental conception as something that could happen in a "moment of weakness" and said he intended to find someone qualified to answer the biological and magical questions.

He also stated the attraction behind the concern plainly: Glaze was **"irresistible."**

The important change was that fertility stopped being only a hypothetical conversation. Singer and Glaze wanted enough knowledge to make deliberate choices rather than discovering compatibility by accident.

During later discussion Singer disclosed that he and his late wife had spent roughly six years in fertility clinics before their son was born. He described failed treatment attempts and some of the biological uncertainties involved. Glaze learned that the present question touched an old and painful part of Singer's family history as well as their own future. When he chose not to continue the subject, she let it stop there.

---

### Glaze chooses learning before errands

Singer laid out the day's practical pressures—identity work, transportation, hotel logistics, Taetzel, and corporate matters. Glaze chose **morning lessons** first.

They spent roughly half a day on intensive English work using VR/AR goggles, then continued practical immersion while moving through waterfront shops and reading signs, labels, objects, and transactions in context.

They later watched fishing boats unload, bought fresh fish and crab with simple cooking supplies, and cooked lunch at Seacrest Park.

The day showed the teaching relationship changing further: Singer still supplied language and modern context, but Glaze increasingly chose when learning happened and used the city itself as practice rather than following a curriculum imposed on her.

## Sunday afternoon / evening, May 28, 2051

### Dr. Miriam Okafor — Glaze begins forming her own expert relationship

At the University of Washington, Singer located **Dr. Miriam Okafor, D.P.V.M., Ph.D.**, whose work overlapped awakened reproductive physiology, magical heredity, and phenotype stability.

Once the real consultation began, Singer largely yielded the conversation to Glaze and acted as translator where necessary rather than speaking for her.

Glaze questioned and judged Okafor herself. What persuaded her was not Singer's endorsement but Okafor's willingness to distinguish evidence from speculation, admit uncertainty, respect confidentiality, and discuss the science without immediately treating Singer and Glaze as specimens or identities to expose.

Their practical questions narrowed to Singer and Glaze specifically: whether conception is viable, whether Singer's altered biology creates predictable embryo or inheritance risks, and what changing form could mean if Glaze were the gestational parent.

Okafor explained that anonymized cellular samples could support an initial investigation and that harder germ-line questions would eventually require more specialized material and context. Singer and Glaze preferred compartmentalized handling rather than attaching their complete identities to the research.

This was an important autonomy step. Singer found the expert and supplied translation, but Glaze decided whether she trusted the expert and whether the work was worth continuing.

---

### Alamaise and a difference between old knowledge and modern knowledge

After the consultation Singer asked Glaze about **Alamaise**.

Glaze knew Alamaise from the Age of Dragons and could give Singer historical and political context from periods she had actually lived through. Singer, in turn, knew that an `Alamais` appeared in modern material Glaze had not seen.

The exchange reinforced an emerging intellectual pattern: Singer's modern and future-contaminated information did not automatically outrank Glaze's ancient firsthand knowledge, and Glaze's age did not automatically tell her what became of someone after her own known history ended.

---

### Burke Museum — Glaze begins using modern institutions for herself

At the Burke Museum, Glaze chose exhibits, read what English she could independently, asked Singer for terminology when she needed it, compared archaeological and zoological claims against her own experience, and collected names and citations she wanted to investigate later.

She also moderated how much she corrected a younger museum volunteer rather than turning the encounter into a demonstration of superior knowledge.

Singer told her about an old Makah friend whose language and oral-history knowledge had once forced him to reconsider parts of a museum presentation. The story reinforced the idea that institutional presentation and lived knowledge can disagree without either automatically being dismissed.

The important relationship change was that Singer was increasingly becoming a companion inside Glaze's investigation of the modern world rather than the person choosing what she would learn about it.

---

### Evening recreation

Singer and Glaze later went to a large Bellevue Megamax-trideo theater. Glaze chose an awakened-ocean exploration film, enjoyed the spectacle, criticized implausible zoology, and continued watching when Singer eventually fell asleep leaning against her.

She woke him when the credit alarm sounded.

The scene was ordinary established intimacy: they could spend time together without every shared outing being a lesson, mission, or relationship discussion.

## Monday, May 29, 2051

### Singer exposes uncertain magic without demanding that Glaze prove trust

Singer showed Glaze several unusual abilities and uncertainties, including **Infinite Warehouse**, **Astral Shift**, concern that the Warehouse might interfere with bonded foci, and uncertainty over whether Astral Shift had anything to do with his arrival in her cave.

Glaze did not accept Singer's theories merely because they were possible, and she would not risk one of her own bonded foci simply to test the Warehouse.

That refusal mattered because their growing trust was not becoming a requirement to experiment recklessly for one another. Singer could disclose uncertainty; Glaze could take it seriously without treating disclosure as a demand for proof.

---

### English teaching becomes more physically direct while remaining chosen

Singer gave Glaze intensive pronunciation instruction using articulation, playback, and practical correction. Where useful, Glaze allowed close physical guidance to the positioning of her mouth and tongue.

The larger change continued to be Singer reducing unnecessary intervention. He still supplied vocabulary and correction, but increasingly allowed Glaze to work through speech and ordinary interactions herself.

---

### Renraku Arcology — shared cover becomes play

Inside the Renraku Arcology, Singer and Glaze jointly performed the cover of an affluent Tir elven couple. The cover worked because they could coordinate public affection and status signals without either treating the role as their actual hierarchy.

Singer leaned into the role of the decorative romantic companion while Glaze shopped. Their teasing included:

> Singer: "You are quite lovely hun."

Glaze answered in elaborate ancient Sperethiel:

> "You say this as though you have only just noticed."

After Singer returned from Matrix work and carried the bags, Glaze told him:

> "You appear very well trained. I may keep you."

The joke worked because neither treated it as literal ownership.

---

### Singer gives Glaze a functional electronic identity

The Debbie Harry identity had become functional enough for Glaze to begin having her own electronic life. Singer used it to initialize personal communications and data devices, then hardened them against casually exposing one persistent signature.

His summary was:

> "Now they will lie for you."

This continued a pattern already begun with the credstick: Singer used his expertise to give Glaze independent modern access rather than making himself the permanent intermediary through which she had to act.

---

### Face work — Singer identifies a modern use for expertise Glaze already has

Singer explained the shadowrunner **Face** role and told Glaze she would be very good at it.

Glaze recognized the work as management of people through information, positioning, trust, access, timing, observation, and misdirection. She did not regard herself as socially inexperienced; what she lacked was current slang, references, and rapidly changing social context.

Singer agreed with that distinction. He could teach changing context without pretending to be the source of Glaze's underlying social ability.

---

### "I like libraries. And librarians."

Glaze strongly liked the Downtown Library and the librarian **Mara Velez**. Singer deliberately let Mara and Glaze continue talking and exploring without inserting himself into every exchange.

Glaze's interest was not merely in books. She liked the library as a place for research, access, shelter, meetings, and simply existing without a purchase requirement.

After dinner she said:

> "I like libraries."

Then added:

> "And librarians."

Later Singer let Glaze choose the remainder of the evening. She chose to walk, selected a route with enough people to reduce pointless trouble, and then chose a small open-stage music venue because she wanted to hear what ordinary people sang when nobody important was listening.

Her curiosity was becoming increasingly self-directed.

---

### Practical preparation for reproductive research

Singer and Glaze acquired ordinary contraception and supplies suitable for eventual coded biological testing. No sample was taken or sent and no attempt to conceive occurred.

The important change was practical rather than emotional: their earlier discussion about preserving choice was becoming concrete preparation.

## Tuesday, May 30, 2051

### Taetzel becomes a possible shared home

Singer and Glaze spent substantial time considering **Taetzel** as somewhere they might actually live rather than simply property Singer remembered from another history.

Glaze liked the current building for its strength, privacy, overbuilt structure, adaptable volume, and the possibility of eventually creating covered space large enough for her full dragon form.

Singer treated her judgment as consequential. During his bargain with the Force 9 hearth spirit he said:

> "My love thinks the place is good."

When asked whether he was staying, Singer answered:

> "Probably, if I can face my own fears."

He offered the spirit:

> "For as long as I can"

and clarified:

> "Not a promise, just our bargain."

The exchange connected two important facts: Glaze's opinion mattered in deciding whether the building became home, and Singer would not make a false promise of permanence merely because the place carried deep emotional weight.

---

### Active Masking around outside magical attention

Before the Yakuza/Aztechnology-connected visitors arrived, Singer asked Glaze to **actively Mask** rather than rely only on passive concealment because both organizations could bring initiated magical assets. Glaze accepted the precaution and stayed out of view during the key meeting period.

This was a small but concrete example of tactical trust: Singer supplied a specific modern threat assessment, and Glaze chose to act on it without the precaution becoming a general authority over her visibility.

---

### Singer distinguishes his remembered Taetzel from this Taetzel

On the roof Singer and Glaze examined evidence from a fight in this timeline: missile damage, blood, bullet marks, and loose scales from **Perianwyr**.

Singer then described the Taetzel he remembered from his other history: a mixed-use stronghold and home connected to clinics, businesses, weapon facilities, neighborhood politics, his team, his late wife, and violent conflict.

Glaze initially blurred parts of that remembered history into the present building's past. Singer challenged the assumption—effectively, **none of that happened here**—and Glaze corrected herself.

The correction was important to their shared way of handling Singer's memories. They could use them for comparison without silently converting them into facts about the current world.

---

### Singer explains why Taetzel is emotionally dangerous to him

Singer described the conflict that had led to his remembered acquisition of Taetzel as a hellish week in which his entire seven-person family/team group was badly battered. He described an ex-military mentor who handled tactical and magical support and nearly lost her magic, and explained that their initiation group had included that mentor and his family.

Glaze therefore understood before the worst scene upstairs that Taetzel was tied to family, survival, violence, and loss rather than merely nostalgia for valuable property.

---

### Home planning includes Glaze's full form

Singer and Glaze explored the upper floors and discussed how the building could eventually accommodate her at dragon scale. They considered opening larger volumes, covered full-form space, roof access, structural loading, and how Glaze actually lands rather than treating a dragon as a static weight calculation.

Glaze's desire was simple in relationship terms: if Taetzel became home, she wanted a home in which she did not always have to reduce herself to human dimensions.

Singer took that requirement seriously as a design constraint rather than treating her dragon body as an inconvenience.

---

### Neighborhood as part of home

Singer told Glaze that in time **"the whole neighborhood is security"**—not as formal guards, but through ordinary occupation, people knowing who belongs, and relationships that give neighbors reason to notice when something is wrong.

They discussed local addiction and whether Glaze's social or magical influence could help people change. Glaze distinguished changing motivation and priorities from magically curing physical dependence. Her interest was in making people more functional neighbors rather than controlling the district through fear.

This was one of the first conversations in which Taetzel's surrounding community became part of their idea of home rather than only a tactical environment.

---

### "We own a building now."

Singer told Glaze:

> "We own a building now."

At this point she did not yet understand the legal share structure behind Blue Mountain Holdings. What she did understand was that Singer was talking about Taetzel as something shared rather than solely his recovered possession.

## Tuesday, May 30, 2051 — 28th-floor grief collapse and aftermath

### Singer clears the floor, then collapses

After Singer defeated the jammed stair-access maglock, Glaze saw him shift abruptly into tactical clearance. He signaled her to hold, drew so quickly she had difficulty seeing the transition, used UV light and Physical Mask while moving, stopped breathing during the clear, and checked for automation, chemicals, mines, and hidden threats. Even to Glaze's exceptional reactions, he moved as a blur.

Only tens of seconds later, Singer slowed, sat on the floor, and began wailing and sobbing.

His perfect memories overlaid the current rooms with the family life he remembered from another history. Glaze could not read those private memories directly, but his controlled astral presentation destabilized enough that she saw changing self-images associated with them: a younger human Singer with the woman who later became his wife; Singer holding their screaming infant son after surgery; Singer undergoing a painful transformation while holding his family; and further impressions from the life he had lost.

His physical condition deteriorated with the grief. His blood pressure dropped, his strength failed, and he eventually went slack in Glaze's hands. At the lowest point he desired death and held on tenuously. For roughly fifteen minutes he could not articulate himself.

When he could finally speak, Singer apologized:

> "Sorry.. You Had to see this.. you don't deserve to deal with my pain"

Glaze rejected his attempt to decide what she deserved to witness. She had chosen to be his lover while already knowing his grief existed. Her position became:

> "I am not carrying your pain; I am beside you while you carry it."

She did not claim the grief, minimize it, or present herself as its cure.

Singer eventually took her hands, stood with her, moved to the dusty seats, and said:

> "Thank you for being with me."

He then slept for roughly three hours and twenty minutes.

This was the clearest point yet at which their relationship had to contain Singer's dead wife, son, and lost family life without turning Glaze into a caretaker or forcing Singer to hide the grief from her.

---

### Making a home after the collapse

Singer woke around **16:41**. Glaze did not force an emotional debrief. They returned to food, cleaning, choosing rooms, restoring systems, making the bed, showering, and other ordinary work.

Glaze chose a large bedroom with better windows and enough space. When Singer asked for help making the California King bed, the interaction was mundane domestic cooperation rather than a symbolic declaration.

While looking through accessible material, Glaze distinguished looking from searching or forcing locks. Even in property they now controlled, she did not treat other people's sealed possessions as automatically theirs to violate.

Singer later explained what entering the floor had felt like:

> "It Felt like my families tomb when I first entered it, photographic memory is a curse."

That gave Glaze a direct explanation for what she had seen. Singer's perfect memory could make the lost life emotionally immediate rather than safely historical.

That night Singer slept deeply beside her while his Masking remained unstable enough that his astral self-image continued shifting through older forms. By morning Taetzel had begun to become a lived-in home without ceasing to be a place saturated with Singer's grief.

## Wednesday, May 31, 2051 — morning

### Ordinary intimacy resumes without erasing the collapse

The morning did not begin with a required grief discussion. Singer and Glaze woke affectionate, became sexually intimate by mutual participation, and then returned to ordinary life when outside concerns intruded.

Singer told her:

> "Yes, every morning I wake in your arms is good."

Glaze answered:

> "I intend to give you many opportunities to test it."

Singer later proposed:

> "I think we should spend this morning wastefully."

The importance was the return of ordinary affection after the previous day's collapse. The grief remained real without consuming every subsequent interaction.

### Morgan — Glaze separates Singer's history from present evidence

When surveillance around Taetzel raised questions about **Kyle Morgan**, Glaze treated the observers as information rather than proof of a specific theory.

Singer explained pieces of his parallel history involving Morgan, including a later confrontation and Morgan's changed skills. Glaze did not treat those memories as automatic evidence for what present-world Morgan had done.

Singer also explained the role of a **Mr. Johnson** as an intermediary purchasing deniability through shadow assets. Glaze reduced the problem to a principle she could use:

> "There is a difference between disposable and harmless."

The exchange showed her modern social understanding becoming less dependent on memorizing Singer's conclusions; she was increasingly building her own models from the structures he explained.

### Recovered tools and another grant of independent resources

Singer let Glaze choose among recovered modern electronics rather than assigning her a training kit. She selected tools useful for learning how voice masking, maglock defeat, tracking, recording, and surveillance worked, leaving behind equipment that was not useful to her body or current skills.

After cleaning the selected equipment, Singer gave it to her along with a **50,000¥ certified credstick**. Another **75,000¥** was set aside as **"Rent and supplies."**

As with the earlier 8,864¥ gift, Singer was increasing Glaze's ability to act independently rather than making his expertise or wallet the only route through modern life.

### Perianwyr's scale

Among Morgan's private possessions was an old dark scale. Singer identified it by scent as **Perianwyr's**.

Glaze knew Morgan and Perianwyr had a long personal/professional association, so the object plainly carried some significance, but neither of them established exactly how Morgan obtained it or what it meant. The item remained evidence rather than a story they invented to fill the gap.

## Wednesday, May 31, 2051 — afternoon / evening

### Hair, teasing, and comfortable domesticity

Singer cut his rapidly growing white hair short and kept his beard after taking Glaze's opinion seriously. When he offered Glaze the cut hair or proposed burning it, she accepted it as potentially useful ritual material and joked that she would try not to summon him from the dead without asking.

Singer later carried her to the kitchen and they ate lunch.

The exchange is useful history because it shows how ordinary their intimacy had become: magic, bodily material, flirtation, and domestic routine could coexist without solemnity.

---

### Glaze begins occupying Taetzel's neighborhood in her own right

When local people came asking for work, Singer offered bounded exterior labor. Glaze chose to go down among them rather than remain upstairs while Singer handled the interaction.

She helped feed workers, talked with them, watched how they behaved, evaluated what people could actually do, and reinforced the household's privacy. She made clear that the food was for people working that day, not a substitute wage or open promise of future employment.

Glaze independently identified **Raymond Cole** as worth further attention because his claims about contaminated water and pumps were bounded and realistic, including what he admitted he could not solve. After Singer later discussed the technical work and hiring arrangement with Cole, Glaze remained willing to have him return.

This was a further change from her first Redmond outing. She was no longer only studying how a neighborhood worked; she was becoming a recognizable participant in the neighborhood around their home.

---

### The evening walk — Singer leaves social space open

Singer and Glaze walked the block around Taetzel while he inspected fence problems and remapped the area. Singer was quieter than usual and did not pull Glaze away from stalls, stores, or people when something caught her interest.

He joked about the local shorthand for the building:

> "Blue Mountain after we wash the place down."

The earlier Rake/library pattern was now appearing at home: Singer could remain present and highly alert without making himself the social center of Glaze's interaction with everyone around them.

### Attraction inside Singer's constant situational awareness

During the walk Singer explained how his systems could map and remember persistent environmental patterns even when his conscious attention was elsewhere.

He framed the explanation with direct flirtation:

> "Yes I was watching your butt, but you're asking about awareness..."

and said his systems could remain alert even if he was more interested in:

> "a beautiful woman that might want to sleep with me tonight."

By now Glaze understood Singer's divided technical attention well enough that his constant surveillance capability did not automatically read as emotional distraction from her.

### Awakened children reveal more of Singer's own history

Singer asked whether Glaze had noticed Awakened people in the neighborhood and then focused on children, telling her:

> "It's hard to be a kid with magic out here."

He described the lack of training and the danger to children who manifest abilities without support.

The conversation became personal when Singer spoke about an old mentor:

> "my mentor, um he ... he, still bothers me"

He described being told about a society where magically active people were trained very young, contrasted it with the current world, and said:

> "This world is doing a bad job at it"

He added that **2011 was pretty harsh**, that he had learned quickly, and that he had taught his family.

Glaze therefore learned that Singer's concern about unsupported Awakened children was tied to his own early magical education, his family, and a mentor who still affected him emotionally.

### Singer tells Glaze his age

During a lighter age discussion Singer told Glaze:

> "I'm 95 now but elven genes are much kinder to aging than human."

They also discussed changing human lifespans and ork/troll use of **"Breeders"** as an insult for humans. Singer mentioned that he had once told the swordsman's ghost that term and the ghost had laughed and agreed.

The exchange gave Glaze a concrete age for Singer rather than merely a sense that his history was unusually long by modern metahuman standards.

### Urdesh — the identity mismatch finally becomes clear

Singer again wished he could tell Glaze the forgotten swordsman's name. He wondered whether the swordsman might have lived during an era when Glaze was awake and mentioned fragments of Fourth-Age material the man had discussed.

Then Singer made the point that broke Glaze's previous understanding:

> "I know the only name he gave me was his, so naming things won't help since I lost even that."

Until this statement, Glaze had understood **Urdesh the Carnivore** and Singer's unnamed metaplanar/Horror-associated swordsman as two different people. She had not knowingly possessed the name Singer was trying to recover.

At this point she recognizes that Singer has been talking about **Urdesh**.

The GM correction attached to this scene is controlling: Glaze does **not** casually say the recovered name out in public and trigger Singer's established reaction on the street. She takes him home before giving him the name.

The detailed recitation of Urdesh's life and its aftermath is not included here because it had not yet been roleplayed in this Sessions 8–14 source. This file ends with the misunderstanding resolved and the name ready to be restored privately.
