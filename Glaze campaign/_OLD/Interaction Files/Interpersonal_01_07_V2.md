# Interpersonal 01 07

Historical interpersonal material from Sessions 1–7, arranged chronologically. This file preserves events, exchanges, and changes in Singer and Glaze's relationship. Older state summaries are retained only when they show a change in the relationship. Instructional portrayal language, future-behavior directions, and bulk descriptive material that does not record an interaction have been removed.

## Session 1 — the cave

### First meeting

Singer arrived inside Glaze's sealed sleeping chamber catastrophically injured, bleeding, nearly unconscious, initially unable to see normally, carrying his military rucksack and debris from the medical facility he had escaped. His arrival was extraordinary because the chamber should not have been physically reachable.

Glaze was already nearing wakefulness as the ambient mana rose; Singer's impossible arrival completed that awakening. His Masking had collapsed badly enough during transit that she saw him unusually exposed: immense hidden power, a very different astral self from his physical appearance, profound grief, and a man close to death without panicking over it.

Her first response was curiosity rather than fear.

---

### Singer's first words

Singer's first spoken words to Glaze were:

> "oh...you are beautiful... but no, I am trying to gain control before I do... I don't have time to hallucinate angels coming to take me home."

He curled into a fetal position and quietly sang a modified modern-elven **Caoineadh na dTrí Mhuire**.

Other early lines included:

> "My angel is still here."

> "you even smell nice, I hope that a good thing?"

> "I still can't see you, so I assume you aren't real if not for the warmth"

Singer sent a telepathic:

> "Thank you"

When Glaze could reasonably have taken that as thanks for helping him survive, Singer clarified:

> "Not for the aid, but for being here."

He also told her:

> "my enemies succeeded at eliminating me."

and asked:

> "Can you tell me where we are?"

Even while nearly nonfunctional, Singer was trying to understand the cave, hostile mana, his damaged vision, and whether there was a route out. He also asked what he could do for Glaze.

The important early shift was that Singer responded to the enormous dragon in the sealed chamber as a person and possible companion before he was in any condition to demonstrate power over the situation. Glaze in turn encountered his vulnerability before she understood the scale of what he could do.

---

### Medical trust and first physical contact

Glaze spent the first hours helping Singer survive. His autonomous medical systems instructed her in Elvish on how to hold wounds together, position tissue, apply pressure, and support the body's repair systems. She followed the instructions despite not understanding the technology.

Singer rebuilt his visual systems gradually, subsystem by subsystem. While he was still cold, feverish, and weak, he repeatedly rested against Glaze for warmth. She adjusted her body so he could lean against her without being pinned or injured.

Singer later approached her head and placed a hand on her. Glaze allowed the contact.

Their first physical closeness therefore began while Singer was badly injured and dependent on her cooperation, but it remained voluntary as he recovered. It was not founded on dominance by either of them.

---

### Grief in the cave

Singer's grief for his wife and child was visible almost immediately through tears, songs, statements about what he had lost, and the collapse of his normal emotional/astral concealment.

Glaze did not treat the grief as weakness. Singer's thanks for her presence rather than her aid became one of the first things that moved him, in her view, from an extraordinary intruder toward someone she personally liked.

---

### Masking, secrecy, and choice

Singer at one point bypassed or saw through Glaze's Masking and apologized. He explained that he was extremely practiced at Masking and at slipping past protections.

Glaze distinguished between rudely ignoring a protection and successfully **passing** it. She did not regard those as the same act.

Singer also explained that he habitually concealed his true astral nature, magical capacity, augmentations, biological changes, and other capabilities. The principle that emerged in their conversation was:

**secrets preserve choice.**

Once other people know what someone can do, fears, loves, or knows, they can begin planning around that information. This became an early compatibility point between them rather than a demand for total disclosure.

---

### Names

Singer told Glaze that his personal name is **Brian**, but that his institutional identity had effectively been lost and **Singer** was the name that still functioned through reputation. Glaze accepted Brian as a real name even if the surrounding world did not recognize it, but generally used Singer because that was the functioning identity he presented.

Glaze first gave him the accessible name **Glaze**, then later her name in her own Dragon Tongue voice.

Singer asked for it:

> "in your own voice, not the echoes of others."

Using his voice-duplication system, acoustic analysis, and extended vocal range, he tried to reproduce it rather than reducing it to a convenient metahuman approximation. His first attempt was recognizable but imperfect. Glaze corrected him, and he kept working at the difficult harmonics until he could speak it much more carefully. He later spoke the name and bowed to her.

The exchange established that Singer regarded her actual identity as worth the effort of learning rather than something to simplify for his convenience.

---

### Reciprocal protection

Singer told Glaze that he had repeatedly been used by powerful people because threats to family, community, nation, civilization, or survival could compel him to act. If someone convinced him that something genuinely needed protecting, that could become leverage over him.

Glaze identified the danger as **who gets to define the threat**. She told him:

> "That is where I would guard you, if you permitted it."

Singer answered:

> "let me be the guard, princess Glaze."

This was the first explicit reciprocal-protector exchange between them. Singer's protection tended toward physical capability, information, warning, and options; Glaze had already begun recognizing that Singer's autonomy itself could need protection from manipulation.

"Princess Glaze" began here as a private teasing title rather than a political claim.

---

### Singer gives Glaze independent money

Singer gave Glaze a certified credstick containing **8,864¥**, explained its approximate practical value, and made clear it was hers to use.

When Glaze recognized that it represented a substantial portion of his anonymous liquid resources, Singer told her:

> "I won't say I won't miss having it, but I'd miss you not having it more."

The gift mattered because it increased Glaze's independent ability to act in the modern world instead of making her more dependent on Singer.

---

### Early teaching becomes companionship

During the cave period Singer became Glaze's primary bridge into the modern world. He explained unfamiliar systems by starting from things she already understood, distinguished what he knew from what he suspected, and let her questions control where explanation went.

Glaze was herself an ancient teacher and noticed that Singer did not need a student to feel ignorant in order to demonstrate expertise. His teaching therefore became part of why she enjoyed his company rather than merely a service she needed from him.

By the end of the cave-only period they were no longer strangers. The relationship was still new and undefined, but it had become warm, teasing, physically comfortable, intellectually engaged, and increasingly protective in both directions.

## Session 2 — emergence into the modern world

### From emergency trust to ordinary companionship

Once they left the cave-only situation, Singer and Glaze traveled together, met strangers, built a cover story, shared a room by mutual preference, handled ordinary domestic routines together, ate socially, and continued teaching and translating.

The relationship changed because it survived outside the emergency that created it. Glaze increasingly thought of Singer as someone she was **with**, not merely the wounded stranger who had appeared in her chamber.

Her earlier impression of him as simultaneously **companion, guide, and problem** remained, but the companion part became much stronger.

---

### Singer asks Glaze to question him more

Singer explicitly encouraged Glaze to ask increasingly detailed questions rather than remain a passive student. Her questions shifted from basic identification toward system-level reasoning: who actually has authority to bypass a lock, how service access differs from security, how infrastructure regulates heat and water, how media framing changes interpretation, and what programs truly understand.

That changed the teaching relationship from simple explanation toward dialogue. Glaze was increasingly using Singer as a source to interrogate rather than an oracle to follow.

---

### Lodge archive work and Singer's computational scale

During lodge Matrix/archive work, Glaze saw Singer reorganize large bodies of information extremely quickly, use multiple agents in parallel, build cross-references, translate material for her, and distinguish stronger from weaker evidence.

At first she credited too much agency to the software. Singer corrected her with a principle that she retained:

**Programs aid. Machines enable. Skill and adaptability are human.**

The exchange mattered less as a technology lesson than as another piece of Glaze learning how Singer understood himself: powerful tools were extensions of judgment rather than replacements for it.

She also learned that Singer could become physically still while doing intense internal or Matrix work, and that his pauses did not necessarily mean inattention.

---

### Music, joy, and grief outside the cave

Glaze saw Singer use music for play, teaching, humor, emotional expression, and self-regulation.

His unmasked, tearful mountain performance of **"Walking on Sunshine"** was emotionally different from later lodge karaoke and joke songs. At the lodge he could genuinely laugh and play through bright music, then still remain deeply grief-stricken afterward.

When the cheerful music ended, Singer simply asked:

> "Are you ready for dinner?"

Glaze accepted the transition rather than forcing an explanation. She learned that Singer's joy could be real without cancelling his grief, and his grief could remain real without making every pleasant moment false.

---

### Shared room and ordinary bodily trust

Singer asked whether Glaze wanted a separate room. She chose a shared room.

They learned hotel routines together: bathroom use, showering, towels, dressing, hair, and ordinary privacy became practical rather than inherently intimate. Singer cleaned up after himself, left supplies ready for Glaze, and helped with her clothing and hair when she accepted the help.

A private joke about **"plumbing adequacy"** grew out of those mundane lessons.

Singer also refused to casually alter Glaze's ancient original clothing because it might be irreplaceable, historically important, or magical. Modern clothes could be experimented with; her surviving possessions from the old world were treated differently.

---

### Public partnership and friendly disrespect

On the mountain and at the lodge, Glaze watched Singer operate socially among ordinary modern people: approaching strangers, helping people, gathering information conversationally, shifting between languages, and using humor to normalize his own oddity.

At dinner Singer joked with **Marek**, an ork, and **Jory**, a troll, about his impossible appetite and an invented happy tapeworm "passenger." Glaze saw that accepted mockery could function as inclusion rather than hostility.

This was part of her first lived experience that Singer could guide her through modern social life without permanently speaking for her.

---

### Shared uncertainty about Singer's remembered history

Singer did not insist that his remembered 2064 history must be the truth of this world. The missing Schwartzkopf event gave them a concrete discrepancy, and Singer preferred leaving the cause unresolved rather than inventing certainty.

Glaze came to treat his remembered history as **valid evidence from somewhere else, not proof of what is true here**.

That shared habit of separating memory, evidence, inference, and uncertainty became one of their strongest intellectual compatibilities.

---

## Session 3 — companionship becomes the dominant frame

There was less new relationship structure in this session than in the surrounding ones. The important change was consolidation: Glaze's view of Singer shifted further from "guide and problem" toward **companion**.

They were now comfortable putting extraordinary material beside mundane life—parallel history, dragons, Matrix work, languages and magic alongside dinner, clothes, showers, jokes, and room logistics. Their relationship was becoming meaningful not only because of crisis or discovery but because they genuinely liked ordinary time together.

## May 24, 2051

### Explicit affection before sex

Singer kissed Glaze on the forehead and told her:

> "Thank you for making me have a good day."

and:

> "I enjoyed being with you."

Glaze answered that she enjoyed being with **him**, not merely the city, lessons, or novelty, and rested her forehead against his.

Singer later said directly that he enjoyed her company and joked that he could probably sleep beside "angelic beauty."

This was an important shift: attraction was acknowledged and warmly reciprocated, but they had not yet defined the relationship sexually.

---

### Sleeping as a physical anchor

Singer asked Glaze to use him as a body pillow because close physical contact might reduce his sleep-thrashing. Glaze agreed and slept closely against him.

Singer also asked her not to make him ashamed of the way he had clung to her when feverish or delirious. Glaze did not regard that behavior as shameful.

During a Horror-linked nightmare or memory, Singer reacted violently enough in his sleep that Glaze woke and held him more firmly. When he partially woke, he looked at her once, hid his face against her chest, and wept and shuddered until he fell asleep again.

Glaze stayed with him without demanding an explanation.

---

### "You have me"

Singer's grief later focused on the lost name of an ork swordsman whose skill he had retained. He remembered the face but not the name and believed that the name was what mattered.

His anger turned inward. He embraced Glaze too firmly, thanked her for supporting him, and described himself as acting like a child with no one else.

Glaze rejected the idea that grief made him childish and told him:

> "You have me."

This was the clearest personal commitment Glaze had made to him so far. She was no longer merely present because circumstance had put them together; she was telling Singer he could count her as someone in his life.

---

### Glaze speaks about her own losses

Singer asked Glaze whether losing everything she had known before caused her pain.

Glaze said yes, but distinguished her experience from his. She had expected mortal acquaintances and civilizations to vanish across immense periods of sleep; expectation did not erase attachment. She still remembered faces, places, students, and eras that might now survive only in memory.

Singer therefore learned that Glaze's ancient composure was not an absence of grief.

---

### White-light weapon and another care episode

Glaze saw Singer reproduce the white-light weapon effect from his Horror-linked memory while awake. The blade produced a clean, focused white magical effect and cut ordinary metal with almost no resistance, but the demonstration exhausted him badly and left a strong astral residue.

While Singer slept, Glaze examined the residue without disturbing it. When he later asked whether she had looked, she told him she had and offered to examine it more carefully.

Singer's strength had failed badly after the manifestation and a low-blood-pressure warning appeared. Glaze caught and lowered him safely, reduced excess bedding while he was running hot, moved the knife aside, stayed awake, and monitored him until he recovered.

This repeated the pattern from the cave: Glaze could care for Singer when he was physically compromised without their relationship becoming organized around permanent caretaker and patient roles.

---

### Glaze's growing autonomy in ordinary life

During May 24 Glaze increasingly acted through her own choices. She chose hotel arrangements, handled payment, participated in negotiations, chose clothing, controlled the pace of nightlife, chose foods from curiosity, and decided how she wanted to approach English study.

Singer supported this by translating or supplying missing vocabulary without automatically taking the conversation back from her.

The relationship was moving away from the practical necessity of Singer doing everything modern on Glaze's behalf.

## May 25, 2051

### Becoming lovers

Glaze had spent her prior established life celibate, but she was not ignorant of attraction, desire, courtship, intimacy, rivals, or political relationships.

Affectionate teasing around showering led into massage; the massage became reciprocal and increasingly intimate. Singer explicitly stated that his regard for Glaze mattered more than satisfying his libido and that desire could be set aside. Glaze indicated that she would say if she objected.

Singer wondered aloud how her lips tasted. Glaze initiated their first deliberate kiss and told him:

> "That is a much better use of curiosity."

After further reciprocal kissing Singer asked:

> "I ask consent to be your lover, angel of the blessed blue skies."

Glaze answered:

> "Yes, Singer. Be my lover."

Sexual intimacy followed with explicit communication and consent. The aftermath was warm, playful, and unawkward. Glaze joked that the experience had been considerably better than arbitration.

The change was substantial because it grew out of an already-established pattern of trust, companionship, attraction, and respect rather than rescue debt or dependence on Singer for the modern world.

---

### Fertility becomes a shared uncertainty

After they became lovers, Singer asked whether Glaze's human form could conceive with him and whether pregnancy would be desirable.

Neither knew whether conception was biologically or magically possible between Glaze's dragon-origin human form and Singer's heavily altered metahuman biology.

Glaze's position was that possibility and probability should be understood before accident made the choice for them. She could not honestly classify the possibility as simply undesirable, but she did not make a decision about wanting children.

Singer told her the answer would not change what he thought of her; he wanted knowledge for planning. Glaze believed him.

---

### Glaze sees Singer in the city rather than only as her guide

During their first full Seattle day, Glaze watched Singer interact with people when he was not focused solely on teaching her. He sang with a street guitarist and quietly gave the performer useful food coupons, helped an elderly woman with a walker and shopping bags, and repeatedly noticed emerging problems before Glaze had fully identified them.

Glaze asked him to leave room for **cheap mistakes** because harmless mistakes could prevent more dangerous ones later. This became part of how she wanted Singer to help her adapt: provide information without eliminating every opportunity to learn independently.

---

### Darnell Washington and the organlegger crew

Glaze witnessed Singer respond to the attempted abduction of **Darnell Washington**, a drugged ork being forced toward a van by a fake-medical organlegger crew.

Singer recognized the danger, put himself between Glaze and the obvious weapon threat, directed Glaze toward the coordinator, disabled an augmented handler, rescued Darnell, contained and questioned the crew, gathered evidence, provided medical triage, and remained until public EMS and Lone Star arrived.

Glaze used **Influence** on the coordinator at Singer's direction.

The incident gave Glaze a more complete view of Singer than the cave or social outings had: he could use sudden severe violence, move immediately from violence to treatment and reassurance, manipulate modern systems effectively, and deliberately refrain from using every predatory advantage available to him.

---

### Puget Sound as date and classroom

Singer and Glaze took an electric sightseeing/dinner skimmer around Elliott Bay and Puget Sound. The trip mixed practical learning with an ordinary date. Glaze increasingly followed the guide and other passengers herself rather than receiving everything through Singer, and particularly liked seeing Seattle from the water because the city's shape became easier to understand.

Singer summoned the water spirit **Sgáan**, politely asked whether nearby sea life might choose to visit, changed his astral presentation, and sang an ultrasonic welcome. Harbor porpoises approached without coercion.

Singer explained that animals and paracritters often reacted badly to his aura even through Masking and said he was grateful Glaze had not reacted that way.

Glaze distinguished between Singer being **frightening** and Glaze being **frightened of Singer**. They were not the same thing to her.

---

### Vancouver Island and Singer's family dead

Singer mentioned visiting his grandparents' graves on Vancouver Island and became calm, distant, and sad.

Glaze told him they could go when he wanted. She did not turn the disclosure into a larger conversation he had not asked for.

---

### The mountain spirit

When Glaze's former cave became a public magical curiosity and possible talislegging site, Singer consulted the powerful mountain spirit associated with the domain.

After the spirit reported astral and physical intrusions, Singer asked it to remove an artificial watcher and close the exposed cave entrance. He then gave the spirit **7 Good Karma**, released every remaining service or claim, and told it:

> "You may be free of my interference."

He added:

> "Travel with my blessing, do not harm needlessly, protect your domain, but be a bear not a fire."

The spirit became a powerful free spirit, took the mountain as its domain, removed the watcher, and closed the entrance without deliberately killing the intruders.

Glaze observed the exchange. Singer later said he preferred gifts made by his own hands. Glaze told him she understood him better afterward.

---

### Thinking about a shared home

Singer said he needed work and a better place for them to stay. Their discussion established that Glaze cared much more about space, security, privacy, a useful kitchen, room for magic, room to become larger, practical access to the city, and an easy route outward than she did about prestige, servants, or ornamental luxury.

The important relationship change was the distinction between temporary lodging and somewhere that could feel like **theirs while they were there**.

## May 26, 2051

### Morning after becoming lovers

Singer and Glaze remained comfortable rather than becoming awkward after sex. The following morning included showering, teasing, practical cleanup, and consensual exploration of how Glaze's transformed and hybrid anatomy differed from a human body.

Glaze explained aspects of support structure, scales, scale beds, and horn-root sensitivity. The relationship point was that Singer asked and observed rather than assuming, while Glaze was comfortable explaining her body directly and humorously.

---

### English as intimacy and independence

Singer continued intensive English teaching through food, media, real conversations, demonstrations, and direct feedback. Glaze increasingly used English because she wanted independent function rather than because Singer demanded it.

Her English reached **Rating 2** during this period. Singer also taught her the practical communication boundary that telepathy did not itself travel through phones, radio, or the Matrix, while modern devices could translate writing and speech with varying delay and ambiguity.

One of Glaze's own summaries of the learning process was:

> "I did not know all words. I did not need all words."

---

### Redmond — Glaze studies how people actually live

The Redmond outing mattered because Glaze was not merely shown poverty or gang territory. She actively questioned **Tomas**, tested explanations, watched who watched the street, and built her own model of informal infrastructure: quiet ownership, local eyes versus formal surveillance, repair and power as social glue, shadow economies as infrastructure, and why ruined places can still be people's kingdoms.

Singer sometimes became introspective during the outing. Glaze continued learning and talking to Tomas instead of making his mood the center of the scene.

---

### Taetzal becomes Glaze's choice too

Taetzal was emotionally complicated for Singer because he had owned and transformed a version of the building in his original history. The present building contained recognizable events arranged into a life that had never included him. Singer described the experience as wrongness, divergence, and **jamais vu**.

Glaze reframed his memory as **comparison, not prophecy**.

Singer then explicitly asked Glaze to decide whether they should pursue the building. He told her that he did not need Taetzal, that he had first thought of it because it could give her room to be herself, and that they could choose another path if the building was rushed or wrong for her.

Glaze gave a clear **yes** for her own reasons: space, privacy, vehicle access, security potential, and room to exist without constantly pretending to be small. She also set conditions—walk away if contamination could not be controlled, the structure was unsound, or the title carried obligations they could not control.

Taetzal therefore became a shared choice rather than Singer simply reproducing his lost home.

---

### Glaze handles the fixer conversation

Singer told Glaze he wanted her to practice dealing with shadow agents and fixers and let her choose how to approach **Dillon Harmon** about identity work.

Glaze led the important conversation. Singer supplied difficult English terms when needed but did not take it over. She established the need for an identity capable of surviving banking, property, and government scrutiny and independently asked which identity maker burned clients before choosing the safer route.

After Singer's immediate business ended, Glaze kept talking to Dillon herself about what fixers actually sell, what makes a good contact or bad client, what fixers refuse, and why reliability can matter more than brilliance.

This was a concrete autonomy milestone: Singer could remain the technical expert without being required as Glaze's social intermediary.

### The Rake — Glaze leads the night

After the business portion of the evening, Singer deliberately stopped teaching and working the room. Glaze led; Singer followed.

She chose where they moved, when they danced, which conversations she opened, when she returned to Singer, and when the night was over.

When another clubgoer joked that Singer looked "claimed," Glaze answered:

> "He is with me."

The line was public certainty about their relationship rather than literal ownership.

Singer later told Glaze he wanted the only scent on her to be his. Glaze teased him for being possessive without rejecting the intimacy. When Singer asked whether he was allowed to be proud of being her lover, Glaze distinguished pride from treating her like a prize: she was not a trophy; **she was his lover**.

---

### Singer performs for Glaze

Singer joined the Rake's DJs for a live vocal passage and aimed the performance at Glaze. He moved through multiple vocal ranges and projected the love song's emotional content astrally toward her.

Glaze's immediate response was:

> "That was not fair."

then:

> "Do it again sometime."

Singer thanked the DJs and openly said he needed to impress his love. Glaze pulled him back onto the dance floor, told him it had worked, and told him to stop performing for everyone else and dance with her.

---

### "You let me have the room"

For the rest of the club night Singer stayed focused on Glaze without making himself the center of her social interactions. He followed her lead, danced with her, kissed her when she closed the distance, and remained nearby while she spoke with other people.

On the way back Glaze told him:

> "You let me have the room."

She liked that he could be highly capable and charismatic without needing every shared social space to become his.

---

### End of the night

Singer and Glaze left the Rake at closing after hours of dancing, laughing, and talking. After the long drive back, Singer carried Glaze into the hotel and asked whether she had enjoyed the night.

She specifically named the fixers, dancing, DJs, English, Singer singing at her, and Singer letting her lead.

They settled into bed together. The important endpoint was comfortable domestic intimacy rather than another dramatic declaration.

## May 27, 2051

### Glaze chooses the day

Singer explicitly handed the day's agenda to Glaze. She handled housekeeping and breakfast herself in English, selected the Space Needle, made the reservation, and later chose how long they stayed at the carnival and Big Rhino.

Singer used leading questions and demonstrations when she needed help rather than reflexively solving each ordinary problem for her.

This extended the Rake lesson into daytime life: Glaze could organize a shared day, and Singer could genuinely be the companion rather than the manager.

---

### Singer's introversion and time as a gift

At the carnival Singer told Glaze that, despite appearing highly social, he was normally introverted when off work. He described his earlier work pattern as potentially **19–20 hours a day**, with overlapping electronic, physical, social, Matrix, and magical activity.

He told her there was never enough time, that Glaze deserved his time, and that he would rather give it to her than endlessly feed the struggle.

Glaze understood his attention as something he was actively choosing to spend with her, not simply idle time left over after work.

---

### Singer lets himself be seen

Singer told Glaze that before recently he rarely traveled without some mixture of disguise, Masking, invisibility, spirit Concealment, computer obscuration, camera destruction, or drone hijacking.

With Glaze he had hidden himself much less and told her:

> "If you are not hidden, neither am I."

Glaze liked the intimacy of the choice while rejecting the idea that it obligated Singer never to hide again. The importance was voluntary visibility: someone who had spent years preventing outsiders from assembling all the pieces of him was allowing Glaze to see more of those pieces.

---

### Family support without dependence

Singer clarified that when he said he would support Glaze, he meant **like family**. If she did not work, he would. He said his ambition was not centered on money for its own sake, but that for her he would "move mountains, even if slowly."

Glaze accepted the security without accepting dependence. She did not want a future desire to work answered with "you don't need the money." Work could matter because she wanted identity, influence, learning, purpose, or simply the work itself.

She floated possible directions of her own: teaching, languages, magical work later, enchanting once she understood modern markets, and consultation.

This was the first explicit articulation that modern life should eventually contain things that were **hers**, not merely things she did alongside Singer.

---

### Shared-home requirements become more concrete

Singer explained how modern housing prices reflected security, infrastructure, schools, transit, space, comfort, entertainment, and location.

Glaze refined what she actually wanted from a home: privacy, room for possessions, working space for both of them, control over who entered as a guest, practical access to the city, and somewhere that did not become impossible when she chose full dragon form.

Taetzal remained attractive because it could solve those unusual requirements, not merely because Singer remembered owning it in another history.

A joking hypothetical about future children and Singer becoming unbearable about schools remained a joke rather than an established plan.

---

### Prejudice, Big Rhino, and ordinary play

Singer explained metatype slurs, stereotypes, the Night of Rage, and the difference between cultural teasing and genuine hostility.

At the Big Rhino, Glaze watched Singer answer an actually prejudiced troll with humor and an arm-wrestling wager. Singer won and then waived the promised payment. The troll remained anti-elf while developing personal respect for Singer, giving Glaze another example that prejudice and individual relationships could coexist messily rather than collapsing into one simple reaction.

Glaze enjoyed the Rhino because it centered ork and troll scale and informal customs rather than merely tolerating them.

Her appetite also became shared comedy rather than embarrassment. At karaoke she discovered an activity where competence was not the point and deliberately asked Singer not to rescue her pronunciation unless she accidentally insulted someone's mother.

---

### Dillon and professional reciprocity

Singer explained that Dillon's successful work made him more reliable as a contact but that they should also ensure Dillon had real opportunities to profit so the relationship remained reciprocal.

Glaze agreed while rejecting invented debt. The principle was not "reward him because we owe him friendship" but "use him for work where his professional role genuinely earns money."

Singer's later software sale and reseller arrangement gave Glaze a practical example of maintaining a fixer relationship through mutual utility rather than fear, patronage, or false intimacy.

---

### VR — Glaze sees another part of Singer's life

Singer brought Glaze into his forest-glade VR meeting space and taught her virtual movement and information manipulation in Sperethiel.

Glaze experienced that VR space was not physical geography, that movement could be metaphorical or discontinuous, and that virtual architecture could organize information rather than represent real distance. She also understood the practical similarity between Matrix immersion and astral projection: attention could be somewhere else while the body remained physically vulnerable.

Singer demonstrated a high-end competitive game and understated his hardware rather than exposing the full truth of his cyberware. His response speed was so extreme the game interpreted it as cheating.

Glaze wanted to try trodes eventually; Singer wanted to verify they were safe for her first.

The relational change was not that Glaze became technically dependent on Singer. She had now been invited into another environment in which Singer lived naturally, and the experience became another route toward her own eventual independence.

---

### End of Session 7

After VR they returned fully to the hotel room, moved naturally into intimacy, and slept together.

By the end of Session 7, the relationship had progressed through distinct stages rather than simply arriving at a static "current state":

- Singer first met Glaze while nearly dead and emotionally exposed.
- Glaze helped him survive before she understood the scale of his power.
- Singer thanked her for presence rather than rescue.
- They learned each other's names, boundaries, humor, grief, and habits.
- Emergency trust became ordinary companionship outside the cave.
- Singer increasingly gave Glaze tools and room rather than making himself necessary.
- Glaze moved from being his ancient host and student of modernity to being his chosen companion, reciprocal protector, and increasingly independent social actor.
- Affection became explicit before sex.
- On May 25 they deliberately became lovers.
- On May 26 Glaze led the Rake night and named Singer publicly as being with her; Singer deliberately let her have the social room.
- On May 27 Glaze increasingly set the shared agenda while Singer accepted being passenger, companion, and supporter.
- Singer began allowing Glaze to see more of a life he had spent years concealing.
- Glaze accepted family-like support without giving up the desire for work, projects, and choices of her own.

The durable historical point at this stage is not merely that they were lovers. It is that the relationship was growing toward intimacy **without either person's competence, protection, money, knowledge, or vulnerability becoming a claim of ownership over the other**.
