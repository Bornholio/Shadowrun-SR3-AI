# Major Gangs — Seattle, 2050

**Source basis:** GM-supplied scans from the *Seattle Sourcebook*.  
**Campaign use:** Shadowrun Third Edition campaign set in 2051. This is a period reference for major Seattle go-gangs and street gangs. Current campaign continuity overrides sourcebook state where play has changed it.

# Go-Gangs

Go-gangs are motorcycle gangs operating along Seattle's highways and streets.

Typical sourcebook characteristics:
- roughly **50–100 members**;
- motorcycles modified for intimidation and combat;
- many bikes carry a mounted slug-throwing weapon;
- territorial claims often follow stretches of highway or major road;
- gang territory is described as a claimed **"patch of crete"**;
- inter-gang disputes may become running highway battles known as **"crash-and-burn fests"**;
- many Seattle go-gangs have working ties to either the **Mafia** or **Yakuza** and may perform intimidation or killing for them;
- those ties are often transactional rather than deeply loyal.

## Major highway gangs described in prose

The prose specifically identifies five major go-gangs:
- **Red Rovers** — predominantly Ork; northern Intercity 5.
- **Eye-Fivers** — Human; central/downtown stretch of Intercity 5.
- **Spike Wheels** — mostly Trolls; Tacoma/Fort Lewis area.
- **Leather Devils** — Human; Intercity 90.
- **405 Hellhounds** — Human; Intercity 405.

## Ancients

The **Ancients** are an Elven go-gang whose location is listed as **Varies**.

The prose distinguishes them from normal territorial highway gangs: they do not claim a conventional fixed territory, instead appearing against rivals and disappearing again.

## Blood Rumblers

The printed gang table lists the **Blood Rumblers** as a Human go-gang on **Intercity 9**, colors **Red and Blue**.

**Source discrepancy:** the accompanying prose says there are five major go-gangs and then names five groups, omitting the Blood Rumblers and treating the Ancients separately. Preserve the table entry rather than silently reconciling the discrepancy.

# Street Gangs

Street gangs are the pedestrian counterpart of the go-gangs.

Sourcebook baseline:
- they claim sections of city blocks called **zones**;
- a typical gang has at least **20 members**;
- the source organizes members into functional categories: **Warriors, Pounders, Breeders, and Thieves**;
- the text says these categories are based on role rather than sex;
- abandoned apartment buildings are common gang hideouts;
- the source estimates **almost 300 known street gangs in Seattle**;
- most are concentrated in the **Redmond** and **Puyallup Barrens**, though gangs exist throughout the metroplex.

The role terminology, especially **Breeders**, is sourcebook language and should be treated as period gang-culture description rather than a narrator-wide social category.

# Redmond Conflict Use

For 2051 Redmond, the four sourcebook-established major street gangs are:

1. **Rusted Stilettos**
2. **Crimson Crush**
3. **Brain Eaters**
4. **Red Hot Nukes**

These are period-appropriate organizations for establishing gang presence, local turf, contacts, rumors, or conflict boundaries without importing later 2060 status.

The table establishes only broad district-level location. It does **not** by itself establish exact 2051 block-by-block territory near Taetzel.

# GM Use

Use this file when:
- choosing a period-correct Seattle or Redmond gang;
- deciding whether a gang has a broad geographic reason to appear;
- establishing colors or dominant metatype;
- distinguishing go-gangs from street gangs;
- determining whether a road or district plausibly has a gang boundary;
- or checking which named gangs are explicitly present in the 2050 source material.

For exact turf, leadership, strength, weapons, contacts, or 2051 changes, retrieve a more specific source or ask the GM if the available reference does not establish the needed fact.
