# DNA/DOA — GM Conversion Notes

## Plot Synopsis
**The Setup**
Biogene Technologies, a mid-size genetic engineering firm, hires the team to run a datasteal against Aztechnology's Tacoma Research Park. Objective: pull data and physical samples, deliver both to the client for payment.

**What's Actually Being Stolen**
The Metavirus — a recombinant DNA process using a viral vector to inject a fertilized host egg with RNA and enzymes, grafting chosen traits into the developing organism. Unlike enzyme-based bacterial splicing, it scales to organisms as complex as humans and can graft multiple characteristics at once. Perfected, it lets Aztechnology manufacture custom metahuman subspecies.

**Biogene's Plan**
Biogene has located the Tacoma facility and obtained the address of Aztechnology's offsite backup system. They're running a simultaneous double hit: the team physically enters the Research Park to grab the mainframe data and a virus vector sample, while a separate decker team hits the backup system to steal or wipe the research files.

**The Hands of Five**
The Tacoma chapter of Alamos 20,000, tipped off by an informant inside Biogene. They want the virus as a purification tool — revert fertilized metahuman eggs to human, erasing all metahuman species in one generation. Their political front, the Humanis Policlub, would lobby to legalize a variant as a legitimate alternative to "the unfortunate genetic damage that can occur during pregnancy."

They lack the time and the facility's exact location to mount their own raid. Their play is to let Biogene's operation do the work, then take the prize at the handoff.

**The Job Offer**
The team meets Mr. Walker of Biogene at the Space Needle. Entry is via the city sewer system into the Tacoma Research Park — steal the data, get out. The run happens almost immediately after the meeting, leaving effectively no time for legwork.

**Into Beta Section**
The team enters the sewers roughly a kilometer out and works toward the breach point into Beta Section on the facility's lower level, dealing with sewer denizens along the way. Inside, they find an accident has already happened: an infectious variant of the Metavirus spilled about 24 hours earlier, tearing through Beta Section, killing or mutating everything it touched. Survivors began showing physical manifestations within hours; by the team's arrival, few lab workers resemble their original forms. Aztechnology has sealed Beta and the connecting Alpha Section to contain it.

**Objectives On-Site**
Two locations: a secure vault holding viral samples, and an inert data storage unit that can only be accessed via cyberdeck in its current state. Getting both means dealing with the lab's current inhabitants — sane and insane.

**The Docks**
The team heads to the drop point to meet their Biogene contact. The Hands of Five got there first and killed the contact; Alamos 20,000 heavies are waiting in ambush for whoever shows up carrying the goods.

Almost immediately an Aztechnology full-force combat team arrives in pursuit. The firefight between Alamos and Aztechnology gives the team an opening to escape into a nearby warehouse.

**The Warehouse / Ork Underground**
The warehouse was partially burned during the Night of Rage eleven years ago; Ork survivors now use it as an access point to their underground town. Ork children are scurrying in the shadows — a trigger-happy team can easily kill them. Their "baby-sitter," a Metahuman Rights Activist, leads the team to the enclave either way. Reception varies accordingly.

**Wilhem Park**
The Ork town sits in the buried shopping mall formerly known as Wilhem Park. Allan Bronston leads the Night of Rage descendants. Friendly or hostile depending on the children, he tells the team that the Aztechnology facility has been scouring nearby sewers and capturing test subjects. A few days ago a group of Ork women and children were taken — Bronston believes they're held in Alpha Section.

**Dr. Carol Owens**
Bronston also reveals that the Metavirus's original designer, Dr. Carol Owens, is detained in Alpha Section — held over her protests about the corporation's handling of the project. A second run serves three purposes: rescue the Ork captives, free Owens, and potentially recruit her for Biogene. Owens initially believes the team are Aztechnology agents in disguise.

**Second Run**
Alpha Section is untouched by the virus — meaning intelligent, sane guards and functioning security systems. Once inside, the team locates the missing Orks and Owens, who has to be persuaded to come along. Getting out is easy; staying alive is the hard part.

**Aftermath**
Biogene, Alamos 20,000, and Aztechnology are all looking for the team. A Biogene rep settles up quickly for services rendered. The other two are another matter — the team may need to lie low until the heat dies down, though their reputations should rise in the meantime.

## At Hand Over Fist — The Meet
**Read Aloud**

You're at Hand Over Fist when a nervous-looking sarariman approaches your table.

"You… eh… are requested to attend a private dinner hosted by, um… Mr. Johnson. I have a car waiting. Ah… no heavy weapons, please."

Declining the invitation, or its conditions, seems likely to complicate the evening. Getting into a stranger's car unarmed isn't ideal either. But you're independents — meets like this are how the work comes in, and corps generally understand that burning independent assets is bad for business.

The car is a nice set of wheels. It takes you into downtown Seattle and pulls into the underground garage near the old World's Fair Grounds, in the shadow of the Space Needle. Corporate neutral territory — a place where deals get made without anyone worrying about stray death.

The garage lighting is bright, maybe too bright. Pivoting cameras cover the main entrance and parking areas. Your guide's car has polarized windows. The ramp takes a sudden 90-degree turn; anyone taking it at speed ends up against several meters of concrete. Behind you, heavy metal grates slide into place at the entrance — proof against even a GMC Banshee.

When the car stops, electronic sensors extrude from wall panels to examine the vehicle. Your driver codes in an electronic key and feeds it to one of them. The security computers approve the vehicle ID, and you can almost hear safeties clicking back on across whatever weapons are covering you.

The car pulls up a short ramp into an electrical recharge slot beside a bank of elevators. Your guide directs you to the lifts. More devices, more codes, and you pass through Plasisteel™ doorways.

Up you go. The pressurized doors whoosh open onto another lobby — thick cream carpeting, beige walls, and an Elven maître d' in formal black. This is one of the foyers to the Eye of the Needle Restaurant, eatery of choice for high-powered mages.

The Elf's innocent-looking podium and light probably contain more electronics than a medium-sized office. He looks almost effeminate, but the hidden eyes watching this room will not tolerate violence.

Your guide indicates you should follow the Elf into another lift, silver and black. The Elf proves a fount of information that dries up immediately after he gives his name: Cripps. A brief ride, and the elevator opens at the very peak of the Needle. Far below, the multi-colored lights of Seattle.

Several small, intimate dining areas offer comfort, privacy, and security. You note a bank of White-Noise Generators and a Hermetic Circle worked into the decor. Cripps informs you that any requests should be made via the service board concealed within the table.

"Your host will be here momentarily."

**Behind the Scenes**
The team has been contacted by Wendell Holmes, assistant to Jason Walker. Walker is Assistant Director of Special Operations for the Seattle branch of Biogene Technologies.

Security at the Eye of the Needle is very high. The GM makes an 8-dice Perception check against the Concealability of any weapon carried to see whether the foyer security system detects it. Anyone flagged is politely asked to check the weapon before entering the elevator. Anyone refusing is invited to wait in the foyer while the rest of the team goes in to dinner.

Aggressive action will not be tolerated in the Space Needle. Arguing with Cripps about surrendering weapons brings a warning first, then the Needle's security team. That team normally consists of eight heavily armed Corporate Security Guards (Partial Heavy Armor, Clubs, Tasers, Uzi IIIs), a Company Man, a Street Mage, and a Wage Mage. They arrive within one minute of any disturbance. During that time, security doors seal and the elevators serving the area shut down. The team enters from multiple directions and seeks to neutralize rather than kill.

The dining area has a Rating 10 White-Noise Generator and a Rating 8 Hermetic Circle — no eavesdropping, no astral beings.

Walker needs the team's talents for a datasnatch from an Aztechnology research facility in Tacoma. Holding the meeting in the Needle's revolving restaurant is meant to impress with wealth and reach. Walker is not a fool, however. By spreading nuyen liberally, he's confident no one but Holmes and Cripps has "seen" the team, and he's ensured that electronic surveillance "forgets" they were in the Space Needle at all.

From the moment the team got into the car, Walker has been watching how they conduct themselves. Are they discreet? Can they hold a confidence? Do they handle themselves as well in corporate surroundings as in the field? The GM should note the team's conduct during the trip and adjust the offered salary accordingly.

**Debugging**
The main purpose of this encounter is to set mood, so it shouldn't suffer major bugs. The team should come away impressed less by Mr. Johnson's credit balance than by the fact that this is a big-time operation. This is the bright side of Seattle — opulence and power, the upper crust covering the wounded shadows.

The initial contact can happen anywhere. Holmes can approach team members individually, or the group's fixer can arrange the meet — in that case, simply pick up the action with the drive to the Space Needle.

If the team refuses to meet a new Mr. Johnson, one or more of their Contacts can let it be known on the street that their rep is suffering for not even hearing out a straightforward business proposal. Have these operators turned into *shaikujin* overnight?

## The Briefing — Mr. Johnson's Pitch
**Read Aloud**

"You understand that anything I say to you must remain within this room, yes? If any word of this operation leaks out into the streets, we'll know who to blame.

"The Aztechnology corporation has been working on a project that we're very interested in. It is a biotechnology project, but nothing you need be concerned about. It is harmless, unless administered properly. We wish a sample of the project and the complete file on it from their computer.

"Some arrangements have already been made for the run. You will get in through a semi-abandoned section of the Tacoma sewer system that runs adjacent to the Aztechnology facility. Based on information we've been able to obtain, the ventilation/heating duct system for the facility runs only a meter or so from the sewer wall. It should be a simple matter to break through the brickwork there and get access to the ducts. There is, however, an extra wall, made of Plastisteel-7™, surrounding the entire facility to prevent exactly this kind of break-in. Fortunately, we have been able to obtain an instability catalyst that should weaken the material sufficiently for you to get through.

"The product sample will be somewhere in the laboratory section that you'll be entering. Look for the analysis labs or storage rooms. The data file will, of course, be somewhere in the computer system, but we do not know whether or not it is isolated. We do know that Aztechnology had some of their specialists in to work on the system when it was installed, which probably means it will take some work on your part. We also know that in the last day or so Aztechnology isolated the two internal systems servicing the laboratory to prevent access from an external node. All access will have to be from within the facility itself.

"Once you have completed the run, make your way to the wharves and rendezvous with the *S.S. Misha*, a container vessel docked at Wharf Number 114. Get there before daybreak. Your contact will be waiting for you on the ship. He is an Elf, tall and thin, with black hair. When he asks you if the sky is still blue, tell him that it hasn't been since you were a child. Give him the sample and the data file. He will pay you.

"Being evening, and the weekend, there should be few people at either the Aztech facility or at the wharf. I leave to your discretion what you should do about anyone you chance to meet.

"Hmm? Oh, didn't I tell you? We go *now*."

**Behind the Scenes**
As far as Walker is concerned, this part of the run is as straightforward as he portrays it. At no time will he mention that he works for Biogene, or that Biogene is sponsoring the run. Nor will he mention his name or that of Holmes. He's seen too many runs self-destruct because a Mr. Johnson let something slip. He does not intend to make that mistake.

Walker is also mute on the fact that the Aztechnology bioengineering project is a virus.

Because Biogene has learned that Aztechnology plans to move the research to a more secure facility in Aztlan, the run is scheduled for this very night. If Biogene does not strike now, they could completely lose their chance to obtain a sample of the virus.

The corp is offering 120,000¥ as payment, with the team deciding among themselves how to split it. As a gesture of goodwill, the corp will pay 60,000¥ in advance. The remainder is due on delivery of the virus and the data file. If the team attempts to negotiate with Walker, use the Social Skill Use procedure found on page 153 of the **Shadowrun** basic rules.

Should the team request back-up, Walker replies that he doubts it could be arranged at this late date, but that he will try. Ultimately, no back-up will be available, due more to lack of time than Biogene's unwillingness to cooperate.

Walker will, however, be able to offer the services of two other operatives — the GM may choose from the pregenerated characters in the **Cast of Characters** section. The team has the option of completely refusing this additional assistance, which will *not* be offered again. If one or more operatives are accepted, they will expect and demand full-payment shares.

After negotiations and briefing are complete, Walker provides vehicles to transport each character home. He also provides a source of equipment. The team is given only enough time to get home, grab their gear, and be driven to another meeting spot. During that time they may attempt to gain information from their Contacts, but their escort — two Street Samurai — will not tolerate unnecessary delays. Consult the **Legwork** section for what information might be available at this point.

Walker has access to an L-R Transport Mini-Bus filled with gear. At the GM's discretion, the team may only be allowed to draw equipment from it and not their respective stashes. All firearms are fitted with smartlink adapters. Available:
- Four (4) AK-97 Assault Rifles (w/10 clips each)
- Four (4) AK-97 SMG/Carbines (w/10 clips each)
- Two (2) AK-98 Assault Rifles (w/10 clips each)
- Two (2) High-Explosive Missiles, in single-shot tube launchers
- Ten (10) Mini-grenades: 4 Offensive, 4 Defensive, 2 Concussion
- Four (4) Regular grenades: 2 Offensive, 1 Defensive, 1 Concussion
- Eight (8) Armor Jackets (2 troll-sized)
- Eight (8) Rating 6 Chemsuits (2 troll-sized)
- Eight (8) Rating 6 wireless, scrambled, two-way micro-transceiver headsets
- Two (2) Rating 6 Maglock Passkeys
- Shovels and picks
- Eight (8) kilograms of BCE-4 plastic explosive and 2 timers
- Two (2) catalyst sprayers (2 uses each)

In addition, Walker will volunteer Holmes (but will not name him) to drive the mini-bus and drop the team near the sewer entrance. He will also provide a partial map of the section of the Tacoma sewer system they'll be traveling through. The team's version of this map is included at the back of this book; the GM's map is in **Down Under**.

**Debugging**
The worst that could go wrong here is that the team might, for whatever reason, refuse to go along with Walker. If so, Walker will first attempt to convince them. Failing that, he takes a different tack — his voice and demeanor grow cold as he looks them over one last time. If they still refuse, he reaches into his pocket, removes a small microtransceiver, and says: "This is Johnson. Dispatch Team Two." He then puts the transceiver away and walks off. If the team does not stop him and agree to the mission before Walker reaches the elevators, Biogene's offer is withdrawn. Following Walker's departure, Cripps will enter and insist they all leave immediately.

Who knows what kind of word Walker will put out on the street about this team? Nor does it make them feel any better when one of them later meets up with a friend who took part in a recent run on Aztechnology. It went like clockwork, and the operators involved made a lot of money — including a handsome bonus from their unnamed employer.

## Scouting
**Behind the Scenes**
If the team has their wits about them, now is a good time for some scouting. There won't be much time to check with Contacts, but one or more characters may decide to do some scouting of the Matrix or some Astral scouting.

There is definitely no time for physical scouting.

**Matrix Scouting**
Aztechnology's Tacoma Research Park does not have its own connections to the telecom grid, but works through the main system in the Aztechnology Pyramid in Seattle. If a decker wants to investigate the lab computer system from the outside, they will have to penetrate through nearly the *entire* Aztechnology Seattle computer system — all 38+ security levels, including the quasi-mythical Level 30 where all the prototype Black IC gets tested. No one has ever done it and lived long enough, or come out in good enough condition, to tell about it.

Obviously, this direction is not recommended, and the decker should be smart enough to realize it.

**Astral Scouting**
If there is one thing Aztechnology does better than computers, it's magic. If an astrally scouting Mage approaches the general area of the Aztechnology facility, they will be set upon by three Rating 8 Earth Elementals if coming in underground, or two pairs of Rating 8 Fire and Air Elementals if approaching in any other manner. The Elementals will "strafe" the scouting Mage as a warning. If the Mage does not flee, they attack.

If the conflict lasts more than four turns, the original Elementals will be joined by another identical group. At this point, the Mage can assume that the magical guardians of Aztechnology are aware of their presence and are, no doubt, at that very moment preparing to come after them.

## Down Under — The Tacoma Sewers
**Read Aloud**

The mini-bus ride through the darkness is cold and uneventful. The driver says nothing, but glances back at you frequently, his large, round eyes darting from the mirror to the road and back again. You, too, are silent, listening to the eerie outside noises that leak into the nearly soundproof vehicle.

Finally the bus stops and the driver motions you to get out. He follows moments later, pausing only to activate a sophisticated alarm system. From the look of things, you are in the midst of an industrial park somewhere in Tacoma. Without full moonlight or obvious landmarks, it's hard to tell exactly where.

The driver takes you back behind some buildings and down a small stairway. At the bottom is a rusted, closed grating leading to another set of stairs. A foul, rotted smell seeps up from far below. It takes most of you to loosen the grating and break it free of the rust.

The driver flashes you a half-hearted smile before handing you a hand-drawn map of the sewers. He steps aside and watches as you descend carefully, one by one, into the darkness. He closes the grating behind you.

It's far worse down here than you hoped, but only a little worse than you'd expected. Small animals scurry violently about in their rush to avoid you. Touching the walls, you feel them damp with various kinds of evil muck. Underfoot, the ground feels dry in some places, then knee-deep in something vaguely passing for water in others. The foul odor almost takes your breath away. It smells like many things have died down here. Or will.

**Behind the Scenes**
Welcome to Tacoma, or at least its sewers. The section the team is moving through is part of the old system, abandoned some 30 years ago and replaced by a new storm system. This network of tunnels is still useful as a catch for runoff and as an overflow vent for the new system, but mostly it just collects debris — natural, Human, and otherwise.

The odor is staggeringly brutal. Though the team may be repulsed, they will not have problems breathing after a bit. Only the most sensitive will have difficulties, and then only if the GM is in a particularly vile mood.

The GM's map of the Tacoma Sewer System is accurate in showing a cave-in or two that will make passage less direct than the team's own map would indicate. These obstacles should prove only minor inconveniences — the team will have no problem locating an alternate route, even though it is not marked on the team map.

Though the sewers themselves offer only a temporary obstacle, the denizens are another matter. For every separate section of sewer, be it passageway or intersection, roll 2D6. On a result of 9 or higher, an Encounter occurs. Roll 3D6 against the Sewer Encounter Table.

*\*Street Gang:* One of the Gang Members present is the leader. Normally a Gang Leader Archetype, but roll 1D6 — on a 5 he is a Street Samurai; on a 6, a Street Mage.

For all other encounters, use the statistics for the appropriate Critter or Average Metahuman, per the tables on page 191–2 of the **Shadowrun** rules. All applicable skills are at Rating 2.

**Light and Navigation**
Visibility in the sewer tunnels is restricted. Without external light, nothing is visible — not even to a character with low-light eyes. (Low-light works through light amplification; *some* light must be present.) A flashlight illuminates 20 meters in a narrow area. An area-style camping light illuminates up to 10 meters all around.

At each intersection there is a 3 in 6 chance of coming upon a manhole opening into the streets. In any other section, the chance is 1 in 6.

**Toxin Exposure**
Regardless of whether the team wears the chemsuits provided by Biogene, they will be subject to minor physical effects from contact with sewer toxins. These show up as rashes and blotchiness over the body.

Each character makes a **Body Test against TN 10**. Chemsuit rating applies as a TN reduction, as do any applicable disease resistance modifiers. Baseline coverage is 80 percent of the body; **each success eliminates 15 percent** of the affected area.

The team should be led to believe the effects could be much worse. Note that the effects will only surface 1D6 days *after* the completion of the entire **DNA/DOA** adventure — leaving the team more inclined to blame possible contamination during their visits to the Aztechnology labs. Very few things get characters squirming faster than the threat of BIO-HAZARD.

**Through the Wall**
Once the team reaches the appropriate tunnel section, they must decide *how* to get through the wall. The tunnel is Normal Concrete (Barrier Rating 8). The team may attempt magic or explosives, but they should have considered this ahead of time.

Beyond the tunnel wall is the Plastisteel-7™ wall surrounding the primary lab area — off-white plastic with an almost mirror-like gloss, some 30 centimeters thick, Barrier Rating 35, and virtually impervious to any form of attack. The team should have the catalyst agent provided by Biogene. Contained in a small, palm-sized canister, the catalyst is in aerosol form and must be sprayed directly on the material. The spray covers roughly 4 square meters (4m x 4m). Each canister contains enough for two uses.

Once applied, the catalyst turns the Plastisteel-7™ bright pink. After a moment the material begins to bubble slightly, then steam, then lose its luster. At this point the catalyst has done its work, and the plastisteel takes on the consistency of chewed bubble gum. To enter the ventilation duct system, the team will have to push their way through the wall section — and it will look and feel exactly as though they are crawling through a wall of bubble gum.

By reaching through, they can locate the ventilation duct by feel. The energy given off by the chemical reaction has damaged the duct enough to melt its plastic walls. The team can crawl from the tunnel directly through the destabilized wall and into the ventilation duct.

Once through, the wall seals up again behind them, retaining the bubble-gum consistency for one hour. In this state it has a Barrier Rating of 16 against high-impact, high-energy damage such as firearms, projectile weapons, and explosives. Against low-energy attacks — simply trying to walk through it, or magic — it has a Barrier Rating of 1.

After passing through, the team exits covered in sticky, membranous sections of wall. They can spend about five minutes removing it. If they don't, the substance hardens again in one hour, reducing Strength and Quickness by 4 each. Reaction Rating and Dice Pools must be recalculated accordingly.

Once through the wall, the team should quickly begin to realize that something is very wrong in the lab.

**Debugging**
There is little that can go wrong here unless random chance runs against the team, making sewer encounters come so fast and furious that it jeopardizes their ability to complete the run. The GM should be wary of overdoing the encounters. The real action takes place immediately after the team enters the Aztechnology lab — they'll have been expecting strange things in the sewers, but certainly not in the lab.

Movement in the sewers should be restricted to the local area, i.e., this side of the Aztechnology facility. If the team somehow ends up exploring or gets lost, take pains not to let them wander into the area occupied by the hidden Ork town.

The GM must also remember that this is an *abandoned* sewer, not the monorail station during rush hour.

**Tacoma Sewers — Team Map**
A single main east-west trunk line runs from the team entrance (southwest) to the Aztechnology Facility (east). Several short north-south spurs branch off the trunk. A diagonal tunnel cuts across the western end near the entrance. Access point **B** — Beta Section — sits at the far east end where the trunk meets the facility's western boundary.

## Beta Section
**Read Aloud**

Compared to the sewers, the ventilation duct is a sanitary paradise. The duct is clean and only slightly damp from condensed moisture, but the mass of pipes and bundles of fiber-optic cable running throughout leave little room to maneuver. The only way through is to crawl… slowly.

Eventually you begin to notice a dim, pulsing, red-tinted light illuminating the duct. You have reached a grating, one that has been burst open from the inside, from *your* side. Moving carefully, you slide your body forward and look beyond it.

Amber light panels positioned intermittently around the room, just below the ceiling, flash continuously. The room itself is in shambles, as though a miniature whirlwind or other destructive force had passed through. Debris litters the floor, and the far wall is splattered with an ominous dark stain.

The door leading into the room is ajar. Suddenly, you catch a glimpse of movement, a quick darting away of something just beyond the door. You think it may have been a man. Maybe.

**Behind the Scenes**
What has happened here is the Metavirus IV accident, which has turned Beta Section into a war zone. The rampant killing has subsided, with the various survivors having staked out their own "territories." Food is scarce, so anything wandering too far from home is fair game. That includes the intruding team.

The large ventilation ducts can access Beta Section at only a few points. Beta-Room 3, the rooms adjoining 9, and 10c-d all have connections to the large ducts. All other non-isolation lab areas are serviced by small ducts.

The only illumination in Beta Section is from the flashing amber lights. Unknown to the team, these signal that a biohazard spill or accident has occurred. Major doorways — the access to Alpha Section from Beta-Area 1, the door to Beta-Area 6, and the door to Beta-Area 9 — are flanked by a pair of twin security lights of a normal color.

### Security Notes
Aztechnology officials are not concerned about the personnel in Beta Section, because "The Tailor" (Dr. Carol Owens) is safe in Alpha Section. She can easily duplicate her research, plus there are back-up copies of her research files hidden away elsewhere. (Unknown to Aztechnology, Biogene has a hidden agent at the back-up file site who will be stealing the file and erasing the master copy at the same time the team is making their run on the Tacoma facility.)

Neither is Peterhoff of much concern, because his part of the virus development is done, as witnessed by the current state of the lab. What does concern them is how well the various Humans, Metahumans, and virally altered creatures are faring in a self-contained competitive environment. They could not have devised a better "survivability" test if they had tried.

To that end, they are periodically checking on the status of the inhabitants of Beta Section. They were originally using the security cameras, which were slaved to both Alpha and Beta Sections' security processors. After damage to the Beta processor left the entire system shorted out, they called in William Blount, an apprentice Hermetic Mage from their parazoological research department. For the past eight hours, the Mage has been astrally surveying Beta Section and relaying his sightings back to the corporation.

### William Blount (Aztechnology Security Mage)
Born in the UCAS, Bill Blount grew up on a farm in Iowa, but went on to earn a master's degree in parazoological magical studies from the University of San Diego. He was recruited by Aztechnology just prior to achieving his degree. Blount had been working in the Aztechnology parazoological research division for six months when the Tacoma Research lab sent its emergency call for a skilled field observer. He leaped at the chance and has not regretted the opportunity.

**Detection**
For every turn (roughly three seconds) the team is in Beta Section, there is a chance the Mage will spot them. Roll 2D6 every turn; on a result of 4 or less, they have been spotted. At that point Blount returns to his body and reports intruders in Beta Section.

*Note:* If a team Magician goes astral while in Beta Section, he is immediately set upon by at least two Fire Elementals (Rating 8) on security patrol. If the Magician has Spirits of any kind tagging along, Elemental or Nature, they are also subject to immediate attack. The Elementals have been instructed to patrol the facility and drive off astral intruders. It is standard procedure for them *not* to report intruders, because there are so many false alarms. The Mage on security duty is aware when an Elemental is destroyed, and Aztechnology considers that fair warning of active magical intrusion.

**Response**
It takes some time for Blount to convince his superiors that intruders have, in fact, penetrated the isolated, sealed, impregnable Beta Section. When he finally does succeed, his bosses spring into action.

They send Blount back to the team's location, where he tells the Security Elementals not to interfere during the initial conflict. He cannot do much more in the way of commanding them, for they are not his. Aztechnology policy is to pull the Security Elementals back into a second line of defense behind the security Mage.

Though ill-suited for active combat, Blount will begin to assault any Magician characters present. Being in Astral Space, he cannot affect the other Magician directly. He can, however, affect any Elementals or Spirits the Mage may have. Any magical items the Mage is carrying have a presence in Astral Space, so Blount can affect them too. He will first try to eliminate any Elementals or Spirits present, assuming the Security Elementals haven't taken care of them. He will next try to destroy any high-powered magical items the Magician may be carrying, such as a Power Focus. (A better tactic would be to destroy as many low-powered items as possible, thereby inflicting as much damage as possible.)

Unless a Spirit or Elemental has been instructed to report, a controlling Magician will not know one has been destroyed until after the fact. He *will* immediately know when any of his magic items are under attack. His alternatives are to stay out of Astral and take the attack, or to go astral and defend his items. At the point the Magician goes astral, Blount engages him directly.

The Security Elementals remain separate from the fight unless Blount orders them to attack, which he will not have the head to do. Even after Blount has been removed from the fight, the Elementals remain passive, for they have not been instructed otherwise.

At this point, the team should realize they have been discovered and time is of the essence. At that very moment, Aztechnology is dispatching a Response Team to the area. The team arrives at the facility, evaluates the situation, and concludes the intruders must have entered Beta Section from the sewers. They then begin to deploy in the Tacoma sewers in an effort to intercept. By that time, the team will already be on their way to the Tacoma docks and clear of the sewers.

This does not mean the chase ends here. By hook or by crook, Aztechnology will learn the team is headed to the docks — through conventional means such as reports from observers, magical means such as another Mage trailing them from Astral Space, or any other means the GM deems logical. The ultimate goal is for the Aztechnology Response Team to arrive at the wharf at just the *wrong* moment.

### Accessing the Beta System Matrix
One goal of this run is for the team to acquire the Metavirus data file from the computer system. Biogene has learned the lab's computer systems are currently off-line, which means accessing the Matrix requires physical penetration of the facility. What they are not aware of is that Aztechnology sealed off the computer systems of Alpha and Beta sections following the biohazard accident, trying to ensure that none of the contaminated persons would panic and notify the outside world.

The Beta Section computer system is crashed. Physical damage to vulnerable systems within Beta Section has rendered the system useless as a whole. The individual components, however, can still function if run from a cyberdeck. The Metavirus file is located in the data storage unit in the northeast corner of Room 10, the Analysis-Experimentation Area. It requires an Electronics (3) Test to wire the cyberdeck to the data store, but then the store's secrets are there for the taking. The file, with its inoperative safeguards, is 150 Mp in size.

### Debugging
This is why you make the big bucks, chummers. If the job was easy, they'd have called on the local Elf Scout troop instead. But it wasn't, and they didn't.

There is much that could go awry at this point in the story, most of it involving one or more characters getting seriously hurt. The GM must do what he can to keep the team's romp through Beta Section from turning into a bloodbath. After all, the adventure isn't even half over.

It is important to remember there are two objectives here: obtaining the bio-sample and the Metavirus data file. The former is located in a vault in Beta-Area 12, the latter in an accessible store in Beta-Area 10. Retrieval of either one constitutes a success as far as Biogene is concerned, but both are required for a complete success.

The GM's job at this point should be to turn the team's time in Beta Section into a physical and sensory gauntlet, and to take every opportunity to embellish descriptions of the environment to set the mood. This section should be a challenge, but it is not the climax of the adventure — even though the team may at first think so.
