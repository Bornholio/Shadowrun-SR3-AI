# DNA/DOA — GM Conversion Notes, Part Two

## Out Of The Sewers
**Read Aloud**

You were hoping the trip out would be better than the trip in? No such luck. Not only does the tunnel smell just as bad, but every noise you hear might be an Aztechnology security guard just around the corner. Things have gone wrong before, but this time…

**Behind the Scenes**
The trip out of the sewers should be similar to the trip in. Roll on the Sewer Encounter Table, but again, don't overdo it. Every now and then, tell the team they catch a glimpse of something dark and fast-moving down a side tunnel. If they look, they'll find nothing. They are being watched, though.

All the activity in the sewers has alerted Allan Bronston's people that something is happening, and he has dispatched a couple of Ork scouts to find out what is going on. The most the team will glimpse is a pair of Orks in dark clothing moving through the tunnels.

When the team finally exits the tunnel at their original entry point, the mini-bus is there, but Holmes is not. Finding the vehicle's keys in the sun visor, the group gets it started with no problem. They drive immediately to the docks, as per their instructions.

**Debugging**
If the GM keeps the action flowing and the team moving, not much goes wrong in this section. The Aztechnology people are not in position to intercept the group by the time they exit the sewers, which delays that complication temporarily. Allan Bronston's Orks will only watch, but are near enough to overhear any comment concerning the relatively nearby wharves. The location of the vehicle pick-up point and the wharf meeting place is such that a particularly agile Ork could keep the mini-bus in sight while cutting through buildings and across open lots.

Also nearby to keep an eye on things are Alamos 20,000 people. They know the dock is the meeting point, and will signal the other members of their group to move in on the *S.S. Misha* once the team is on its way.

The GM's priority is to get the team out of the sewers and to the docks. It is definitely bad form to be late for one's own funeral.

## The Wharf
**Read Aloud**

You drive hard to the docks, whipping every bit of horsepower that you can from the vehicle. At the dock, the security gates are open, with no guards in sight. Biogene has been true to their word. Scanning the darkness, you spot Wharf 114 and steer toward it.

As you move past some old, burned-out warehouses, one of you realizes that this is the area Alamos 20,000 used to corral and burn thousands of Metahumans during the Night of Rage some eleven years ago. You stop here and hide in the shadows.

Peering out into the gloom, you see a rusty, old container ship, the *S.S. Misha*, looming up before you. This is it.

You move quickly and silently to the ramp leading up to the deck of the ship. You take the stairs two at a time. There, waiting for you at the top, is your contact.

Or is it?

**Tacoma Wharf Map**
Scale: one square = 10 meters. The *S.S. Misha* is berthed at the center wharf finger. Three large warehouses sit inland across the dock road, each with a front entrance facing the water.

**Behind the Scenes**
Alamos 20,000 has taken control of the container ship and killed the Biogene contact, substituting one of their own members, sans hood, who is tall and thin enough to resemble an Elf at a distance. Once the team gets within a few meters of him, they will easily see the man is not an Elf. If they do actually get close enough to speak to him, the phony contact does not know the correct pass-phrases.

Either way, within moments of the team's arrival on the vessel, the Alamos 20,000 team will reveal itself. Stepping out of the shadows are the three main Alamos-Tacoma leaders: Claude Pierce, Terry Carey, and Daniel Sinclair. They are dressed in urban fatigues and combat gear and their faces are masked, but arm-bands boldly display the Alamos 20,000 insignia.

### Alamos 20,000 Team
Use the **Merc Archetype** for Pierce and Carey. For Daniel Sinclair, use the **Corporate Security Guard** stats.

**Claude Pierce** — Gear: smart AK-97 (6 clips), smart Fichetti Security 500 (2 clips), Armor Clothing.
**Terry Carey** — Gear: Enfield AS7 (2 clips), Beretta Model 101T (3 clips), Armor Clothing. Does not have a Smartgun Link.
**Daniel Sinclair** — Gear: Uzi III SMG (4 clips), Ruger Super Warhawk (12 extra rounds), Armor Clothing.

With them, and similarly garbed, are 6 Alamos 20,000 members. Use the stats for the **Humanis Policlub Member**. All wear Armor Clothing, and each carries a Remington 750 sporting rifle (10 extra rounds).

The Alamos team demands the viral sample and the data file. They will agree to no other terms — they get the virus and data file now, or nobody walks off this deck.

Just at the moment that negotiations are reaching the critical stage or weapons are about to be fired, Aztechnology shows up. In force. Converging on the dock area is a full Aztechnology Response and Pursuit Team, which includes ground vehicles and helicopters. The works.

### Aztechnology Response and Pursuit Team
Use the **Company Man** statistics for Captain Strum. Use the **Corporate Security Guard** stats for all the rest.

**Captain H.C. Strum, Commanding Officer** — Gear: "smart" FN HAR assault rifle (Gas-Vent 2, 4 clips), "smart" Browning Max-Power heavy pistol (3 clips), Partial Heavy Armor and Helmet.
**Lieutenant Carl Hollis, Second In Command** — Gear: HK227 SMG (5 clips), Browning Max-Power heavy pistol (2 clips), Partial Heavy Armor and Helmet.
**Troopers** — Use the **Aztechnology Security Guard** stats. Gear: HK227 SMGs (3 clips), Armor Clothing and Helmet.

Arriving in dozens of vehicles and helicopters, both armed and transport, the Aztechnology Response Team is an insurmountable foe. They immediately open fire on any visible target on the deck of the vessel, be it team member or Alamos 20,000. Moments later, vanloads and carloads of Alamos 20,000 members and supporters begin to arrive to shoot it out with the Aztechnology team.

The GM should use the arrival of the overwhelmingly superior Aztechnology team to "force" the team in the direction of one of the warehouses. One way is to throw so much firepower into the local scenery that they have to dive for cover. If they choose the *S.S. Misha*, use the massive fireworks to force them away from the ship. It is only when they get to one of the warehouses that they will find any respite.

The GM is free to let the Aztechnology team destroy as much as necessary of the wharf area. This will create so much confusion that the team can escape into the nearest warehouses. Be sure, too, to position the Aztechnology forces to prevent any other exit.

**Debugging**
The likeliest outcome of the fighting is that the Aztech Response Team wipes out the Alamos 20,000 contingent to a man. It is also more than likely that the team will retain possession of the virus sample and the data file. If they have lost either, or both, they can redeem themselves by rescuing Dr. Carol Owens later on in the adventure, but say nothing about this now.

The fallout from the firefight on the wharf leaves both Aztechnology and Alamos 20,000 more than a little miffed at the team. Just how miffed will become apparent later on.

To escape, the team must use one of the warehouses. No matter which one they choose, it will be the right one.

## The Warehouse
**Read Aloud**

You hurl yourself through the semi-open warehouse doors as half a dozen assault cannon rounds split the wharf behind you. If you're lucky, the Alamos 20K jerks will throw themselves at the Aztechnology goons long enough for you to get free.

It takes all your collective strength, but you finally manage to close the battered, rusty warehouse door behind you. Just in time, too — a dozen shots cut through it, barely missing their mark. You.

The warehouse interior is dark, except for occasional bursts of light from searchlights and explosions outside. Much of it is blackened by flame and age. It must certainly be one of the warehouses used during the Night of Rage, and you flash on the thought that the victims trapped inside that night must have felt much the way you do now.

More shots hit the outside wall and some punch through to ricochet madly around the building. You move quickly, skirting debris and climbing over collapsed wall sections to penetrate deeper within. You think there may be an exit on the other side of the building.

Suddenly, a motion close by and to your right startles you. Two shapes dart past you in the darkness ahead…

**Behind the Scenes**
The warehouse is one of the infamous Metahuman holding pens from the Night of Rage. Long-abandoned, it now serves as a hidden, rarely used entranceway to the Wilhem City Ork enclave. It is also a favorite "forbidden" play area for some of the Ork children who live in the city. Two such children are playing in the warehouse when the Aztech-Alamos firefight begins outside. Unaccustomed to such displays except for tri-vid dramas, they have gathered at a window to watch. When the team explodes through the front door, the Ork children try to get away. Moving quietly, they make their way through the warehouse toward the hidden tunnel entrance. The team, being larger and less inclined to stealth at this point, makes better time.

What the team does when they spot the moving shapes is a major factor in what happens next. If they do not inadvertently shoot the children, Jenny Hernandez, a Human, will dash out of the tunnel entrance to retrieve the young Orks. Encountering the team, she promises to guide them safely through the sewer system. Instead of leading them out, however, Jenny takes them to the Wilhem City enclave.

If the team kills one or more of the children, Jenny bursts from the tunnel, cursing violently. She insists that the team help her carry the children to safety and medical attention.

The choice facing the team is whether to follow Jenny to possible salvation or else face the wrath of the Aztechnology APC that is about to come barreling through the door.

**Debugging**
Whether or not the team shoots the Ork children sets the tone for the remainder of the adventure. If the children are unharmed, Allan Bronston and his people will be friendly, even offering assistance and information on how to pay back Aztechnology and get more money from Biogene.

If the team has harmed the children, Bronston and his followers will hold them virtual prisoners. The Orks will demand that the team redeem themselves by returning to the Aztechnology facility to rescue a group of Orks held captive in Alpha Section.

This is an important encounter because it sets up the Ork plot thread. The spectacular firefight between Aztechnology and Alamos out on the docks is merely a dramatic backdrop to events unfolding in the warehouse and then underground.

If the team is foolish enough to stand and fight against Aztechnology, they richly deserve what is coming to them.

## Wilhem Park
**Read Aloud**

Jenny leads you deeper down into a long-forgotten section of the old Tacoma sewers. The trek is long, but nowhere near as sludge-ridden as your previous passage. It almost looks as though the Orks have worked to keep it clean.

Jenny says little as she leads you quickly through the dark, nor will she respond even to the most persistent questioning. All she will say is, "You'll have to ask Allan."

You have gone some distance by now, and begin to notice activity around you. Dark and shadowy figures seem to be moving parallel to you in other tunnels. Jenny takes notice of them, but does not seem concerned.

A collapsed section of tunnel looms before you, the broken stone and shattered wood shoring completely blocking passage. As Jenny pauses here calmly, the rest of you fidget behind her. It is only seconds, however, before a portion of the collapsed area moves in one piece away from the rest, revealing a hidden opening. She leads you through, and into a different world.

As you step through the opening, you enter an enormous enclosed area lit by the flickering light from dozens of small gas lamps. Your eyes adjust, and you begin to absorb the sight of dozens of small Ork children at play in a still-functional multi-level waterfall.

Jenny leads you along the upper level past rows of stores, some of which still function as such, while others have been turned into living areas. Ork men, women, and children watch with wide-eyed interest as you pass. You realize that this entire area was once a shopping mall.

Descending to the lower level of the mall, you glimpse Humans among the Orks, and these exchange wide, welcoming smiles with you. Passing a restaurant, you catch an appetizing whiff of food cooking, and a peek inside reveals a group of Orks and Humans diligently engaged in preparing what appears to be a huge meal.

Finally, Jenny leads you to what was once a bookstore, its shelves still stacked with preserved volumes. Clustered here is a group of Ork young people, all seated around an older Ork in a large, comfortable chair. He is telling them the story of the night Tacoma burned.

*GM Note:* At this point, read (or allow the team to read for themselves) the Prologue. The older Ork recounting his memories is the leader of the enclave, Allan Bronston.

**Map Legend**
Isometric view of the buried mall. Upper level holds Hydroponics, reached by escalators. Mid-level contains Living and Schooling areas with the waterfall feature at center. Elevators connect all levels; two escalator runs link upper and lower concourses.

### Into The Flames — Prologue (Paraphrased)
*A retelling for GM use. Read aloud, summarize, or hand over as background.*

An old Ork — old by the standards of his listeners, which means barely past thirty-five — settles his bulk into a chair in front of a room full of silent young people. To be an Ork, he tells them, is to know your own particular history. And knowing it means remembering the ones who came before and suffered. Their story, he says, belongs to the listeners as much as to him. He can still see it happening as he speaks. For him it will always be yesterday.

**February 7, 2039. Tacoma.**
A cold, windy day, with a hard wind off the north. In the failing light, a young Allan Bronston made his way home down an empty street littered with blowing refuse. Trouble was already in the air — the big Humanis Policlub rally hadn't even started yet. Common sense said this was not the night to test his legal right to walk a public street. He took the back alleys instead.

He was nearly home when the amplified voice from the rally carried to him. He stopped to listen, then heard something about the "guardians of human purity" and decided he'd heard enough. The memories came anyway: Goblinization, the first transformations, the early quarantine camps. His fists clenched and his vision went white before he forced it down. A siren brought him back. Didn't people love to say an Ork out of control was a dangerous thing? He climbed a fence and kept walking.

The next few hours were quiet. The rest of the family had made it home safely. Allan lay in bed listening to the wind — a sound he'd always associated with warmth and safety — and fell asleep easily.

He never knew how long he'd been out when he heard glass breaking somewhere distant. He thought he was dreaming. Then came a low rumble, like surf or far-off thunder. Or marching feet.

The pounding on the front door woke him fully. Three or four fists, furious. Light under his door, then his father's footsteps on the stairs, the maglock releasing, strange voices. Then his mother going down. Then the voices coming up. His door opened and a light blinded him. A man's rough voice said something about city health regulations and inspection codes before moving on to another part of the house.

"Father?"
"Stay there and be quiet."
"What's going on?"

His father didn't answer, and followed the stranger down the hall.

Allan got up and nearly collided with a second man in a heavy white chemsuit, punching data into a portable identification scanner, who shoved past him as if he weren't there. Something began burning in the young Ork's chest and got hotter with every breath the man in white took. Why were they doing this? What right did they have? After all the harassment from outside, this was still their home — the place where it was supposed to stop.

His jaw shook. He stepped in behind the man in the chemsuit.

"Allan!"

His mother's panicked voice spun him around. Behind her stood two men with assault rifles up. For a fraction of a second he considered defying them — then saw his mother standing between him and the muzzles, and backed off with an audible hiss.

Outside were hundreds of Orks, Trolls, Dwarfs and Elves crowded into the cold, their homes declared health hazards. Human family members were being turned out too. Many stayed defiantly beside their Awakened relatives; others loudly denied any connection to them. Humanis supporters milled around the edges, jeering. More police kept arriving, and the mood kept souring.

As the Bronstons filed out onto the street, someone in the crowd threw an empty bottle. The shattering glass triggered a rain of debris onto the families. Allan heard a scream and saw a young girl crumpled on the pavement, blood running from a gash on her face. He gathered his two sisters in his arms. His younger brother was in their mother's arms; their father watched it all with open fear.

Then came heavy wheels from up the street. Three riot vehicles took up positions abreast, water cannon and machine guns manned. Allan noticed the guns were pointed only at the huddled Orks and their families. Ten riot police spilled from each vehicle and formed a cordon across the street.

A flat voice from inside the command vehicle announced that by authority of the Seattle Metroplex Guard, all citizens without properly authorized shelter were to report immediately to the nearest deplacement center. Violators would be detained. After a pause it repeated the order, adding that they had fifteen minutes to gather belongings, after which everything would be decontaminated and shipped out.

Allan looked to his parents. Like everyone else, they had no idea what to say or do. The voice came again: transports would take them to the docks, where the liner *Asian Princess* was waiting to sail for a deplacement center near San Francisco. Part of a nationwide resettlement program. Their cooperation was encouraged. Repeat — their cooperation was encouraged. That last phrase put ice down every Ork spine.

"What are we going to do?" Allan asked. "You know who they really want."
"Go help your mother pack."
"They can't do this!"
"They already have."

The Bronstons joined thousands of others in the forced exodus. Helicopters circled overhead; armed Metroplex guards lined the convoy routes. The docks were only minutes away, and so crowded that vehicles stacked up on one another. Orders were shouted from every direction and for a while nobody seemed to know anything. Any chance of slipping away died under the floodlights and the surveillance drone screens. Watching his family, Allan thought it wasn't fair they should suffer because of his condition — especially his younger brother and sister, who might never be afflicted at all. Then again, was it fair that he should suffer either?

An amplified voice rolled down the docks: all personnel to proceed to designated warehouses for processing. Boarding would begin shortly. By order of Governor Allenson, any Metahuman without proper identification would be detained without warrant.

Two Metroplex Guards passed in front of the Bronstons' transport, and Allan's gut turned over as he watched each man cross his arms over his chest — the Hands of Five salute. That faction inside Alamos 20,000 was believed responsible for years of terrorist attacks on Metahumans. Their presence meant this was more than a security operation. He thought about telling his father, then held his tongue. He wanted to act. He had no idea what action was even possible.

Half an hour later the family was ordered out and directed into the long line moving toward the warehouses.

"All contaminated Humans process to the left. All Orks process to the right. Families will be reunited before boarding."

Children were pulled from parents, siblings from each other. Without a word, Allan took the hand of his one afflicted sister, looked at his parents one last time, and joined his line. Passing through the warehouse door he caught a guard's eye. "Damned freaks," the guard muttered, gripping a shotgun and waiting for an excuse.

Inside, high ceilings and hardwood floors turned every sound into a din. Rows of Orks stood shoulder to shoulder under bright lights, waiting. The air smelled of dry lumber and heavy oil as more subjects were packed in. Be ready to present all forms of identification on request, they were told; the sooner processing finished, the sooner boarding could begin.

What happened next happened too fast to assign blame. A scuffle broke out among frightened Orks near the front, and the violence rippled backward. A short burst of noise — probably gunfire — and a chorus of fear went up through the room. Shouted orders vanished under screaming. A surge of bodies shoved everyone back against the wall. Another burst, then the blinding flash of a concussion grenade. The blast shook everyone and knocked out a row of ceiling lights. Smoke curled around the remaining ones, and the smell of burning wood rose. The tinderbox had caught.

In the crush, Allan lost his grip on his sister. He could see her but couldn't free his arm. He shouted; the roar swallowed it. Wall to wall, everyone was locked in place.

Within seconds the lights dimmed under black smoke. The coughing started; the heat became real. Allan watched his sister pushed further away, and forced his body toward her, fingers almost touching hers — and then one of the side doors gave way. The few who escaped created a vacuum, and his sister was swept off in the rush like a swimmer in a flood. He could only watch her face disappear into the crowd.

Flames appeared above the heads of the crowd, and the first howls of pain came with them. *This is it*, he thought. *It's over. Damn your human race.*

Unable to move or fight, smoke burning his lungs, he shut his eyes and waited.

Seconds later he felt cold air. The double doors he'd come through had been torn off their hinges by people clawing for life. Bodies were being fired out through the gap like rounds from a gun. There was still hope.

The fresh air fed the fire, and it climbed. Thousands of Orks were still trapped. Some escaped; not enough. Allan felt the heat on his back, getting closer to the opening, the flames getting closer too. The roof was burning and the middle had begun to sag. Embers rained down while Orks everywhere tore off burning clothes. The poisoned smoke started killing by the hundreds.

He was within meters of the door but the pressure from every side pinned him. So many wanted to live. So few would.

Between the smoke and the crush he couldn't breathe. One last push was all that was left, and there was no room to make it. Under his feet he felt what he thought was a metal grate. That was when everything erupted.

The next thing he knew he was on the ground breathing cool, damp air, feeling something like reborn. He got to his knees and looked up. Above him was a blackened hole crossed by smoldering beams, and past that, the stars. Around him were tunnel walls — a drainage tunnel running down from the warehouse into unknown depths. The darkness below was the answer.

Then something caught him from the side: the butt of an assault rifle. "Hold it right there, Ork, or I'll put you away for good."

Allan turned with animal fury, tore the weapon out of the guard's hands, shoved him against the wall and put the sights on him. His rage slid into a smirking, victorious grin. His finger found the trigger. "Your cooperation," he gasped, squeezing, "in this… matter is… greatly… encouraged."

"Did you kill him?" one of the young Orks asks.
"I did what I had to."
"Did you see your family again?"
"No. Never." A silence settles over the room. "Every Ork in the land lost someone close that night."
"Is that why they call it 'The Night of Rage'?"
"In part, yes."
"How many escaped in the tunnels?"
"Enough for us to start over."
"Is it because we're afraid of the Humans that we live underground?"

He thinks about it a moment, pushing himself up out of the chair. "Not afraid," the old Ork says proudly. "Different, but never, never afraid."

All along the Tacoma waterfront that night — and across the whole continent — the flames of hatred against Metahumans were fanned. Whether that first warehouse fire was deliberately set is still disputed, but within an hour Tacoma's entire downtown district was gutted. Only direct intervention by the United Corporation Council got Lone Star Security to step in and stop the spread. By then the damage was done.

The Orks who survived the Tacoma fires took refuge in the network of sewers and drains beneath the city. And so the underground town of Wilhem Park was born.

### Behind the Scenes — Wilhem Park
Jenny Hernandez has led the team to Wilhem Park, a former enclosed shopping mall buried following the Tacoma volcano eruptions of 2017. Written off as a total loss, it was left entombed under ash and rubble. A modern block of condoplexes is built directly above it. Discovered by the Orks who escaped the burning Tacoma warehouses, it has since become their home and refuge. They maintain little contact with the outside world except to stay informed. Their main fear is that someday their sanctuary will be discovered and they will be forced to leave their now-generational home.

Though a major portion of the upper level is devoted to hydroponic agriculture, Wilhem Park is not self-supporting. Small meat animals are bred in one of the former department stores and fed a nutri-grain substitute grown in hydroponics. Illumination comes from dozens of small gas-burning lights illicitly tapped into a nearby gas main.

In all, close to 400 men, women, and children live in Wilhem Park. The majority are Ork, but some are the Human offspring of the older Orks. Care is taken to educate the children and assure their literacy. Any who reach adulthood are free to leave, but only on the condition that they never reveal the existence or location of the enclave.

The group lived in relative peace until the past year, when their calm was shattered by activities at the Aztechnology Tacoma Research Park. Desperate to test Peterhoff's gene-splicing experiments, the Aztechnology bosses sent out their minions to scour the sewers for test subjects. A number of Orks vanished this way, the most recent group just before the viral accident.

Allan Bronston and his people have been aware of the team's activities ever since they first entered the Tacoma tunnels. He knows they penetrated the Aztechnology facility and got back out again. Though he is not aware of the details of the viral accident, he does know something serious has occurred.

The tone of this encounter depends on whether the team killed Ork children in the warehouse. If they did not, Bronston will be friendly and offer assistance. If they did kill any Ork young, he will demand they stage a raid on Aztechnology's Alpha Section to rescue the kidnapped Orks. He will mention that Dr. Carol Owens, aka "The Tailor," is also being held in Alpha Section. He tells the group she is the designer of the Metagene virus, but apparently wants to leave Aztechnology.

If asked, Bronston reveals that he knows of her presence from an Ork who was captured by Aztechnology but managed to escape. It was this individual who told him about the goings-on in the lab. Bronston and Owens knew each other in another era, and he cannot believe she would be responsible for something as hideous as the Metavirus. He wants to confront her personally to learn the truth, though he says nothing of this to the team. The only thing preventing him from staging his own raid has been means of entry and a fear of retribution from Aztechnology, who suspect the existence of an underground Ork colony. To date, Aztechnology has not investigated further, except for the occasional "specimen sweeps." Bronston and the others fear Aztechnology would root them out with force if they knew the enclave's exact location.

The arrival of the team presents Bronston with an unhoped-for boon. Whether he is friendly to them or not, Bronston will try to use them to do the enclave's dirty work. In exchange for their services, he will offer the team escape from the sewers and the potential for a much bigger payment from Biogene (to whom he claims he can relay a message if the team wishes). He will not mention his personal interest in Carol Owens.

**On the Orks**
The most important thing to note about the Ork enclave is that it is civilized. These are not the grimy, illiterate, brainless beings most non-Orks hear and read about. These Orks live among their families and have the same concerns as any other "unawakened." They read the same data-faxes and watch the same Urban Brawl games. Young Orks even listen to the same brain-twisting music as other adolescents.

Indeed, there is no "Ork culture" and no "Ork language." How could there be when the oldest Orks are barely 30 years old? Though many Orks claim a distinctly Ork culture or language, much diversity still exists in both areas. These various elements may one day mesh to create a culture that gives birth to a language, but not within this century.

Orks receive much of their bad press from the fact that their adolescent males tend to be more "active." Physically, Orks reach maturity at about age 12, while a Human does so at 18 or so. Though an Ork reaches an advanced state of physical development earlier, he still has the emotional maturity of a 12-year-old. (Put a 12-year-old in the body of a body-builder to imagine what happens.) A venerable, old Ork is one who has reached the ripe age of 35, and he will begin to show signs of aging at about 20 years.

If the team reveals any kind of anti-Ork prejudice, and especially if they have shot any Ork children, Bronston will go out of his way to enlighten them on what it means to be an Ork. He will give them a full tour of Wilhem Park and show them how the Orks have been able to survive, and then some. Orks are not monsters. Orks are *people*.

**Preparing the Second Run**
Bronston recommends the team make their run against Aztechnology as soon as possible. While the group partakes of a quick but satisfying (and delicious) meal, the Orks assist in preparations. Bronston will offer them a guide through the sewers to a tunnel adjoining Alpha Section. The rest is up to them.

If the team is short on ammunition, Bronston can provide about 30 rounds each of whatever type of normal ammo they want. He has no specialized ammo and nothing for weapons heavier than assault rifle.

Once the team is into the sewers, Bronston will have the pathway sealed behind them. His main concern now is the safety of Wilhem Park.

**Debugging**
One way or another, the team should head back into Aztechnology. Bronston can persuade them to go for either moral or monetary reasons. He may even resort to threatening to turn them over to Aztechnology, stuffed into plastic bags, if they refuse. Either way, get them back into Aztechnology.

The only other major problem that might surface here is that the team is so blundering that they end up having to fight their way out of Wilhem Park. In that event, they will face serious opposition from at least 20 adult Ork males equivalent to the **Ork Mercenary Archetype**. Once back in the tunnels, they will immediately be set upon by either Alamos 20,000 members or Aztechnology soldiers, both of which have been diligently looking for them.

## Alpha Section — The Second Run
**Read Aloud**

Trudging through the muck, you begin to wonder if you, too, should start thinking of these sewers as home. In the last 24 hours, you've spent more time sludging through them than most people would in a lifetime.

Fortunately, these tunnels are a little more hospitable than the last. Nothing seems to be lurking in the darkness, and the fluorescent pools of blue-green liquid seem fewer and farther between. Your Ork guide even gives you a mini-tour, describing the different events that have occurred in the various sections of sewer over the last 10 or so years.

Finally, you reach a tunnel alongside the Aztechnology Alpha Section. Your guide pulls out an ultrasonic ruler and begins to measure off sections of the wall, pausing occasionally to make cryptic markings in chalk. Finally, he seems satisfied and informs you that the place he has marked is the location of a systems junction box within the Plastisteel™ wall. Take out that box and you eliminate Alpha Section's security system and elevator controls for however long it takes their repair team to reroute the systems.

With a nod and a smile, the Ork turns back in the direction he came, promising that he will be waiting for you at the next intersection when you get out. The next moment, he has vanished into the darkness.

The wall stands before you.

**Behind the Scenes**
This time, the trip through the sewers is like a vacation. No squirming monsters or rabid squatters block the path. There is still slimy gunk everywhere, but this is, after all, a sewer.

The team should still have enough explosives and digging tools to take care of the sewer wall. If not, Allan Bronston can probably supply extra. There should also be enough spray-catalyst to take care of the Plastisteel™ wall one last time. Just beyond it, the team will see a systems junction box. To get at it, one of them will have to crawl through the wall-slime and remain half-hung in the wall while working. The box can be easily disabled with a minimum of physical force, but that would immediately alert Facility Security upstairs that something is amiss in Alpha Section. A character making an Electronics (5) Test can also disable the box without alarming security. The Alpha Section computer system is not accessible from this box.

## Return to Wilhem Park
**Read Aloud**

You arrive back at Wilhem Park through a different entryway than the first time. Your guide leads you directly to Allan Bronston. Fixing his eyes on Carol Owens, he says, "Dr. Owens, I am ashamed."

Owens seems taken aback for a moment, perhaps by her surroundings, perhaps by the Ork's tone. She says nothing.

"You had such bright dreams for our future, Carol," he continues, "yet you threw it away for a research grant." His eyes seem hard and cold, much darker than you've seen them before. The other Orks around him remain silent.

Dr. Owens straightens herself up and stares directly at Bronston. "You don't know what you're talking about."

Bronston smiles thinly. "No?" he says. "Would you like me to rattle off the mapping codes for the Metagene spiral, Carol? The sequence is a little old, so you may have updated it by now."

Her face pales, and her hands clutch reflexively at her chest. "Allan…" she says.

"You've done horrible things, Carol. I want you to give me one good reason why I shouldn't have you shot."

**Behind the Scenes**
At this point, the team will discover that many Orks have gathered around, also listening carefully to what Bronston is saying. If any of the team makes a move, at least a dozen Orks will step forward to ward off any one of them.

Dr. Owens shakes her head violently. She explains to Bronston that Aztechnology brought in Peterhoff to develop alternatives to the Metagene virus she was researching. The goal of her research had always been toward finding beneficial uses for the Metagene virus, and she strongly opposed Peterhoff's work. When she learned he was kidnapping sewer-dwellers for raw genetic material, she complained loudly to Aztechnology. Instead of disciplining Peterhoff, they responded by sending in Sanchez to intimidate her research team into cooperating. The viral accident occurred shortly after that. Beta Section was completely sealed off from Alpha, which in turn had been isolated from the remainder of the facility. At that point, Sanchez ordered Owens thrown into detention, acting on Peterhoff's word that she was somehow responsible for the accident. She remained there until rescued.

The team should be able to back up Owens on some of this, gradually convincing Bronston that she is telling the truth. If the GM wishes, he could make a Persuasion Test for Owens against Bronston, using the Social Skill Use rules and applying modifiers appropriate to how well the team supports or refutes Owens' testimony.

Remember, too, that the first part of the data file taken from Beta Section contains two files that somewhat support Owens' version of events.

Once Bronston is convinced Owens is telling the truth, he will hand over the safe-house location to the team. Owens will agree to go to Biogene because it is run by people she believes are trustworthy. Bronston will then provide a guide to lead them back to the world above ground.

He hands them a slip of paper with the address of a Biogene safe house written on it, and tells the team that he trusts Biogene not to create monsters and to use the Metagene virus to positive ends. Bronston informs them that he has been in contact with Biogene and that a Mr. Johnson will meet them at the safe house.

Just before leaving, Owens will make overtures of friendship with Bronston, which he rebuffs. Though he is letting her go, he will claim that she lost her conscience by continuing to work for Aztechnology after she knew the real state of affairs.

**Debugging**
The worst that could happen in this part of the adventure is that the team foolishly decides to take on the Orks for possession of Dr. Owens. Impress upon them how many Orks are present and that most of them seem to be armed. Some are even wearing armor.

If events begin to get out of hand, Bronston will volunteer the information that he has no intention of killing Owens, but rather wants to detain her in Wilhem Park to assist in the education of the next generation of Ork children.

Though he is fearful of what people like Aztechnology might do with the virus, he still has hopes for the good the virus might someday do for the more unfortunate of his own kind, and for other Awakened Beings.

## After Effects
If nothing else, the team will have both Aztechnology and Alamos 20,000 out for their blood by the time this adventure is over. Odds are that Aztechnology will not actually know the identities of whoever crossed them, but they will be doing their damnedest to find out. Alamos 20,000 will know the identities of the team, but events during the adventure will drive the organization further underground in Seattle for a short time. Biogene, though pleased with the team's work, will disassociate themselves from them. Once Aztechnology learns that Biogene was behind the raid, there will be enough overt and covert corporate hostility to keep both sides busy for awhile. Biogene will want to keep the team at arm's length to prevent adding to its problems with Aztechnology.

The upshot is that the team will be so hot for awhile that no one in his right mind will have anything to do with them. Runs will be few and far between, with any offers probably originating more from the lunatic fringe than normal avenues. At some point, they may decide to take a vacation. A long one.

Figure it to take a month or two for Aztechnology's and Alamos 20,000's thirst for vengeance to wind down. As it does, the team's reputation will begin to take an upswing. To those who know, they are the operators who burned Aztechnology. Remember, though, a reputation is a difficult thing to live up to.
