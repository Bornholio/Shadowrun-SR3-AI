# HF-591a — Redmond, 2050–2051 Sourcebook Supplement

**Source basis:** GM-supplied scans of the Redmond section from the *Seattle Sourcebook*.  
**Campaign use:** Shadowrun Third Edition campaign set in 2051. Use this as a period-setting supplement to HF-591, especially for Redmond organizations, neighborhoods, institutions, and conflict boundaries. Current campaign continuity always overrides sourcebook-era state where play has changed it.

## Source Reliability Rule

The source contains two different information layers.

### Main text
Material presented in the ordinary sourcebook prose is the baseline setting description for the GM. Treat it as period canon unless current campaign continuity supersedes it.

### `>>>>>[ ... ]<<<<<` shadowtalk
Bracketed Matrix comments are **not authoritative setting truth**. They may be:
- firsthand observation,
- useful rumor,
- local knowledge,
- exaggeration,
- prejudice,
- propaganda,
- misunderstanding,
- deliberate lies,
- jokes,
- atmosphere,
- or adventure hooks.

A timestamp shows when the comment was posted; it does **not** make the claim true.

A poster's Matrix handle can suggest persona, interests, social position, prejudice, or self-presentation, but it does not prove identity, metatype, affiliation, or reliability.

When a shadowtalk claim is useful to the GM, treat it as something people may believe, repeat, investigate, exploit, or react to unless the GM deliberately establishes it as fact.

## Example: Tarlan Greenbough

The first shadowtalk voice is **Tarlan Greenbough**, timestamped **2-19-51**.

The post describes abandoned buildings, dark alleys, scavengers and survivors, then repeats the line that paranoia in the Barrens is "a way of life."

What this actually establishes:
- Tarlan Greenbough posted that opinion in early 2051.
- The wording is strongly atmospheric and dramatic.
- The phrase "they say" explicitly makes part of the statement hearsay.
- The handle `Greenbough` and the overall presentation suggest a deliberately cultivated Matrix persona, possibly nature/elf-coded or romanticized, but that is inference only.
- Nothing in the post proves that Redmond residents universally live in constant paranoia or that the poster is a representative local voice.

Use it as **tone, rumor, or characterization of the speaker**, not as an objective GM rule for portraying everyone in Redmond.

# DISTRICT HISTORY AND ECONOMIC CONDITION

Redmond was once evergreen forest and rich farmland. It became a suburban district serving Bellevue and Seattle workers, then developed heavily around information gathering and distribution as the computer industry expanded.

The Crash of '29 devastated the district. Roughly eighty percent of Redmond businesses collapsed according to the source, taking much of the tax base and employment with them. Residents with resources fled, while abandoned housing and low-cost shelter attracted increasing numbers of Seattle's homeless. The result was a long-running cycle of unemployment, abandoned property, squatting, poverty, and informal or criminal economies.

By 2050 the district shows the first signs of economic improvement in almost twenty years: new business openings have begun to outnumber failures and major corporations are again showing interest. Recovery remains incomplete and uneven.

Tourists are advised to enter Redmond through organized tours rather than treating the district as ordinary Seattle.

# GEOGRAPHY AND DEMOGRAPHICS

Redmond is mostly flat, with hills toward the south. The Snoqualmie River system dominates a large portion of the district and materially affects transport and industry.

The nominal population is close to 500,000, but the source notes that a precise count is difficult because many residents have no permanent address. A plausible actual population range is given as roughly **300,000–700,000**.

The metahuman share of the population is relatively low. After the Night of Rage, many Awakened-race/metahuman residents concentrated together for mutual defense.

# GOVERNMENT AND POLICING

## Mayor Jeffrey Gasston
Mayor of Redmond in the source period. First elected in **2048**.

His administration is associated with efforts to revive Redmond economically, including support for the Redmond Center Mall. Governor Schultz has taken an interest in Gasston and has reportedly mentored him politically.

The source presents municipal corruption as a major practical problem, particularly bribery or influence by organized crime.

## Practical government reach
Formal local government has limited practical control across much of Redmond. The source describes the strip nearest Bellevue as the portion where conventional law and order is most visible.

Police presence elsewhere is sparse and intermittent.

## Police operations
Sourcebook prose establishes Redmond as sufficiently dangerous that police operations use heavier vehicles and team strength than ordinary patrol conditions. Police aviation is more common than in many other districts because helicopters can cross terrain quickly and carry heavier firepower.

Specific comments about exact patrol minimums, which vehicles are always used, or police mortality appear in shadowtalk and should be treated as claims rather than automatic mechanical rules.

# CONFLICT BOUNDARIES AND ORGANIZATIONS

This section is the main reason to load HF-591a during play.

## Human-supremacist organizations
The source establishes active racist organizations including:
- **Humanis Policlub**
- **Ku Klux Klan**
- **The Order**

They flourish in parts of Redmond and can organize rallies, recruitment, intimidation, and attacks against unprotected metahumans.

This is a real setting pressure, but it does not mean every Human resident is actively hostile or that every encounter should invoke racism.

## Factories and corporate enclaves
Factories operating in Redmond often fortify themselves heavily against theft, vandalism, and unrest. Armed guards, fencing, and protected transport are normal for significant industrial sites.

These installations function as small islands of private control inside otherwise weakly governed territory.

## Yakuza
The Yakuza maintain black-market activity in Redmond and use the district as a place to identify and recruit capable fighters and magicians.

Promising recruits may receive training, cyberware, or access to magical resources.

The source therefore supports Yakuza influence as a **real Redmond conflict boundary**, even where formal territorial ownership of a specific block is not stated.

## Mafia
The Redmond Mafia is in prolonged internal turmoil originating in **2039**, when Carl Decavitt and supporters attempted to break from the Seattle Mob.

Decavitt was executed, but the breakaway struggle continued.

Period source status:
- **Ra Halquist** — leader of the Redmond renegade faction.
- **Don James O'Malley** — Seattle Mob leader who still considers Redmond under his authority.
- **Theodore Atencio** — acting as O'Malley's capo in relation to Redmond.

This creates an actual organized-crime boundary independent of the Yakuza.

## Seoulpa Rings
Redmond is used as a recruiting ground by Seoulpa Rings.

The source states that **five known Rings** operate in Redmond and continually provide recruits to Rings elsewhere.

The most active named Ring is:
- **III Squires**
- led by **Dan Aurigemma**

## Street gangs
The source describes **hundreds, possibly thousands**, of gangs or small violent groups in Redmond.

Three infamous groups are specifically named as current to the 2050-era source:

- **Rusted Stilettos** — metahuman gang associated with **Glow City**.
- **Crimson Crush** — associated with **Bargain Basement**.
- **Red Hot Nukes** — associated with **Hollywood**.

These are much better 2051 campaign anchors than silently importing later 2060 territorial data.

## Local neighborhood power
Weak formal government means neighborhood businesses, squatter communities, gangs, black markets, fortified institutions, and organized crime can create meaningful local boundaries without possessing formal jurisdiction.

A conflict need not involve a city-wide faction. A single market, hospital, factory, apartment tower, bar, clinic, or gang-controlled intersection may have enough local power to matter.

# ECONOMIC AND INDUSTRIAL CONFLICT

Businesses that survive in Redmond often do so through one or more of:
- heavy private security,
- illegal or semi-legal production,
- black-market distribution,
- low-cost industrial processing,
- recycling,
- chemical or petrochemical work,
- supplying low-grade goods to larger corporations,
- or operating in places where city enforcement is weak.

Industrial transport is dangerous because valuable material must pass through squatter territory and weakly policed roads. Some major facilities use helicopters or tilt-rotor aircraft to move goods instead.

This creates natural conflict around:
- convoy routes,
- material theft,
- protection,
- labor,
- sabotage,
- industrial pollution,
- gangs trying to tax movement,
- corporate security crossing local turf,
- and black-market diversion.

# NEIGHBORHOODS

## Redmond / Touristville
The most developed and orderly part of the district.

Its relative stability is attributed to the presence of the Capitol Building and proximity to Bellevue. Most formal tourism is confined here.

**Lifestyle:** Lower Class.

## Rat's Nest / North Seattle Garbage Center
Large open-air garbage dump supporting nearly a thousand residents living in improvised shelters among the refuse.

## Plastic Jungles
Former giant greenhouse complex built before the Crash.

The greenhouses were constructed on heavily polluted land and once produced enormous crops that were unsuitable for public consumption. After the Crash the project was abandoned.

By the source period the complex houses a substantial metahuman squatter population.

## Glow City
A severely contaminated region.

Key source history:
- **2013:** Trojan-Satsop nuclear plant meltdown contaminated Beaver Lake and surrounding land.
- **2028:** Shiawase Atomics built another power plant near the remains.
- Post-Crash: the newer plant stopped generating power.
- Squatter occupation increased despite government attempts to clear the area.

The area suffers exceptionally high rates of cancer-related illness and infant mortality.

## Bargain Basement
Former high-quality apartment district badly degraded after the Crash.

Buildings rely on illegal utility hookups. Street markets and bazaars sell a wide range of goods.

The source explicitly establishes both **Mafia and Yakuza black markets** operating here.

# GM USE SUMMARY

Load HF-591a when play needs:
- a **2050/2051**, rather than 2060, Redmond organization or gang;
- organized-crime boundaries;
- local political or police reach;
- a named bar, hospital, clinic, business, mall, prison, or neighborhood;
- a plausible local faction that might care about an event;
- a black-market or industrial conflict;
- period geography;
- or sourcebook rumors that can be promoted into a deliberate GM hook.

Do **not** use shadowtalk as omniscient narrator truth merely because it appears in the sourcebook.

For conflict generation, prefer established 2051 structures first:
1. neighborhood/local interests,
2. gangs with period-established turf,
3. Yakuza/Mafia/Seoulpa interests,
4. Human-supremacist organizations,
5. fortified businesses/corporate enclaves,
6. weak but real municipal/police authority,
7. only then new or imported factions when continuity supports them.
