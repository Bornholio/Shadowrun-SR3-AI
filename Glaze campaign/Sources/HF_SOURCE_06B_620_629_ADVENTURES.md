## HF-620 — Blood in the Boardroom: Setup and Hooks

<!-- HF-SOURCE-BEGIN HF-620 -->

# Blood in the Boardroom — GM Setup & Hooks

## What This Is

A campaign sourcebook, not a linear adventure. The megacorp wars of 2057–2060 play out in the background of the Sixth World regardless of what your runners do. This sourcebook gives you the machinery behind those events and multiple entry points for runner involvement. The outcome of the corporate conflicts is largely predetermined by canon — your job is to use the chaos as a backdrop for missions, not to simulate the boardroom votes.

There are four major conflict threads (Tracks), each following a different corporate power struggle. You can run one track start to finish, let runners jump between tracks, or use all four simultaneously as background noise that occasionally explodes into a firefight on the runners' doorstep.

---

## The Big Picture

Everything flows from one event: **Dunkelzahn's assassination on 9 August 2057**, the night of his inauguration as UCAS President. His will, read publicly afterward, contained dozens of bequests that destabilised the megacorporate balance of power. The specific bequests that matter:

- **Miles Lanier** (Fuchi head of security) receives Dunkelzahn's Renraku stock and a board seat → triggers Fuchi civil war
- **Arthur Vogel** (eco-lawyer) receives Dunkelzahn's Ares stock and a board seat → threatens Damien Knight's control of Ares
- **Nadja Daviar** (Dunkelzahn's translator, new UCAS VP) inherits Gavilan Ventures, which holds 12% of Ares
- **Wu Lung-Wai / Wuxing** receives 200 million nuyen → enables the Pacific Prosperity Group
- **Inazo Aneki** (Renraku CEO) receives the Seal of the Green Gloves (entry to Tibet — becomes relevant in 2060)

These bequests set four corporate conflicts in motion simultaneously. The runners will feel the effects long before they understand the causes.

## Hooking the Runners

The corp war creates an almost unlimited supply of shadow work. The key is that **runners don't need to understand what's happening to be caught up in it** — they just need to take jobs. Understanding comes later, if they survive.

### The Johnson Approach

The simplest entry point. A corporate Johnson hires the team for what looks like a routine run — extraction, datasteal, sabotage, protection — and it turns out to be a move in the corp war chess game. The runners may not realise this until they're knee-deep in it. Let them accumulate a couple of ordinary-seeming runs before the bigger picture starts to emerge.

- Corps can't trust their own employees right now. Divided loyalties mean internal security may be compromised. Outside deniable assets are more reliable.
- A Johnson working for one faction of Fuchi won't necessarily identify which faction they're working for — or even that Fuchi is involved.
- Payment is usually good because the stakes are high.

### The Contact Web

If any runner has corporate contacts, those contacts are living through the corp war. A fixer who works with Fuchi corps is suddenly having to pick sides. A corporate contact might reach out to the runners — not as a Johnson but as someone who needs help and is out of options.

- A contact may be caught between factions and need extraction from their own employer
- A contact may have information that multiple factions want, and need a safe house and bodyguards
- A contact may have gone missing, and another runner's contact is asking questions

### Making It Personal

The corp war's collateral damage lands on ordinary people. Runners have SINless lives, but they have neighborhoods, they have people they care about, they have fixers and bartenders and street docs who are all affected.

- The Redmond Barrens crash (Flight 1118, July 2059) destroys acres of the Barrens and kills hundreds. If any runner lives in or has ties to Redmond, this hits close to home.
- Corporate security crackdowns spill into the sprawl — Knight Errant patrols in unexpected areas, Renraku Red Samurai operating in the open.
- The Seattle Matrix RTG attack (March 2060) is terrifying and unexplained. Any runner who was jacked in during the ten-minute window has a very bad time.

### Security Work

Corps and their subsidiaries are hiring outside security during the war — they don't know who on their internal team has been turned. This gets runners embedded inside corporate operations, where they'll inevitably witness things they weren't supposed to see.

---

## Running the Corporate NPCs

The megacorp executives in this book are powerful, paranoid, and operating through layers of deniable assets. Runners will rarely if ever meet them directly. What they'll meet are:

- **Mr. Johnsons** who work for those executives (and may not know their employer's full agenda)
- **Security teams** acting on orders from those executives
- **Other runner teams** hired by rival factions for conflicting objectives

When portraying corp employees, resist the temptation to make everyone a loyal drone or a scheming villain. The war has created genuine confusion and distress at every level:

- Some employees are picking sides based on personal loyalty to a manager
- Some are terrified of losing their jobs and not thinking about the bigger picture
- Some are opportunists looking to land somewhere profitable
- A few are true believers in their corp's mission

This variation makes the corporate world feel real and gives runners room to find unexpected allies — or unexpected enemies — within the same corporation.

## Story Structure Options

### Single Track

Pick the conflict most interesting to your group and run it start to finish. Use events from the other tracks as background noise — things runners hear about on Shadowland but don't get involved with.

### Jumping Between Tracks

Let runners follow the work. A run for Novatech (Track 1) connects to a Renraku datasteal (Track 2) when they discover the same research is at the center of both. This requires more GM prep but creates a richer experience.

### Freeform

Leak information slowly through contacts and Shadowland posts. Let runners decide what they want to pursue. Have all four tracks running simultaneously in the background. This is the most realistic approach and the hardest to run — requires the GM to know all four tracks well enough to improvise.

### Novel Structure

Pick a clear starting point (the Dunkelzahn assassination, or the Flight 1118 crash, or Novatech's announcement) and run events in rough chronological order toward a defined endpoint. Good for groups that want a campaign with a shape.

---

## Key Seattle Flashpoints

Most of the war's effects can be felt in Seattle regardless of where specific corporate HQs are located. Seattle-specific pressure points:

- **Fuchi's Seattle operations** are a battleground between the Villiers, Nakatomi, and Yamana factions throughout 2058-2059
- **The Redmond Barrens crash** (Flight 1118) creates weeks of chaos, emergency response, and media attention
- **David Hague's body** is found in an abandoned Redmond apartment building a week after the crash
- **The Renraku arcology** closes in December 2059 — runners who try to get in or out will find it impenetrable
- **The Seattle Matrix RTG attack** (March 2060) affects every decker and anyone with headware in the city
- **Samantha Villiers** runs Novatech Northwest from Seattle and is a potential ongoing contact/employer
- **Darren Villiers** (Richard's dwarf brother, physical adept, Grade 3 initiate) operates covertly in Seattle as Novatech's director of special assets — he's the one hiring runners for sensitive Novatech work

## HF-621 — Blood in the Boardroom: Civil War

<!-- HF-SOURCE-BEGIN HF-621 -->

# Blood in the Boardroom — Track 1: Civil War (Fuchi)

## What's Happening and Why

Fuchi Industrial Electronics is destroying itself from the inside. For twenty years, three families have run the corporation as an uneasy triumvirate: **Richard Villiers** (Fuchi Americas), **Shikei Nakatomi** (Fuchi Asia), and **Korin Yamana** (Fuchi Pan-Europa). None of them could gain a decisive edge over the others, which kept the company stable if not exactly harmonious.

Dunkelzahn's will broke that equilibrium two ways:
1. Miles Lanier — Fuchi's head of internal security and Villiers' closest ally — received Renraku stock and walked out the door to join Renraku's board
2. Villiers received additional Fuchi stock, solidifying his position as the largest single shareholder

Lanier's departure looked like a disaster for Villiers. In fact, it was the opening move of a long con. Lanier fed Renraku inside information on Fuchi's systems and security — which Nakatomi and Yamana used as a pretext to come after Villiers. What they didn't realise was that Villiers was already building his escape hatch: a separate corporate empire that he'd eventually incorporate as **Novatech**.

By the time Nakatomi and Yamana figured out what Villiers was doing, he'd already stripped Fuchi Americas of its most valuable assets. He then sold off his remaining Fuchi stock at peak price (forcing Nakatomi and Yamana into a bidding war that tripled the stock value), pocketed the profit, and left them holding the ruins.

What's left of Fuchi after Villiers leaves tears itself apart between Nakatomi and Yamana. Yamana eventually wins, marries into the Shiawase family, and sells Fuchi to Shiawase entirely. Nakatomi, furious, throws his lot in with Renraku. On 28 July 2060, Fuchi Industrial Electronics ceases to exist.

---

## The Fuchi Civil War in Play

During 2058-2059, before the endgame plays out, Seattle is a low-intensity warzone between the three Fuchi factions. This is the sweet spot for runner involvement:

- **Datasteal and counter-datasteal** between Fuchi subsidiaries nominally owned by different factions
- **Extraction runs** on employees who've defected or are about to
- **Sabotage** of one faction's facilities disguised to look like the other faction's work
- **Security testing** — factions checking whether their own people are loyal, using runners as cat's-paws
- **Protection work** for executives who don't know which of their own bodyguards can be trusted

The war intensifies after Flight 1118 (July 2059) and Hague's death. By October 2059, when Villiers announces Novatech, the pretense of unity collapses entirely. After that, it's open season.

---

## Key NPCs

### Richard Villiers
**Role:** Architect of the whole thing. CEO of Fuchi Americas, then founder/president of Novatech.

The most dangerous man in this story, and probably the one runners will work for most often without knowing it. Villiers operates through layers of intermediaries. He is methodical, patient, and ruthless — his ability to manipulate people and events over a multi-year timescale while keeping his true intentions hidden is extraordinary. He may have arranged Kiyoshi Nakatomi's murder forty years ago. He definitely engineered the Lanier/Renraku scheme. He probably had something to do with Flight 1118 and David Hague's death — but nothing can be proved.

**For runners:** Villiers will pay well and his Johnsons will always have plausible cover stories. The catch is that "working for Villiers" and "working for a Fuchi Americas Johnson" may be the same thing for months before runners realise it. Once they're associated with Novatech, Nakatomi and Yamana will treat them as enemies.

### Miles Lanier
**Role:** Former Fuchi security chief, Renraku board member (briefly), then Novatech director of security.

A former UCAS Army sniper who transitioned into corporate security. Brilliant tactician. Arrogant, demanding, difficult to work with. His "defection" to Renraku was a performance — he was always working for Villiers. After the Corporate Court forced him out of Renraku, he went straight to Novatech.

In the Novatech period, Lanier is purging executives at former Fuchi Americas subsidiaries that are being converted to Novatech divisions. He's looking for Nakatomi and Yamana loyalists and removing them. He'll also use runners for this work — it's cleaner than using Novatech's own security people.

### Samantha Villiers
**Role:** Richard's ex-wife, VP of Novatech Northwest (based in Seattle).

Divorced over a decade ago but still a valued partner. She's the one who holds the 2% of post-Villiers Fuchi stock that makes her the tiebreaker between Nakatomi and Yamana — she sells to Yamana in January 2060, triggering the endgame. In Seattle she's a legitimate executive contact, one of the city's most eligible singles, and a potential recurring employer for runners who make a good impression.

### Darren Villiers
**Role:** Richard's younger brother, Novatech Seattle director of special assets.

A dwarf. A physical adept with a knack for stealth and burglary, Grade 3 initiate. He held the title of senior VP of Seattle operations at Fuchi but spent most of his time running covert operations rather than sitting behind a desk. Now does the same for Novatech. He's the one who directly hires runners for sensitive work — sometimes running with them on particularly delicate jobs without revealing who he is or who he works for.

### Shikei Nakatomi
**Role:** Head of Fuchi Asia, later a Renraku board member.

Holds Fuchi together with Yamana after Villiers leaves, then loses the tiebreaker vote when Samantha sells to Yamana. His hatred of Villiers becomes the dominant force in his life. He eventually buys the Lanier Renraku stock block from the Zurich-Orbital bank, joins Renraku's board, and begins using Renraku as a platform for revenge. Runs as "the Business Butcher" — professionally ruthless, now also personally motivated.

### Korin Yamana
**Role:** Head of Fuchi Pan-Europa, eventually head of what remains of Fuchi, then a Shiawase board member.

Ninety years old by the endgame. He achieves the goal he's pursued for forty years — sole control of Fuchi — just in time for it to be worthless. He doesn't have the energy to rebuild from scratch and sees Shiawase as the best available deal. Marries Mitsuko Shiawase in a nationally televised ceremony and sells Fuchi to Shiawase in its entirety. Moves to Osaka. Possibly the saddest figure in this whole story.

## Adventure Framework: Crash Team

**When:** Early July 2059, the night of Flight 1118

**The Job As Presented:** The runners are hired to intercept and detain Corporate Court justice David Hague when he arrives in Seattle on a late flight from Tokyo. Their Johnson wants Hague brought to a safe house in Redmond, where the Johnson will take over. The job is presented as a snatch — keep Hague alive, keep it quiet.

**What Goes Wrong:** Hague is on Flight 1118. It crashes into the Redmond Barrens at 300+ km/h, killing 153 passengers and 51 people on the ground. The runners' plan is immediately obsolete.

**The New Mission:** Johnson calls immediately. Forget the live snatch — now he needs Hague's body (or at minimum Hague's briefcase, which bears the Corporate Court logo). The briefcase is handcuffed to Hague's wrist. Getting to the crash site is the first problem.

**Getting to the Crash Site:**
- Every DocWagon, Lone Star patrol, fire truck, news crew, and rubbernecker in Seattle is heading the same direction
- Go-gangs are running wild while Lone Star is occupied
- Ground travel to the site is effectively impossible
- The Johnson can arrange a Hughes Stallion helicopter pickup from the roof of a nearby parking garage — if the runners can get there

**At the Crash Site:**
The plane hit in multiple pieces across several square miles of the Barrens:
- Tail section in James Lake, mostly submerged
- Nose section against a hillside to the east
- Everything between them is debris, bodies, fire, and chaos

Hague had a first-class ticket — he was probably near the front. When runners reach the hillside where the nose section came down, they find what looks like a DocWagon Crisis Response Team already on scene. Problems:
1. Their ambulance arrived from the east — all other rescue vehicles are coming from the west
2. The "paramedics" are carrying silenced SMGs
3. When a real DocWagon team arrives shortly after, the fake team opens fire on them

The fake team is Renraku operatives. They find Hague's body, cut the briefcase from his wrist, and load both into their ambulance.

**The Chase:** Runners must pursue the Renraku team to an abandoned apartment building in Redmond. Inside, two cyberware techs are waiting to surgically remove Hague's headware memory — they're not worried about damaging the body, but they don't want to damage the gear. One operative is working on the briefcase. Four guards.

**Climax:** Once runners retrieve whatever they can (body, briefcase, or both), the Johnson tells them to return Hague's body to the crash site — it needs to be found there. Getting back through the chaos is as hard as getting out was.

**GM Notes:**
- The Johnson is working for Villiers. Hague was a Yamana supporter — his death (with Villiers' ally Lynn Osborne as the only remaining Fuchi Court rep) is convenient. Whether Villiers arranged the crash is left deliberately ambiguous.
- Renraku wanted Hague's headware memory because he knew about the Lanier scheme. Whatever's in that memory implicates both parties.
- The real DocWagon team, if the runners interact with them, is just doing their job in a nightmare situation. If runners steal their ambulance, DocWagon will want it back.
- Reporters may have caught runners on camera. Depending on their behavior, this could be good or bad for their reputation.

**Follow-Up Hooks:**
- Renraku is spreading rumours that Villiers engineered the crash. Villiers may hire runners to find proof it was someone else (or to suppress proof that it was him).
- Hague's body is eventually found in the apartment building where the Renraku team took it — a week after the crash. The media wants to know how it got there.
- DocWagon's investigation into the fake team at the crash site may circle back to the runners.

## Adventure Framework: Loose Lips Fry Chips

**The Setup:** A freelance decker the runners know — call him HAL — has hit a Fuchi Asia system in San Francisco and run into a piece of experimental black IC code-named "Stoolie." It works like a neurological truth serum: the victim compulsively reveals everything they know, to anyone who will listen, with no ability to stop themselves. HAL is currently in a public shadowrunner bar, loudly describing his illegal runs in detail. To everyone.

**The Problem:** In his current state, HAL will sell out every runner he's ever worked with. He's aware this is happening and can't stop it. He's also carrying information about Fuchi Asia's internal plans that every Fuchi faction desperately wants — and which is a death warrant for him once they have it.

**Event 1:** Fuchi Asia security enters the bar to arrest HAL. If runners don't intervene, HAL gets taken to a secure facility for research and then execution. If they intervene, they now have HAL and Fuchi Asia has them on their list.

**Event 2:** The moment runners hit the street with HAL, two more Fuchi security teams appear — one from Fuchi Americas, one from Pan-Europa. Three-way fight between the factions, all wanting HAL alive, none willing to let the others have him. The runners can use the chaos to slip away.

**Event 3:** Safe house, for now. HAL remembers seeing a psychotropic counter-conditioning program in the Fuchi Asia system during his run. He also now realises he stumbled onto something very big — big enough to sell, but also big enough that Fuchi will kill everyone who's heard it. That includes the runners now.

**Climax:** The counter-program is in the Fuchi Asia San Francisco host, but it's booby-trapped with trace IC and asks for the physical location of whoever downloads it. HAL, in his current state, will answer truthfully. Fuchi strike teams will converge on their location. Multiple Fuchi factions will fight over them. Getting HAL cured and everyone out alive requires outrunning at least two corporate security teams.

**GM Notes:**
- HAL as an NPC is a gift — use his compulsive honesty to reveal old plot threads, embarrassing truths, or seeds for future runs
- Fuchi Americas developed the original "Stoolie" IC. They want to know how it worked in practice.
- The "big information" HAL found can be whatever serves your campaign — upcoming moves against Renraku, Villiers' endgame plan, the identity of a mole, whatever.
- All three Fuchi factions remain on the runners' trail after this run, for different reasons.

## Adventure Framework: Black Operations

**The Setup:** The Yamana faction of Fuchi wants to seize control of a delta clinic hidden inside the Pueblo Corporate Council — a facility that Villiers' people have been running and which is deeply involved in experimental cybermancy research. Yamana can't use Fuchi troops (too obvious) so they're hiring outside.

**The Cover:** A very prestigious fixer offers the runners an exceptional job: penetrate a facility in a Native American nation, pose as a helicopter supply crew, seize the command center, and hold it while a mercenary unit moves in to take control. No unnecessary violence — everything in the facility is expensive. Runners are not told it's a Fuchi facility or a delta clinic.

**Event 1:** Santa Fe. Pick up Fuchi uniforms, IDs, and a rigger (Leonides, if the team lacks one). The helicopter they need to hijack is in a Fuchi hangar across the airport, guarded by armed security and some support staff. Their employers have deckers handling surveillance. The helicopter is loaded with medical supplies and tissue samples.

**Event 2:** The clinic is in the middle of nowhere — helipad, several buildings, serious defenses including rigged security, drones, magical barriers, and background count. The runners' advantage is surprise, detailed floor plans, and access codes. They need to get to the command center. Once they signal all-clear, the mercs move in.

**Event 3:** Dr. Dmitri Zalman, the head researcher and an initiated hermetic mage, is outraged and uncooperative. A Yamana-backed Fuchi executive flies in to take over. The runners are told to guard Zalman in a detention cell while things consolidate. Zalman will attempt mind control spells if given any opening.

**Climax:** While Zalman plays for time, a British vampire named **Mr. Monty Livingston** — a member of the Ordo Maximus initiatory group, which has a deal with Villiers not Yamana — arrives to sabotage the takeover. He's already alerted Villiers' people, who are on their way. The clinic descends into chaos as three-way fighting breaks out between the meres, Livingston's people, and arriving Villiers loyalists. Pueblo Security Forces surround the facility, blocking escape. The runners need to extract anyone they were ordered to extract and get out before it becomes a siege.

**GM Notes:**
- The Ordo Maximus has been teaching Fuchi's mages cybermancy techniques. This is a thread that can lead anywhere you want.
- Zalman has access to tissue samples for ritual sorcery. If runners piss him off and survive, he has options.
- The Yamana faction will reward success. Runners who end up helping Zalman will have a very short remaining lifespan.
- The "experimental tactical computer" subplot from the Adventure Ideas section (Bustin' Out) can be seeded here — maybe Zalman is working on cybermancy applications for tactical headware.

## Shorter Adventure Ideas (Flesh Out As Needed)

### Hostile Takeover
A Fuchi-owned aquasphere near Indonesia has declared independence during the chaos. Fuchi has blockaded it. The workers' council hires runners to smuggle food, supplies, and weapons through the blockade. Getting out is harder than getting in. Internal politics within the council complicate everything — some members want to negotiate with another faction, the hardliners want to stay independent.

### A Gift for the Bride
Yamana wants to impress his future wife Mitsuko Shiawase, who likes large reptiles. He wants a king cobra no less than eight meters long, captured alive in the Philippine jungles. By the time runners close in on a suitable specimen, Mitsuhama's Parashield division has already captured it. The runners need to extract a living eight-meter cobra from a corporate compound full of other Awakened creatures and get it out of the Philippines. Yakuza complications on the way back to Japan.

### Bustin' Out
A group calling themselves Corpbusters wants runners to break their leader out of a Fuchi prison before it changes hands to Novatech or gets dissolved. The catch: their leader has been implanted with an experimental tactical computer, and most of his "comrades" have similar exotic headware implants. None of them are actually Corpbusters — they're Saeder-Krupp acquisition targets. The runners have been hired by Lofwyr's people to extract research subjects under a false flag.

---

## How This Track Ends

By late 2060:
- **Novatech** is a legitimate AAA megacorp based in Boston, with Richard Villiers at the top and Miles Lanier running security. Samantha Villiers runs the Seattle operation. Darren Villiers runs covert ops. Novatech is a potential long-term employer.
- **Fuchi** no longer exists. Its remnants are split between Shiawase (Yamana's piece) and Renraku (Nakatomi's piece).
- **Nakatomi** is on Renraku's board and pushing for revenge against Villiers. Any runners who worked for Villiers have made a permanent enemy.
- **Yamana** is on Shiawase's board and largely content to retire. The Shiawase acquisition of Fuchi's European assets gives Shiawase a major new foothold.

Runners who worked for the Villiers faction throughout this track are now Novatech assets — potentially valuable, potentially exposed if Nakatomi ever gets his revenge campaign running.

## HF-622 — Blood in the Boardroom: Neck and Neck

# Blood in the Boardroom — Track 2: Neck and Neck (Renraku)

## What's Happening and Why

Renraku Computer Systems has been quietly keeping a secret that explains its sudden technological leap: they made a deal with **Leonardo**, an elf of extraordinary and apparently unique talent, who was blackmailing every AAA megacorp simultaneously by demonstrating the ability to penetrate their most secure systems at will. Rather than pay the blackmail, Renraku cut a deal — funding for Leonardo's mysterious "Great Work" in exchange for exclusive access to his capabilities.

The results were extraordinary. Leonardo's technology gave Renraku hardware and software a decade ahead of anything the competition could produce. Their IC became nearly impenetrable. Their deckers gained tools no one else had. Renraku surged past Mitsuhama and began closing on Fuchi for second place in the megacorp rankings.

Then it all collapsed. Fuchi brought the Corporate Court down on Renraku over the Lanier espionage affair. Lanier resigned from Renraku's board, sold his stock to the Zurich-Orbital bank, and walked out. Leonardo disappeared — and most of his technology disappeared with it, leaving Renraku's own engineers trying to reverse-engineer products they'd been selling for a year. The arcology's security system went haywire in December 2059. CEO Aneki departed for Tibet in February 2060. A Court rep died in a bombing in New Delhi. The RTG attack hit Seattle in March. By the time Nakatomi bought the Lanier stock block and joined Renraku's board in April 2060, Renraku was battered but still standing — and now had a partner who shared their hatred of Richard Villiers.

---

## Renraku's Position for Runners

Renraku was already generating substantial shadow work before the crisis (their surge required constant acquisition of competitor research). During the crisis, they generate even more — patching holes in their Matrix defenses, trying to find out what's missing from their databases, investigating the arcology shutdown, hunting for Leonardo. After Nakatomi's arrival, they'll start building toward something against Villiers and Novatech.

Renraku's shadow operations have a different flavor than most corps — they're disciplined, organized, and professional, but they're also more paranoid than usual right now. Runners who work for Renraku will be vetted carefully and may find themselves subject to loyalty tests they don't know they're taking.

## Renraku Corporate History (For Context)

The core of Renraku was originally **Keruba International**, a Slovenian military manufacturer that diversified into telecom and computer hardware. Its CEO died in the Crash of '29 and **Inazo Aneki** — then a young corporate raider — swooped in, bought the wreckage, and transformed it into Renraku. Aneki spent years managing the company poorly (corruption ran rampant throughout the 2030s) before a near-coup scared him into becoming the leader he should have been. By 2045, Renraku was a stable, if predictable, megacorp. Then Leonardo showed up and made it something more.

**Role:** Renraku CEO, absent from February 2060

Seventy years old, patient, intelligent, genuinely devoted to his company and its people. Never married — Renraku is his family. He pulled the company back from the brink twice (once from corruption in the 2030s, once from the Fuchi/Lanier crisis). ## Adventure Framework: This Is Your Brain On Otaku

**Background:** Renraku has been running an undercover operation to learn about the otaku — deckers who connect to the Matrix without hardware. One of their agents, **Diana Peng** (age 20, presented as a data entry clerk, actually a trained security operative), infiltrated an otaku tribe in Seattle called the Beamwalkers. She went through the Deep Resonance as her initiation. Something went wrong. Renraku found her on a street corner, physically unharmed, but severely brain-damaged. She's now in a small private clinic in Tacoma, checked in under the false name "Olivia Tang," her expenses paid by a fake "Roger Tang."

**The Job As Presented:** A middle-aged man named "Stuart Peng" contacts the runners, claiming Diana is his daughter and that a cult called the Beamwalkers is paying for her care. He can't legally remove her because he can't prove his relationship to "Olivia Tang." He wants the runners to extract her illegally and get evidence he can use against the cult.

**Who "Stuart Peng" Actually Is:** A corporate expediter for the Nakatomi faction of Fuchi. The Nakatomis think Renraku's otaku investigation was connected to Villiers' operations and want Diana to find out more. The real Stuart Peng is a mid-level Renraku executive who believes his daughter is on an assignment with the Red Samurai.

**What Diana Is:** A Renraku security agent with a datajack and a high-quality retractable spur she doesn't seem to know she has. Her brain damage impairs her ability to relate to reality — she communicates mostly in confused fragments, and she becomes frantic near cyberdecks, screaming "Back! Back! Back!" Renraku has a tracking signal implanted with her headware memory and will know where she is.

**Event 1 — The Clinic:**
The Stasky Institute is a neurological clinic in Tacoma. Security is oriented toward keeping patients in, not intruders out. Diana is on the second floor in a room with a single narrow window. A Beamwalker otaku is watching the clinic's Matrix systems for any sign of who's looking for Diana — and will trip the alarm as the runners leave to make them easier to track.

**Event 2 — The Transfer:**
"Stuart Peng" wants to meet at the Villa Plaza mall. During the ride, Diana is dissociated and quiet. If she sees a cyberdeck, she goes wild trying to reach it. When runners contact the fake Johnson, Renraku's surveillance drones are already following them and ground teams are moving into position. A few miles from the drop point, two Chrysler-Nissan Patrol-1s with three Renraku company men each plus a rigger block the route and demand Diana back. They'll fight for her but use reduced-lethality tactics to avoid damaging her.

**Climax — The Mall:**
The Beamwalker otaku have also been following Diana's signal (they detected the tracker during the Deep Resonance). At the handoff, the mall's security systems are taken over by Beamwalker otaku. The tribe surrounds the group and demands Diana be released. Diana, distracted, runs toward a Hardware Etcetera window display of cyberdecks. The mall entrance opens for her — the otaku have taken over the building's systems. People inside are waiting to bring her to safety.

The runners are now stuck between:
- The Beamwalker tribe controlling the building's systems
- Fuchi guards loyal to the Nakatomi faction
- Surviving Renraku pursuers
- Mall security

Whatever side the runners choose, or if they try to take Diana somewhere else entirely, the Beamwalkers have the home field advantage.

**GM Notes:**
- Diana's condition makes her impossible to interrogate meaningfully, which is frustrating for every faction. Her value is in what's in her headware memory, not in what she can say.
- The real Stuart Peng will eventually find out his daughter has been moved and will spend serious money looking for her.
- If Diana ends up with the Beamwalkers, runners who treated them decently may find Matrix problems mysteriously disappear. Runners who fought the tribe will find Matrix problems mysteriously appear.
- Several weeks later, regardless of the run's outcome, a runner may spot Diana entering the Renraku arcology's mall — and then she seems to simply disappear inside.

## Adventure Framework: Prove It To Me

**Background:** Yukiako Watanabe doesn't trust Miles Lanier. He's been on Renraku's board for months, his inside information on Fuchi has been valuable, but she despises his personality and suspects his motives. Before he gets too embedded in Renraku's operations, she wants his loyalty tested — through a series of covert operations designed to catch him acting against Renraku's interests.

**The Job:** Runners are hired through the Matrix by a faceless Mr. Johnson offering good nuyen for "diverse activities" in Boston. The Johnson has heavyweight decker support. He says they're "testing new security measures." He's not lying exactly — just omitting that the security measure being tested is a person.

**Event 1 — Surveillance:**
The runners set up in Boston and begin watching Lanier. His residence, office, LTG numbers, schedule — everything. The Johnson wants everything tapped and documented. Lanier's life is not immediately exciting (he's being careful), but he talks to interesting people. He's also a former security professional who knows how surveillance works, so this is not easy work. Whatever the runners find, the Johnson (Watanabe's agent) is never satisfied — she's right that Lanier is being subtle.

**Event 2 — The Fake Hit:**
Watanabe decides to act boldly. She passes the runners information about a marine biology forum and gala at the New England Aquarium that Lanier will attend. Their orders: make an assassination attempt on Lanier — emphasis on attempt, not success. The Johnson says it's a security test and that the attempt is not meant to succeed.

What Watanabe has also done: anonymously told Fuchi (Villiers' people) about the planned hit, and placed Renraku agents throughout the area to watch for Fuchi's response. Villiers does send people to watch out for Lanier — but they spot the Renraku surveillance and smell a trap. They don't interfere with the assassination attempt. They try to track the runners afterward for questioning.

**Event 3 — The Frame:**
Watanabe tries to frame Lanier for espionage. The runners' decker poses as a disenchanted Renraku employee selling classified data — specifically partial codework for a new piece of Renraku Tar Pit IC. The bait gets posted to Matrix shadowboards and sent to a suspected Lanier contact.

A decker named **Phaze** responds and arranges a Matrix meeting at Rox Rootz, a Boston decker bar. The runners' job is to sell the data, then trace Phaze to his physical location for capture and use as evidence against Lanier. Watanabe provides one-shot Rating 10 scanner and tracking programs. She'll assign two Renraku combat deckers as backup if requested.

Phaze takes the data, notices it's incomplete, and will fight or run if threatened. If traced, he's decking from an illegal hookup in Boston's Rox area. When runners arrive at his location, heavily armed corporate types extract him — Lanier's people, who were watching.

**Climax — The Mole:**
Watanabe's final gambit: a captured Fuchi mole from inside Renraku. She has the mole transported to Boston and uses runners as babysitters, planning to leak news of the mole's capture to Lanier's network and watch for an extraction attempt. Lanier's security team has been tracking the runners by now. While some runners go to pick up the prisoner, Lanier's people capture the others using non-lethal weaponry.

Now the group is split: some with the Fuchi mole, some captured. Lanier will arrange a trade. Red Samurai arrive to complicate the exchange. Throughout all of this, Lanier never personally appears — everything is handled through people who will admit working for Lanier only under extreme duress, and who know nothing of his actual loyalties.

**Resolution:** No evidence of Lanier's continuing Fuchi connection is obtainable from this adventure. Everything is inconclusive. Watanabe grudgingly gives him more access. The runners have been used, and their Mr. Johnson's faceless UMS icon isn't enough to trace back to anyone. If they manage to track the Johnson and try anything, Red Samurai show up.

## Adventure Framework: What Does A Ten-Thousand-Year-Old Dragon Get?

**Background:** Leonardo has disappeared, and the great dragon **Hestaby** wants to know why. She recently formed an alliance with him and is concerned about certain plans he was pursuing for his Great Work. She's investigated his destroyed sanctuary in Africa and found nothing — now she's hiring runners to pick up whatever threads Renraku still has.

**The Job:** Runners are approached by a California shaman named Breeze — neo-hippie presentation, serious resources. He wants them to investigate Leonardo's disappearance, starting at a location in Iran. Suborbital tickets to Baghdad, helicopter from there to the town of Ahvaz. Excellent forged IDs provided.

**Event 1 — Ahvaz:**
The observatory Leonardo used is now occupied by Abu Khalid and his Blacksand Revolutionaries — a force that recently drove off a Renraku military unit from the area. The locals recognize pictures of Leonardo and say he lived in the observatory until "the sirrurg came" (by which they mean a dragon) and destroyed it. Runners who piece this together realize a dragon demolished Leonardo's sanctuary.

**Event 2 — Inside the Observatory:**
Abu Khalid has set up a command post in the burned-out tower. Almost everything inside has been destroyed. However: there are German-speaking "military advisers" present who are actually Saeder-Krupp officers. After the observatory was demolished, Lofwyr hired Abu Khalid's group to secure the site and destroy anything remaining. The SK officers are here to make sure that's been done — and to discourage investigation through torture. Some Renraku computer equipment has been salvaged, but runners won't find useful data about Leonardo. The only clues are a pointer to a Renraku matrix host, a specific filename ("MU Zeta Five"), and an optical chip with unbreakable encryption.

**Event 3 — The Renraku Host:**
Accessible from anywhere with satellite uplink (hundreds of miles from Ahvaz). The host benchmarks around Red-5. No references to Leonardo exist — they've been systematically erased. A successful Dump Log or Edit File operation reveals a sophisticated virus did it, on the same day Leonardo disappeared. Searching for "MU Zeta Five" returns a "Slave Syntax Error" and a pointer to a partially deleted personnel file: **Kura Kineda**, personal aide to COO Haruhiko Nakada. A Locate Slave operation finds an encoder system for someone with data lock headware — and the current transponder frequency for Kineda's headware implant. Whatever Leonardo told Renraku is now in Kineda's head.

**Climax — San Francisco:**
Kineda is on a suborbital landing in San Francisco shortly. Runners have just enough time to get there. They arrive to sirens, running guards, and distant autofire — someone else just grabbed Kineda and departed in a stealth helicopter. Pursuing it (steal a helicopter, hire something faster) leads to a chase to the border of Tir Tairngire near Shasta Mountain. The other team: **Tir Ghosts** sent by Ehran the Scribe, with at least one physical adept and one initiated mage. They're racing for the Tir border and calling for backup.

If Breeze has been kept informed, Hestaby's Shamans of Shasta may arrive to provide just enough force to turn the Tir team back — framing it as preventing a Tir incursion rather than revealing Hestaby's hand.

**Resolution:** Breeze takes custody of Kineda. He also has what's in Kineda's head — but he still needs codes to access the data-locked information. Renraku will try to activate Kineda's cortex bomb to eliminate the security threat. Ehran and the Tir are interested in the data and may pursue the runners. The encrypted optical chip remains a mystery.

**GM Notes:**
- Lofwyr's involvement in destroying Leonardo's sanctuary is a major thread. What does Saeder-Krupp know about the Great Work? Why did Lofwyr want it destroyed?
- Hestaby and Ehran being on opposite sides here is a dragon politics hook that can go anywhere.
- Leonardo himself is not found. He may or may not be dead.

## Adventure Framework: This Hurts Me More Than It Hurts You

**The Setup:** Jacob Kitt owns HyperSense, a small simsense production company that Renraku bailed out two years ago in exchange for a 51% controlling interest and use as a testbed for Leonardo-derived tech. The research has secretly paid off — HyperSense has made genuine breakthroughs it hasn't reported to Renraku. Kitt wants to regain control of his company by faking a corporate hit, convincing Renraku to divest from a company that looks like a liability.

He's borrowed money from local yakuza to fund a secret new production facility, where he's producing BTL chips — he owes them a favor. Now he wants runners to burn down the original HyperSense building so Renraku will walk away from it.

**The Job:** A twitchy man presents himself as a Mr. Johnson and offers good nuyen to hit a low-security facility — go in, burn the main building, minimal violence, specific target: HyperSense. He tells runners how to bypass the security. He doesn't want to negotiate much. Something is clearly off.

**Legwork:** Almost everything Kitt told them checks out. Buried in the data is Kitt's name — he owns HyperSense. Twenty-two of Fuchi Americas' recently-lost subsidiaries were taken by Cambridge Holdings — connected to Villiers. A large cash transfer to Cambridge Holdings from Silveri Investments (Villiers). Cambridge Holdings bought Fuchi Americas subsidiaries. Villiers bought Cambridge. His own companies, now his personally. Digging further: a new simsense facility in another part of town, protected by yakuza, clearly just operational. Connecting HyperSense's breakthroughs to a new BTL strain called "Flashers" on the street — distributed by the yaks.

**Yakuza Thread:** Any investigation into HyperSense attracts yakuza attention fast. First a polite warning. Then one runner gets hurt badly as an example. Then escalating violence. The oyabun doesn't know Renraku has a controlling interest — when he finds out, he'll be furious at Kitt for hiding it, because now he's got a megacorp as a competitor for his chip factory.

**Renraku Thread:** Renraku eventually notices interest in HyperSense and starts investigating Kitt's financial irregularities. They beef up security at the facility dramatically (right before the runners try the job). When they find the secret facility, they'll move to seize it — triggering a battle with the yakuza that may destroy everything.

**Climax:** Multiple parties converging: the runners trying to do their job, yakuza protecting their investment, Renraku moving to secure theirs, possibly Triads or Mafia investigating the Flashers BTL supply chain. Kitt is trying to avoid everyone. The secret facility is likely to be destroyed in the crossfire.

# Blood in the Boardroom — Track 3: Cross Purposes (Ares/CATCo)

## What's Happening and Why

Ares Macrotechnology is the largest corporation in the UCAS and the dominant North American megacorp. For over twenty years, **Damien Knight** has run it with ruthless efficiency, fending off every challenge to his position — including a decades-long war with **Leonard Aurelius**, whose father built Ares and who has never accepted Knight's takeover.

Two things happened to shake Knight's control:

1. Dunkelzahn's death ended Knight's two-year proxy over the Gavilan Ventures Ares stock (held by Nadja Daviar). When the proxy expired in August 2059, Daviar regained control of 12% of Ares. She gave those votes to neither Knight nor Aurelius.

2. Dunkelzahn's will also placed **Arthur Vogel** — eco-activist lawyer and former presidential candidate — on the Ares board with a Dunkelzahn stock bequest. Vogel was one of the last people Knight would choose as a board member.

These shifts were enough to give Aurelius one last shot. He sold his entire Ares stake privately to Vogel, then jumped ship to **Cross Applied Technologies** — a Quebec-based megacorp founded by **Dr. Lucien Cross**, who has hated Knight since 2053 and has been building a shadow war against Ares ever since. Aurelius brought money, insider knowledge, and the motivation of a man who's spent his life in his father's shadow and finally decided to live his own life.

With Aurelius's money and Seraphim-gathered blackmail material, CATCo recently achieved AAA megacorp status and got a seat on the Corporate Court. Now they're a genuine threat to Ares, and Knight is scrambling.

Meanwhile, Vogel holds more Ares stock than Knight but hasn't made a move. Whether he's being cautious or has sold out is the subject of constant speculation.

## The Ares-CATCo War For Runners

This is the most active ongoing conflict for shadowrunners because both Ares and Cross Applied Technologies operate in Seattle and have been running shadow operations against each other for years. The corps are roughly matched in operational capability (Ares vastly larger, CATCo's Seraphim vastly more skilled per head). The arrival of Aurelius escalates everything.

Runners can work for either side. Working for both, over time, creates problems — both corps are good at tracking who they've used and comparing notes. The Seraphim in particular have excellent intelligence and will eventually identify runners who've been playing both ends.

## Ares History That Matters

- **Nicholas Aurelius** founded Ares Industries in 2002, turned it into a megacorp
- **Damien Knight** appeared in 2033 via the Nanosecond Buyout — 63 seconds of programmed stock transactions that gave him 22% of Ares; he talked the board into making him CEO
- Knight and **Dunkelzahn** apparently coordinated during the Nanosecond Buyout — Dunkelzahn also acquired a significant stake (via Gavilan Ventures) and used it to help Knight maintain control
- Chicago, 2055: Knight Errant discovered and attacked the largest known insect spirit hive; the raid unleashed the bugs on Chicago's population; the Containment Zone lasted years; in February 2058 Ares used Strain III-Beta (an astral bacteria) to clean it out in under 12 hours
- The Chicago situation created a Ares public image problem that Knight Errant's response to the post-Dunkelzahn assassination riots largely repaired

## CATCo History That Matters

**Lucien Cross** was a programming genius working in corporate intelligence who helped Knight plan the Nanosecond Buyout. He didn't want to attach himself to Ares — he wanted startup capital. Knight paid him his commission. Cross kept copies of all the records proving how the Buyout was accomplished and who "David Gavilan" (Knight's cover identity) really was. He's had blackmail material on Knight for twenty-five years and has never used it — it's insurance.

Cross built CATCo through careful acquisitions, Quebec's anti-competition laws (which protect local corps from external takeovers), and his **Seraphim** — an elite intelligence and operations division that includes some of the best operatives in the world. In 2053, Cross grabbed Bioleve (a bioware company) that turned out to be an Ares subsidiary through shells. Knight sent an assassin. Cross survived because a Seraphim agent took the bullet. Dunkelzahn told Knight to back off. The rivalry went underground but never cooled.

Since Dunkelzahn's death removed that protection, Knight has tried three more times to kill Cross. Cross has beefed up security and intensified operations against Ares everywhere he can reach. Aurelius's arrival with blackmail material on Knight has now given Cross a seat on the Corporate Court and the resources for a genuine offensive.

## Adventure Framework: Not In My Backyard

Cross is furious but his Matrix operations against QTS have failed — QTS has sophisticated defenses.

The Seraphim need to make a physical run on QTS's Detroit HQ to get anything useful. Knight anticipates this and wants extra protection — from the shadows, not from Knight Errant (too obvious).

**The Job (For Runners Working For Ares):** A fixer offers the runners a two-week security contract at QTS in Detroit. Tour of the defenses, equipment provided. Night duty, on-call during days. One easy week. Then the Seraphim hit.

**Event 1 — The Seraphim Attack:**
The Seraphim use a multi-team approach:
- Four agents in stolen Knight Errant uniforms commandeer a KE vehicle
- A GMC truck full of Cutters gangers (paid for chaos) rams through the front gate and crashes into the main building
- The Seraphim follow the chaos inside, order security to chase the Cutters, and make for a jackpoint
- A helicopter with a mage on astral overwatch and sniper drones provides fire support overhead
- The truck explodes. Seraphim finish their job and get extracted

Surviving runners who take a captive Seraphim find out that Cross has discovered enough about QTS to potentially sway the vote — the Seraphim are taking data to Quebec City's chief of police, **Sebastien Hull**, who influences the seven aldermen. **Climax — Stopping Hull:**

The runners are sent to Quebec with the objective: stop Hull from arriving before 11am, by any means necessary. Knight's people handle border crossing and gear.

Creative approaches: traffic incidents, infrastructure failures, kidnapping someone Hull cares about, manufacturing a crisis requiring his personal attention, something that doesn't require fighting the convoy head-on.

**Aftermath:**
- If QTS vote passes: CATCo's Quebec monopoly is cracked, more shadow work follows as the two companies battle for the local market
- If QTS vote fails: the runners have failed a very powerful man; Ares will blacklist them in Detroit and other Ares-heavy cities; Knight will not forget
- Killing Hull makes every cop in Quebec a personal enemy

# Blood in the Boardroom — Track 4: Out of the East (Yamatetsu/PPG)

## What's Happening and Why

For over fifty years, five Japanese megacorps have dominated the corporate world. Everything in the Pacific flows through them. The Pacific Prosperity Group — an alliance of non-Japanese Pacific Rim corporations led by Wuxing of Hong Kong — has tried repeatedly to break that dominance and failed every time. The math never worked: even combining multiple smaller corps, they couldn't match a single AAA Japanese megacorp.

Then two things changed simultaneously:

1. **Yuri Shibanokuji** — an ork, the secret son of Yamatetsu's chairman — inherited his father's controlling stock in Yamatetsu and decided to actually use it
2. **Buttercup** — a free spirit and significant Yamatetsu shareholder who has been quietly working toward metahuman equality for decades — was ready to make her move

Together, they pulled Yamatetsu out of Japan entirely, relocated to Vladivostok, and joined the Pacific Prosperity Group. The combination of a AAA megacorp's muscle and the PPG's network created something that could actually challenge Japanese economic dominance of the Pacific.

Japan's response — harassment through environmental regulations, stock manipulation, racism — backfired. Yamatetsu left anyway. Japanese metahumans were left worse off than before, used as scapegoats for the departure. The PPG now has two AAA members (Yamatetsu and Wuxing, which achieved AAA status by getting a Corporate Court seat) and is growing rapidly.

---

## The Shibanokuji Story

The emotional center of this track is a father who failed his son and spent decades trying to make it right.

**Tadamako Shibanokuji** was a young division manager when he fell in love with a Russian port official named Tatiana Trigorin in Vladivostok. They married. In 2021, their two-year-old son **Yuri** underwent goblinization — became an ork. Tadamako was fighting for control of the family company's board at the time, and an ork son would give his enemies ammunition. He sent Yuri to Russia with Tatiana. She never spoke to him directly again. He spent the rest of his long career overcompensating — voting against metahuman interests to prove his objectivity — while the guilt accumulated.

In his final years, under Buttercup's influence and his own moral reckoning, he began quietly supporting the pro-metahuman faction on the board. He planned to eventually acknowledge Yuri publicly. He never got to. A stroke in January 2059 left him speechless. He died six weeks later without recovering.

**Yuri Shibanokuji** grew up in Vladivostok knowing his father had chosen his corporation over him. He has reasons to be bitter and largely isn't — he made a life in Vladivostok, built his own career, and when the inheritance dropped in his lap, he came to Kyoto not for revenge but to try to finish what his father had started. He has his father's stubbornness and none of his father's willingness to compromise on metahuman dignity.

Buttercup knew about Yuri for years and spent that time ensuring his influence would work in her favor when the moment came.

---

## Yamatetsu's Internal Split

Before the move, the board was divided:

**Conservative faction** (led by CEO Saru Iwano and former chairman Hideo Yoshida): Regards metahumans as deformed and sinister. Wants Yamatetsu to conform to Japanese social norms. Controls the company's day-to-day operations and most of the executive ranks.

**Progressive faction** (Buttercup, Newton Chin whose sister is a dwarf, various lesser shareholders): Wants to make Yamatetsu's pro-metahuman reputation a reality rather than marketing copy. Gained momentum under Tadamako's late influence.

**Yuri's arrival** shifted the math. Buttercup had spent weeks before his arrival buying out minor shareholders. Combined with Yuri's inherited stock and Tatiana's shares, plus Buttercup's own holdings, they had over 50% of voting shares when Yuri's motion to relocate was called. Iwano was unable to block it.

Iwano remains as Yamatetsu CEO — Buttercup and Yuri make no move to remove him. He's doing his job and they don't need a power struggle on top of everything else.

---

## The Pacific Prosperity Group

**What it is:** A corporate coalition, similar to NATO or OPEC. Any Pacific Rim corporation in agreement with the charter can petition for membership. Each member has a representative on the PPG board; the board elects a chairman.

**Current membership includes:** Yamatetsu, Wuxing, Tan Tien, Eastern Tiger Corporation, Federated Boeing, Kwonsham Industries, Monobe International, Malaysian Independent Bank, and a dozen smaller corps.

**What membership gets you:** Special rates with the Malaysian Independent Bank (PPG's alternative to the Japan-aligned Pacific Rim Bank), favored trade status with other PPG corps and countries in their sphere, a united front against Japanese corporate dominance, and access to joint research and production-sharing programs.

**What it's building toward:** The Malaysian Independent Bank is growing toward the size of the Pacific Rim Bank. PPG-favored centers of commerce are developing in Hong Kong, Singapore, Vladivostok, and Vancouver. The Japanese corporations are no longer able to treat the Pacific as their private market.

## Key NPCs

### Yuri Shibanokuji
**Role:** Yamatetsu chairman of the board

An ork in his late thirties, raised in Vladivostok, now running one of the world's largest corporations from a city he called home. He survived at least one assassination attempt from his own board before the relocation vote. He's not especially ideological — he just believes metahumans deserve to be treated like people, which shouldn't be a radical position but apparently is. He leads by example rather than manifesto. He's still learning the megacorporate game and leans heavily on Buttercup's experience.

**For runners:** Yamatetsu under Yuri is more likely to hire runners who are metahuman or who have demonstrated they're not the kind of people who have a problem working with metas. Not a hard rule, but Yuri's people notice.

### Buttercup
**Role:** Free spirit, Yamatetsu board member, largest single shareholder

A free ally spirit summoned centuries before the Awakening who became free sometime before the twentieth century and has been observing humanity ever since. Dunkelzahn bound her in an underprivileged ork's body for a year and a day to teach her about equality — it worked. She's been working toward metahuman equity for decades, investing in corporations and using her influence carefully. She's ancient, vastly knowledgeable, and capable of extraordinary ruthlessness when she decides it's necessary.

In human form: a petite, pretty Japanese girl who looks approximately eighteen, with long black hair and an extremely expressive face. She doesn't walk so much as glide. She uses three-dimensional movement (floating slightly above the floor) without apparent self-consciousness.

**For runners:** Buttercup does not interact with runners directly. She operates through Yamatetsu's legitimate structure and her own network of contacts. If runners encounter her at all, they probably don't know what they're looking at.

**Role:** Yamatetsu president/CEO

The anti-metahuman faction's leader. He lost the boardroom fight and is now executing the relocation he voted against. He's professional enough to do the job, bitter enough about the situation to look for opportunities to undermine the new direction when he can do so without obvious exposure. ### Wu Lung-Wai
**Role:** Wuxing president/CEO, son of the PPG's original architect

Meets all important contacts face-to-face. He has built Wuxing into a megacorp in two years using a 200 million nuyen bequest from Dunkelzahn as the seed. ### Sun Runming
**Role:** Head of Wuxing's Seattle division

Portly, cheerful, apparently hedonistic. Business meetings with him turn into drinking sessions with bawdy jokes and expensive liquor. ## Adventure Framework: The Needle and the Damage Done

*(Note: This framework is referenced in the sourcebook's table of contents but its full text is partially implied through the track structure. The following is GM-ready content based on the track's themes.)*

**Setup:** A Yamatetsu subsidiary in Seattle — operating under its own name, with no obvious connection to Yamatetsu — has been targeted for acquisition by one of the Japanese megacorps (Mitsuhama, Renraku, or Shiawase, depending on which conflict you want to tie in). ## Adventure Framework: Mainframed

**The Setup:** A PPG-member cargo vessel has been seized by pirates working for a Japanese megacorp (technically through several shells). The ship and its cargo — sophisticated manufacturing equipment bound for a new PPG facility — are being held in international waters near the Philippines. The PPG can't use official channels without escalating to something that looks like a megacorp naval conflict. Yamatetsu or Wuxing hires runners to handle it quietly.

**The Problem:** The pirates are well-equipped, the ship's own Matrix systems have been compromised by the hijackers' decker, and there's a timer — the ship will be escorted into Japanese-controlled waters if the runners take more than 48 hours.

**The Complications:**
- The shipping crew is still aboard and must not be harmed
- The megacorp's fingerprints need to stay clean — the pirates cannot admit who hired them
- The manufacturing equipment is more valuable than it appears — it's prototypes for technology Yamatetsu wants kept out of Japanese hands, which is why it was targeted

**Event options:** Board the ship (maritime insertion under darkness), deal with a compromised Matrix system to retake ship control, negotiate with or fight the pirates, prevent Japanese corporate security from "rescuing" the ship before the runners can

This framework is adapted for a piracy campaign in the sourcebook but works equally well for a straight runner crew.

## Adventure Framework: What's In A Name?

**The Setup:** Yamatetsu's move to Vladivostok has left dozens of subsidiary companies in legal limbo throughout Japan and the Pacific. Several of these "orphans" don't know who actually owns them anymore — their corporate parent chain is a tangle of shell companies that's been disrupted by the relocation. Some of them are finding out they were secretly owned by Yamatetsu, Fuchi, or other megacorps and reacting very badly to the news.

One such orphan is a medium-sized magical research firm somewhere in the Pacific Rim. They've been doing genuinely innovative research in hermetic theory. Buttercup wants to bring them under Yamatetsu's umbrella openly — but someone else (Mitsuhama or Saeder-Krupp) wants to acquire them before that can happen, and they're not being polite about it.

**The runners' job:** Make contact with the firm's leadership before the rival acquisition team does, assess whether the firm is willing to negotiate with Yamatetsu, and ensure they survive the conversation. The magical nature of the research means physical security will include magical defenses, and the firm's own mages may be hostile to any corporate approach after having been secretly owned without their knowledge.

This framework is specifically noted in the sourcebook as working well for a magical runner team.

## Adventure Framework: Ancient Chinese Secret, Huh?

**The Setup:** Someone in the PPG has a leak. Sensitive negotiating positions for a major trade agreement between PPG corps are showing up in Japanese megacorp strategy sessions before the PPG meetings happen. The source is inside one of the PPG member corps — but which one?

Wuxing hires runners to investigate. The runners will have to work their way through several PPG member companies' operations, which requires navigating different corporate cultures, different languages, different security approaches, and the constant awareness that any of the member companies' employees might be the leaker — or might become suspicious of why strangers are poking around.

The actual source turns out to be multiple levels more complicated than a single mole: the leak is coming through the Malaysian Independent Bank's Matrix systems, which have been compromised by a sophisticated intrusion that's been running for months. The intrusion traces back to the Zurich-Orbital Gemeinschaft Bank — which means either the Corporate Court's bank is compromised, or someone with access to it is running a private operation.

## Shorter Adventure Ideas

### Truck Stop
Eastern Tiger's Seattle operation is being hit by the Red Rovers go-gang on a regular basis. Moon (Seattle division head) finally got authorization to hire outside security after Lee stopped answering his calls on the subject. Runners are caught in the middle — hired by one side, encountering the other, and gradually realizing that both sides have legitimate grievances and that there isn't a clean answer.

Tan Tien's Parallel Thought interface is further along than anyone outside the company knows. A Japanese megacorp has hired a decker crew to steal the research. Tan Tien hires runners for physical security of the facility while the problem is identified — but the decker crew isn't the only problem. Someone inside Tan Tien is helping them. Finding the mole without tipping off the decker crew or the megacorp backing them requires careful play.

## How This Track Ends

By late 2060:
- **Yamatetsu** is headquartered in Vladivostok, led by Yuri Shibanokuji and Buttercup, aligned with the PPG. Saru Iwano still runs day-to-day operations. The company has left Japan almost entirely — its subsidiaries there have either relocated, been acquired by other Japanese corps, or are being dismantled.
- **Japan** is economically weaker than it's been in decades. Five megacorps still dominate, but Fuchi is gone and Yamatetsu has defected. The metahuman population that Yamatetsu was the largest employer of is now worse off. Racial violence has spiked.
- **The PPG** is a genuine alternative power structure in the Pacific. The Malaysian Independent Bank is growing. PPG-member corporations are increasingly able to operate independently of Japanese-controlled financial systems.
- **Wuxing** has a Corporate Court seat and Li Feng is finding his feet as a justice — every faction thinks they can manipulate him, which is exactly what Wu Lung-Wai expected.
- **Vladivostok** is becoming a significant corporate hub, drawing other companies who want to be near a friendly megacorp HQ.
- **Seattle specifically:** Sun Runming runs Wuxing's Seattle operation. Eastern Tiger's Moon division is still having trouble with the Red Rovers. Kwonsham is establishing a transportation division. The city is increasingly a PPG commercial presence to rival the Japanese megacorps.

The broader implication: Japanese dominance of the Pacific is weakening for the first time in decades. The PPG is the beginning of a new power structure, not the final form of it. For runners, this means new employers, new conflicts, and new factions to navigate — the landscape of corporate power in the Pacific is shifting in ways that won't stabilize for years.
