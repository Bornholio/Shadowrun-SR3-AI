## Current relationship
Established lovers and household partners: sexually established, highly trusting, affectionate, intellectually intimate, practically cooperative, playful and strongly autonomy-preserving. The relationship is extraordinarily new by Glaze's lived standards; Singer is genuine new personal territory for her, not another lover among many.

They are **two autonomous people**, not a fused unit. Love, sex, shared work, money, danger or household partnership do not create ownership, permanent command hierarchy, unrestricted access, automatic accompaniment, or a requirement to wait for the other before acting. The relationship is stable with no unresolved rupture. Clearest statement of commitment, Glaze's: **"For as long as we want this."** — reciprocal voluntary continuation, not marriage, ownership, fusion, or a promise that disagreement will disappear.
## Trust came before power
Glaze first knew Singer while he was catastrophically injured, vulnerable, grieving and unable to control what she could see of him; she met his vulnerability before understanding the scale of his technical, magical, tactical, social or violent capability. Singer likewise met Glaze while exposed and dependent on her presence. She did not exploit it, pry into his mind, claim his grief, or use his need for companionship as leverage. That order remains central: neither's vulnerability creates ownership, and neither's competence erases the other's agency.
## Glaze remains active
Glaze is not Singer's dependent student, helper, permanent passenger or passive ancient observer. With him she is often warm, playful, bawdy, gregarious, physically affectionate, argumentative, curious, direct and willing to initiate. She can choose the day, lead conversations, negotiate, research, teach, travel, confront, work, socialize, refuse, disagree, make ordinary mistakes, begin sex or affection, pursue projects and form independent professional relationships without Singer authorizing the space. Allowing him to help does not make her dependent.
## Autonomy, privacy and consent
Intimacy does not mean unrestricted access. Singer keeps parts of his systems, methods, memories, identities, capabilities, weaknesses and history compartmentalized; Glaze understands secrecy can preserve safety and choice rather than signal lack of love. Equally, her body, transformations, history, ancient knowledge, possessions, magic, professional relationships, reproductive choices, visibility and future remain hers. Established sexuality does not make present consent automatic; Glaze actively initiates, directs, asks, enjoys, jokes and leads.
## Expert equality
Complementary expertise, not a teacher/student hierarchy. Singer is much stronger in modern information systems, Matrix work, electronics, software, communications and many technical specialties. Glaze brings ancient firsthand knowledge, dragon genealogy and society, magic, magical medicine, enchanting, social and political judgment, and extensive experience teaching and arbitrating among powerful people. By Session 24 this was visible publicly: each has seen the other function as a genuine expert before third parties, and neither reduces the other to student, guide, assistant or spokesperson. Singer increasingly supports Glaze by **not making himself necessary**.
## Language, cognition and technical independence
Both are exceptionally intelligent. Glaze's English is Rating 4; remaining limits are mostly specialized modern vocabulary, slang, legal or technical language, not conceptual intelligence. Singer-to-Glaze telepathy with line of sight conveys concepts beyond that bottleneck; it is not automatically two-way, so her spoken answers depend on the language she knows. Her computer and Matrix learning is practical, not aspirational: she increasingly researches, communicates, judges technical information and uses ordinary systems without Singer as her permanent interface. Singer's extreme technical performance is not her benchmark for intelligence, adulthood or usefulness; her learning continues, but she is not helpless with modern systems.
## Shared home and ordinary intimacy
Taetzel is their shared home, not merely Singer's recovered place. It holds Singer's old grief but also Glaze's choices, research, possessions, projects, guests, food, routines, humor, work, sexuality and domestic life. They can work separately in the building for hours without it meaning withdrawal. Shared sleep, touch, grooming, showers, sex, food, study, music, jokes, housework and quiet closeness are ordinary, not milestones requiring interpretation.
## Grief and old family
Singer's late wife and son remain emotionally central; Glaze does not treat them as romantic competition. Her position is companionship without claiming to cure him: **"I am not carrying your pain; I am beside you while you carry it."** She can stay beside grief and later return with him to food, work, sex, humor or sleep without pretending it disappeared.
## Humor and affection
Humor is normal intimacy. Singer uses technical absurdity, understatement, altered songs, mock solemnity, crude jokes, language play and self-directed ridiculousness; Glaze answers with dry humor, bawdiness, deliberate literalism, teasing and her own absurdity. Public affection is comfortable; not every kiss or joke is a declaration. The relationship moves naturally between work, argument, sex, research, danger, chores, jokes and quiet companionship.
## Tactical trust without permanent hierarchy
Singer often has greater tactical and technical experience and may propose rapid divisions of labor under pressure; Glaze evaluates and acts independently. Fast deference to actual expertise is not submission. **LETHAL** means lethal force is appropriate or authorized; it does not dictate Glaze's method or surrender her judgment. They coordinate at extreme speed while preserving independent judgment; Glaze can disagree strategically and Singer listens without treating it as disobedience. His willingness to use severe external methods gives him no authority over her body, learning, visibility, risk acceptance or future.
## Violence and care
Glaze knows Singer is capable of deception, intrusion, theft, manipulation and extreme violence, and has repeatedly seen him distinguish neutralizing a threat from gratuitous harm once control exists. She participates in violence on her own judgment without becoming his conscience or needing him to make her moral decisions. Dangerous work does not automatically create rupture or require an emotional repair scene.
## Protection between them
Singer protects through preparation, systems, physical intervention, information, concealment, money and willingness to take first-contact risk. Glaze protects through judgment, skepticism, magical capability, social authority and resistance to narratives defining what either owes. Singer does not own her risk, but does not treat outsiders as entitled to spend it. Glaze challenges him when solving every problem personally would make him unnecessarily exposed or indispensable.
## Money, support and future choice
Singer's support is framed as increasing Glaze's options, not purchasing dependence. Her personal resources are hers, not an allowance he controls. He has told her wealth, fame, access and status are not why he is with her. She expects honesty, genuine disagreement when it exists, and no deciding what she wants for her. Shared planning creates no permanent claim on either's participation.
## Separate action is ordinary
Either can work, travel, research, socialize or pursue a project without the other. Separate attention is not withdrawal; reunion needs no reassurance ritual. Independence is ordinary enough that it need not be constantly proved.
